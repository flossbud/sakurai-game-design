[00:17](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=17) Now, let's begin.

[00:22](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=22) When working on large games, especially, individuals can lose sight of what it is they're actually making. For example—think about a person making a single screw for a car. They don't know what exactly that screw is going to be used for.

[00:37](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=37) It's hard to imagine the screw eventually turning into a vehicle that transports people around. But working like that—without a clear vision of the larger picture—is stressful, in my opinion. I try to remove that stress as much as possible. To do so, I encourage the team to share information among their peers.

[00:56](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=56) There are three tricks I use to improve this cross-team communication. I may cover these in more detail later, but I'll go ahead and touch on them for now. First, the Daily Report. It's a page I use to post dev progress and news, plus anything else that comes to mind.

[01:16](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=76) There's a comment field, too, so the team can use it to communicate with each other. Some of my posts are just for fun, but I also write about development happenings, as well as my thoughts on the project— making it an effective way to share information. Almost like a development newspaper.

[01:34](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=94) Next is the Screenshot of the Day. Every day, I post a single screenshot from development to the Daily Report page. This routine was repurposed for the daily screens I posted on Twitter, but internally, I also include in-progress content unfit to show to the public.

[01:52](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=112) The team really enjoyed coming up with captions for these in the comments, so it was a nice way to unwind. Last, we have Project Presentations. Once every few months, I ask each team to present the fruits of their labor.

[02:10](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=130) For example, graphics, sound, animation, visual effects, UI... There are many different categories.

[02:22](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=142) Lining up everyone's schedules and securing a venue can be a hassle, so these are somewhat difficult events to put on. But they're absolutely worth it. The work each member is doing will eventually be seen by a great many people.

[02:35](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=155) By having the teams present their progress, everyone gets a broader view of the project instead of feeling like mere cogs in a machine. In any case, improving information flow across your team can keep you from getting stuck in a rut. Creatives need to keep producing one work after another.

[02:54](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=174) And it's great to provide motivation and a sense of accomplishment with each one.

[03:02](https://youtu.be/40ryMOr36fw?t=182) It doesn't come without cost, but it's well worth the effort— so do your best to share info! When you're part of a team, no one should be working alone.
