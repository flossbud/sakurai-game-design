[00:11](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=11) Let's look at Smash Bros. Ultimate. The main menu is divided into several colors. "Smash” is red. Delve into the menu, and all the submenus within are themed around the color red.

[00:26](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=26) "Spirits” uses green. And similarly, green dominates the submenus. I’ve been using color like this for quite a while now, so please take a look for yourself.

[00:38](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=38) Now, color-based menus... Well, they aren't always going to be the right option. If you give your menus lots of color, it may feel like your game is targeted toward younger audiences.

[00:49](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=49) I do it on my projects because I believe there are benefits, but every game has its own tone and manner. As long as you match your game's atmosphere, any color choice should work, I'd say. However, one thing I suggest you avoid, no matter the game, is using a mishmash of colors all on the same screen.

[01:11](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=71) This will depend on the quality of the design and the direction you're going for, but often, it'll result in a mess. In Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U, the menus feature large panels full of character art— so you could say it mixed lots of colors, but it doesn't bother me much.

[01:28](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=88) If anything, with the character art placed front and center, the images themselves will leave an imprint in your mind after enough playtime. Compared to text, pictures can be recognized much faster, and parsed largely through intuition.

[01:45](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=105) Any way you look at it, pictures and color convey more information than just words do. Your left brain has to read the words first and then comprehend them, while your right brain can understand through visualization alone.

[02:03](https://youtu.be/CWvLMK4OKko?t=123) To be extra clear, your game's tone and manner should take top priority over everything else. I used color the way I did because it fit for those projects. But I believe every game has its own right approach.
