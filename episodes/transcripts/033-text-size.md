[00:43](https://youtu.be/W1ubngQNJpw?t=43) In English, the alphabet is visually simpler and easier to understand. It's relatively legible at smaller sizes, but requires more letters than Japanese. With French, sentences can tend to get even longer than that. As a result, each language requires its own way of fitting information into the available space.

[01:06](https://youtu.be/W1ubngQNJpw?t=66) Let's look at a letter in each. Kanji pack lots of meaning into each individual character, while a letter of the Roman alphabet doesn't mean much on its own. If you design your layout with only English in mind, and don't include the flexibility for what other languages may need, your text might just end up too small once it's translated.

[01:26](https://youtu.be/W1ubngQNJpw?t=86) Having said that, I feel like these problems aren't as common now as they used to be. Developers are more conscious of it now, for one, but screen resolutions are higher, too, and it's easier to scale fonts as well.
