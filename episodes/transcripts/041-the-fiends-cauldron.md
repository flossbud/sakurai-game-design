[00:04](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=4) When selecting a difficulty, most games over the years let you choose between Easy, Normal, or Hard. This still holds true today. For the difficulty in Kid Icarus: Uprising, I created a system called the "Fiend's Cauldron."

[00:19](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=19) I think this was a pretty clever mechanic from a game essence standpoint, so let's dive right in.

[00:29](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=29) At the beginning of each stage, the Fiend's Cauldron appears before you. This is where you set your “Intensity." The default Intensity is 2.0. However, if you pour hearts— the game's currency—into the cauldron, you can really crank up the Intensity.

[00:52](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=52) As the Intensity increases, the game grows more difficult. Enemies become more aggressive and have higher attack and defense stats than before, so you need to go in prepared and think about your strategy. On the other hand, you can earn much more in the process.

[01:08](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=68) If you çan finish the stage, you'll be rewarded with more hearts, plus stronger weapons and powers. But if you fail the stage, some hearts will spill out of the cauldron. The difficulty will be lower when you continue, but you've now lost some of your hearts, and it'll be more difficult to get worthwhile rewards.

[01:32](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=92) If you want to lower the difficulty, you can spend hearts to decrease the Intensity, too. If you bring it down to 0.0, you'll be nearly invincible. An almost guaranteed win.

[01:46](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=106) In terms of risk and reward, I believe the advantages of this system are clear. If you take a big risk, you can get a big reward. It's also just a novel approach to tackling difficulty in games.

[01:58](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=118) The system automatically lowers the difficulty when you fail, so the player rarely gets stuck. It may not completely remove the pain of failure, but it's not as bad as bluntly asking if you'd like to switch to Easy Mode.

[02:14](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=134) And since players don't have to play the whole game on asy like in traditional games, this helps keep the experience from feeling unsatisfying. Plus, the game is truly easy if you turn the Intensity down to 0.0, so players won't get stuck on a section that's too difficult.

[02:34](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=154) This system does embody the concept of risk and reward, but it's not without its faults. For starters, a less obvious one: it makes the game feel too hardcore. A game that's constantly pushing you to your limits couldn't exactly be called “casual." The land stages in Kid Icarus: Uprising are also a little too big and have far too many enemies, and we couldn't make players respawn where they died, so not all the pieces mesh perfectly.

[03:07](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=187) Game essence, push and pull, and risk and reward are great for making the experience “gamelike," but they can conversely make games feel less approachable, too. That rings even more true on the smaller 3DS screen. I also suspect it wasn't clear enough that setting the Intensity below 2.0 would lower the difficulty.

[03:29](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=209) Newcomers to the game are the ones who needed this most. This is one of those things we didn't have enough time for during development, but in retrospect, I wish I could've done more to introduce it to beginners. Games today generally let you change the difficulty whenever you want, with no restrictions for doing so.

[03:53](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=233) Obviously it's a necessary feature, and I do think that's how it should work, but part of me still wishes players could be rewarded for strong play.

[04:03](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=243) It wasn't perfect, but I think the Fiend's Cauldron was a smart feature. I'd say it offered a new way of thinking about difficulty that people can still learn from today.

[04:18](https://youtu.be/ai7pBxHAcvU?t=258) Some single-player modes in Smash Bros. for Wii U/3DS and Smash Bros. Ultimate use a similar mechanic to the Fiend's Cauldron, but earning points there isn't as necessary as hearts in Kid Icarus: Uprising, so it's comparatively less important.
