These handy explanations are known as “help text" and show up often, sometimes at the bottom of the screen. And in my view, you should be proactive about including them. I’ve used them in almost all my games, excluding the ones I made when such things weren't possible.

[00:39](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=39) I consider them a relatively vital feature. I'm not entirely sure how they've been translated in international versions, but there's one thing I'm very careful about when coming up with help text. It's a must.

[00:54](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=54) Namely, never using the option's name to explain its function. This is important, so I'll say it again: don't explain using the same words! This example may sound like a joke, but it actually happens quite oten.

[01:10](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=70) Sometimes, the description for "Options” just says “Go to the Options." Please don't do this! The text exists to help thosewho want to know what "Options" means, so this misses the point.

[01:24](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=84) Instead, try to write these descriptions using different words. In the case of "Options," maybe something like “Adjust a variety of game settings." Ideally, that information would be immediately clear, but that's sometimes hard to accomplish in just a few words. That's why it's important when creating these descriptions to drill down to the most important parts.

[01:46](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=106) Look at it objectively, cut out any excess words, and continue to polish the text until it comes across as clearly as possible. Think of it like the difference between directly translating words and translating meaning. When naming modes, too, ask yourself if something like “Play Solo” gets the point across better than “1P Mode."

[02:11](https://youtu.be/mWIbb1l7Wlw?t=131) And of course, you should avoid throwing in technical terminology, too. Not everyone will know what “config" or “key repeat” refers to, for example.
