[00:17](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=17) After we finished Kirby's Adventure, there was a bit of downtime while we set up the SNES dev environment, so it wasn't immediately all-hands-on-deck. I was provided a Macintosh myself, and thus finally switched to an OS with a “windowing system."

[00:33](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=33) Until then, I'd been using computers like the PC-9801. Kirby Super Star was built on two main concepts: two-player simultaneous play and an “omnibus” format. Let's start with the first point, two-player gameplay.

[00:51](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=51) During a conversation with Shigeru Miyamoto of Nintendo, Mr. Miyamoto asked me if a two-player Kirby game would be possible. He said he'd been wanting to add this feature to the Super Mario series, but the fast-paced gameplay didn't allow for it.

[01:07](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=67) "Kirby moves slower than Mario, though, so you should be able to figure it out, right?" Talk about a tall order... In side-scrolling action games, players tend to pull each other along as they scroll.

[01:23](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=83) It's one thing when fights are confined like in belt-scrolling beat 'em ups, but cooperation isn't so simple in games whère you jump over obstacles. It's true the original Kirby was a slower game than Mario, so in theory, it should've been fairly easy to pull off.

[01:41](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=101) But Kirby had already introduced copy abilities by then, and different abilities moved at drastically different speeds. It might actually have been an even tougher ask than Mario. Though perhaps not if the hardware were capable of zooming in and out.

[01:59](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=119) But even tall orders must be filled, and so, I came up with the "Helper" system. In other words, I divided players into lead and supporting roles.

[02:12](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=132) The camera follows the lead character. At times, the supporting character may end up off-camera, so I designed the system to allow for that. If a character ends up too far off-screen, they'll "Space Jump” right back.

[02:30](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=150) Instead of letting both characters scroll the screen with equal weight, I placed the focus on only the lead character. That, I thought, might just work. Next, I decided to combine the concept of lead and supporting roles with the copy ability.

[02:50](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=170) The enemies themselves could become playable characters! This boosted the fun and personality— two birds with one stone. Though...I knew it would be tough to actually make. We'd need to set character traits and parameters for nearly every copy ability.

[03:10](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=190) But that was just what had to happen. That was when I first came up with the idea of copy hats. Originally, I just wanted some kind of item to create Helpers, but they turned out to be a great way to dramatically boost Kirby's character appeal.

[03:25](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=205) Kirby's design is simple to begin with, so the added flair really suits him. Those copy hats would go on to cause many headaches during Smash Bros. development... Those copy hats would go on to causemany headaches during Smash Bros. development...

[03:41](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=221) Another new element of the copy ability was thatthe player could perform a wide range of moves based on button inputs. In Kirby's Adventure, each ability had only one move, but now there was variety, with repeat presses, directional inputs—even command-based techniques.

[03:58](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=238) To compensate, we gave even standard enemies the ability to survive a few hits. In the older titles, it usually took just one hit to defeat an enemy. But in making some moves stronger than others, we could really set different copy abilities apart. This became an essential element of later titles in the Kirby series.

[04:21](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=261) At the same time, we changed up Kirby's health bar, going from the old 6-segment gauge to something more analog. Now, for the other concept: the “omnibus" collection format. In Kirby Super Star, you'll find a number of scearios tacked to a cork board.

[04:41](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=281) Each one you pick will take you to a different game with its own unique theme. There's a racing game about eating food, and a more serious (?) story-driven game too. As for why I made it an “omnibus,"

[04:58](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=298) I did it because I thought we were seeing too many games that took too long to beat. At the time, more and more massive games kept hitting the market. And they all took a long time to finish.

[05:13](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=313) By creating an “omnibus" collection of quick-to-beat individual scenarios, each coplete with their own unique gameplay, I thought I could provide a lot of content without causing players to grow tired of any one style. In truth, the first scenario—Spring Breeze— wasn't planned to include the ċopy ability at all.

[05:39](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=339) I reluctantly put it in at the request of one of Nintendo's producers, but to this day, I still wish you could play without it, at least your first time through. Nothing really sets it apart from the next scenario, after all. By the way, there was one more scenario from the planning stages— “Kagero Mansion"—which didn't end up in the final product.

[06:03](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=363) Kirby's mouth has been sealed shut, so he sets out on a survival adventure using items based on the various copy abilities. But it was ultimately cut for schedule and ROM space reasons. On another note, we decided at some point mid-development to use CG for the backgrounds.

[06:23](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=383) Personally speaking, I don't think this turned out especially well... With the rise of games like Donkey Kong Country, CG backgrounds were growing more and more common at the time.

[06:36](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=396) But our staff wasn't familiar with the format, and it just wasn't a great fit for Kirby, which required piecing together various bits of terrain. Still, it's important to learn new skills. The things you make today can lead to what you make tomorrow.

[06:53](https://youtu.be/PkeETXCSn5M?t=413) With its two-player gameplay, “omnibus" format, and wealth o copy ability moves and hats, Kirby Super Star really was the definitive Kirby experience. In case you didn't know, I only directed three side-scrolling Kirby titles: the original Kirby's Dream Land, Kirby's Adventure, and this game.
