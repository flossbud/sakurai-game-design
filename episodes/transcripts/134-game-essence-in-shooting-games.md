[01:08](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=68) In the boss fights this game is known for, if you stay back and reduce risk, you'll fire fewer shots—meaning less reward. [01:27](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=87) Colliding with terrain costs you a life, so having to face enemies positioned on it is already a risk. Defeating them earns you a reward. Sometimes power-ups are placed next to terrain, and the weapons capable of defeating enemies without getting close to the terrain each have pros and cons— which broadens the game essence.

[01:42](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=102) Take the double cannon for example. It makes it easier to hit enemies above you, but you can only fire two shots at a time, and it's worse than the laser at firing straight ahead.

[01:54](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=114) What downsides will you accept, and what upsides will you gain? I'd say this kind of thinking can be effective in any game.

[02:02](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=122) Easily defeating every enemy on-screen might feel good in the moment, but there's no push and pull there. Still, strong game essence isn't everything, so do whatever you feel is right. Focusing too much on game essence might cost you some flair, and maybe even make your game feel like a chore.

[02:20](https://youtu.be/iN3IyqPjLkc?t=140) But if you want to make a game with its own unique flavor, don't just mimic an existing game. Instead, think about why that game is fun in the first place.
