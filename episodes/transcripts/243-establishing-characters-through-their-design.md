[01:08](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=68) It makes for a clear silhouette. In truth, this emphasis is quite similar to the kind found with characters like Sonic. His head is quite large, and while his arms and legs are skinny, they've got just the right level of exaggeration.

[01:22](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=82) Simply put, it establishes him as his own unique character. So, what's the secret to making a picture of something imaginary feel "cool"? I know a number of ways to do it, but it would be difficult to explain why exactly those ways work.

[01:38](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=98) Having said that, the part I'd say deserves the most emphasis is a character's silhouette. Their form should be exaggerated, even when they're just solid black. If the silhouette is ambiguous and unreadable, or impossible to distinguish from other characters, it's not good enough.

[01:57](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=117) The same is true of a character's poses. Depending on how well they're posed, even the same character can look and feel completely different.

[02:10](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=130) No matter what, it's good to be conscious of how well-established your characters are. The people assigned to a character need to understand its core essence, and work to draw that out even further. This isn't necessarily something one person can strive for alone.

[02:24](https://youtu.be/PDjdquhVHU4?t=144) The whole team needs to come together and make those characters shine.
