[00:04](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=4) It's possible to make a game that only requires one button to play. In fact, I did this in both Kirby's Adventure and Kirby Super Star— which feature minigames you play with just one button. Games that are playable with one button can be broken down into four main categories: rapid tapping, timing-based, reaction-based, and alternating.

[00:28](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=28) First, rapid tapping. These games test how rapidly you can press the button. Certain players will naturally be more skilled at these, and they tend to be quite tiring, so they've fallen out of fashion these days.

[00:39](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=39) Timing-based. These games ask you to press or release the button at a specific time. Crane Fever is a great example of this. You have to release the button when the crane is in position to pick up the plushie.

[00:52](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=52) The visual cues are different, but Megaton Punch works in a similar way. Reaction-based. Once you get the signal, you have to press the button as quickly as possible. Quick Draw and the multiplayer-enabled Samurai Kirby fall into this category.

[01:10](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=70) These games are a test of reflexes, but they allow for push and pull, since players can fake each other out. Making the "kah!" sound of the signal with your mouth, for example. Alternating.

[01:23](https://youtu.be/tafG03n89MY?t=83) These games revolve around knowing when to keep the button pressed and when to release. In the Egg Catcher minigame, you keep the button pressed to catch the eggs, and release it to avoid the bombs. Games where you just hold the button may also work, but it's a bit tough to find game essence in that.
