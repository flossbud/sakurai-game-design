[00:04](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=4) On Kid Icarus: Uprising especially, there was something I wish I had done better during development.

[00:13](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=13) Say, for example, I as the director explained to a planner how a certain element should work. They would then explain those details to the programmers. But what I'd ultimately get back was the polar opposite of what I was hoping for. Uprising's development team was a bit of a motley crew, as everyone came from their own unique work culture.

[00:36](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=36) When I was giving instructions, my usual method sometimes yielded poor results, which was a real problem. So, starting with the development of Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U, I put a new system into place.

[00:49](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=49) Namely, whenever I gave instructions or feedback, all relevant team members were required to attend and listen. Take the supervision process for Inkling's special move spec docs. Under the old system, the only person I gave instructions to was the planner.

[01:06](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=66) But now, everyone involved in Inkling's production would join in to hear what I had to say. Planners, programmers, modelers, animators, effect designers, sound designers, and their team leaders all had to be present for my feedback. There was a large room near my desk with a huge monitor where I could show off the build or my PC screen.

[01:32](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=92) The team members would all pull up chairs to listen. There must have been maybe 30 people on average. Or up to 50, on big days. I also had a rule that anyone could sit in, even if they weren't on the relevant team.

[01:45](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=105) I used a mic so that everyone could hear me as I explained what was on the screen. And of course, I took questions at the end. Separately from that, I had fighter modelers, artists, stage modelers, animators, and more all hold group review sessions to keep everyone on the same page.

[02:06](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=126) By having team members see the feedback everyone was getting—not just their own— they could all learn and grow. This method in particular is something I had used in previous projects as well. This system meant that team members were tied up in more meetings, so it might look like it was wasteful at first glance.

[02:28](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=148) But it was very effective. Everyone was able to understand my ideas and goals much more clearly. If anyone was unsure of something, this helped them know what to do regardless of their role. And on large projects where it's often hard to know what the rest of the team is up to, I'd say this system contributes to better visibility overall.

[02:54](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=174) It does require you to keep those meetings speedy for it to work, but I really do recommend it. The team seemed to appreciate it, too.

[03:04](https://youtu.be/CafmzxRBo4Y?t=184) This is a bit of an aside, but the large room I mentioned was always stocked with sweets and snacks. I bought them myself, and let people have as much as they wanted. Though, due to the pandemic and the switch to remote work, I sadly haven't been able to do it for a while.
