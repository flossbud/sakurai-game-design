[00:21](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=21) To reiterate: strategy is how players minimize losses and maximize gains. But it can also involve taking larger losses for the sake of even greater gains. Try to think about strategy across the following game genres: Action games.

[00:42](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=42) First-person shooters. Fighting games. Role-playing games. RTS games.

[00:57](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=57) Puzzle games. Gambling games. Just about any game with push and pull will also involve some kind of strategy. The thing is, games tend to be constructed in very complicated ways, and at times it becomes possible to "cheat the system," so to speak.

[01:19](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=79) In other words, get gains without taking any losses. In Dark Souls, standing in certain spots will trick enemies into falling to their deaths, letting you rack up lots of souls with no work. Though it's kind of tough to get footage of...

[01:33](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=93) Balance-breaking situations like these are generally stamped out during the debugging process, but letting players cheat the system can be not only fun, but memorable, too. Even Dragon Quest allows for things like metal slime farming, which clearly provides players with a huge advantage.

[01:52](https://youtu.be/MVsVTgJKuzU?t=112) Battles in Dragon Quest are carefully balanced, but players are able to bypass a large number of them just by farming for metal slimes. Still, it's fun, right? That process will really stick with players.
