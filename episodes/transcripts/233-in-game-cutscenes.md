[00:04](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=4) Games these days are capable of some incredible expression. With cutting-edge titles, we've reached a point where pre-rendered cutscenes are sometimes a step down in quality from in-engine ones. The resolution and frame rate actually drop.

[00:31](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=31) Now, on to the main topic. Imagine a game with a long stretch of relatively irrelevant cutscenes. How would such a game make you feel? It depends on what it is, of course, but I personally tend to get impatient.

[00:44](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=44) I want those drawn-out sequences to wrap up just a bit sooner. However, that's not to say that long cutscenes are inherently good or bad— and sometimes they can tell a story more clearly, or help get players emotionally invested. Subtle, meaningful moments in a cutscene are hard to replicate in the midst of gameplay.

[01:06](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=66) Adding cutscenes into a high-paced action game can also give players a nice breather.

[01:14](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=74) Those kinds of intense titles are usually good about setting up areas without enemies, though, so cutscenes in particular don't need to be that high a priority. Sometimes, the cutscenes themselves are the selling point—a reward for playing well. But seeing as those scenes end up all over the internet these days, it's hard to consider that a reward.

[01:38](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=98) Story-focused titles have it especially tough in the modern era, l'd say. In-engine dialogue scenes are often made as a series of lines you can skip straight through. But that's not possible with most cutscenes, making them feel off-putting. It might be more user-friendly if players were able to skip ahead or fast-forward through cutscenes.

[02:03](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=123) With DVRs, YouTube, and other video streaming services, we've entered an age where you can watch videos slightly sped-up. As a creator, I can understand wanting viewers to fully watch something you poured your heart into, but I think there's still room to consider how we can adapt to today's needs.

[02:25](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=145) On a related note, players should always be able to skip cutscenes. That includes the credits. Even if they're on their first playthrough, it might not be their first time watching the scene.

[02:38](https://youtu.be/VZRh0P40RAM?t=158) One more thing. Quick-time events—where players have to press a button in the middle of a cutscene— seem to be quite unpopular. I can see how you might want to keep people on the edge of their seat, but I suggest you implement these with extreme caution.
