[00:04](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=4) Video games employ a wide array of sound effects. It's not hyperbole to say they're playing all the time. In most cases, you'll be choosing between realistic or exaggerated sounds, but you'll still need to think carefully about how loud, how flashy, and how discernible each one is.

[00:23](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=23) For example... Listen to Link walk. And now listen to him swing his sword. Both seem pretty quiet, so which should you prioritize?

[00:36](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=36) Games have all sorts of sounds, each one designed to mimic the action they correspond to, so it can sometimes be a struggle to decide. My advice is as follows: The more relevant a sound is to the player, the louder that sound should be.

[00:53](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=53) I think that's the way to approach it. Let me explain using Smash Bros. as an example. Oh, and I'll be omitting some subtle effects for clarity. The sound effect that should be loudest is the KO sound.

[01:08](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=68) Whether you're KO'ing an opponent or getting KO'd yourself, it's crucial to the push and pull and the outcome of the match, so it's the loudest effect— top priority. Next up is impact sounds.

[01:23](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=83) When striking an opponent, or getting strück, the player needs direct feedback to what they're doing, and that's exactly what we give them.

[01:35](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=95) Perfect shielding gets the same priority. Next is special move sounds. Not their impact, but the moment you use them. These actions have a lot of character and are unique to each fighter, so we make sure they stand out accordingly.

[01:51](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=111) Of course, depending on a move's strength and nature, the volume may vary greatly. Voices deserve the same priority as special moves, but clear speech will cut right through other sounds— so you need to strike a balance.

[02:08](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=128) Explosions fall about midway between impacts and special moves. Though the bigger these are, the more we'll make them stand out. Next are dodges, shield blocks, and ledge grabs. These all require certain conditions, and their sounds signify you've succeeded.

[02:28](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=148) Next is attack swings— minus the impact. By now, the sounds are getting pretty hard to hear. But these provide feedback for button presses, so they do need to stand out at least a bit. Dashing by flicking the stick comes out at about the same volume.

[02:45](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=165) Next we're getting to more minor movement sounds. Things like jumping and landing. Since landing is a reaction, we make it louder than the jump. A lot goes into these landing sounds— and we make each one to match the fighter's clothing or footwear.

[03:04](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=184) Believe it or not, Cloud's landing sounds differ based on his costume. You can hear it on a good set of speakers.

[03:14](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=194) Last up is walking sounds. These, too, are feedback for player input, so we of course make them audible. You might not be able to tell during fights, but each character has unique footsteps, meaning we had to make new ones for every fighter we added.

[03:30](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=210) And we always include a variety of sounds for these kinds of things. The same effect playing repeatedly would sound too mechanical otherwise.

[03:42](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=222) Speaking of footsteps, do you ever feel like they should have higher priority in 3D games? You can barely hear them in a lot of games, making it feel like something is missing— as if your character is hovering, not walking.

[03:55](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=235) Though footsteps can be a real pain sometimes. Floors have different attributes and materials, meaning you have to change walking and landing sounds depending on what the floor is made of. Still, believable sounds feel the best, so you can't help but put in the effort anyway.

[04:21](https://youtu.be/qKjD5qbLUeI?t=261) When reviewing your game's sound effects, consider their importance to both the game and the player character, then prioritize them accordingly. If you only consider the size of an action, you might not reach the right result.
