[00:04](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=4) During development of Smash Bros. Brawl, I started a habit of writing a Daily Report to share with my team every day. This wasn't something for the public to read, but only for the team themselves. I wrote one every day I worked, even if it was supposed to be a day off.

[00:22](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=22) Even the Screenshots of the Day I posted on Twitter had their origins in the daily screenshots I shared internally with my team. I'd put one at the top of each report, then go on to discuss three to five topics. Topics would include things like project news and what I was working on, and I'd post my checklists, as well as issues that needed fixing.

[00:44](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=44) Sometimes I had to get stern, too. Not every report was necessarily conveying project info, though— sometimes I'd give my impressions of a game I played, talk about current events, share my thoughts...

[00:55](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=55) Everything under the sun, really. Team members were also able to comment on each topic, so it was common for them to interact with each other there, too. The Daily Report had some major benefits.

[01:12](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=72) First up is one of the reasons I started it: helping the members of the team get to know me better. Since I don't really belong to a larger company and I don't have my own team, I have to join up with teams that already exist.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=85) To them, I'm some outsider. For the dev team, having to take direction from an unknown person likely wouldn't feel very good. But if they were to read my Daily Reports, then I wouldn't be a total stranger anymore.

[01:42](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=102) Next, it's great for sharing info. You might think teamwide emails are effective at relaying information, but you'd be surprised how often they go unread. However, the Daily Report offers something new every day— so most people take it upon themselves to keep up with it, making it a more reliable method than regular old email.

[02:01](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=121) I once wrote to the Smash Bros. team that I'd be announcing the next fighter there at 5 p.m. that day, and had planned to use the Daily Report to deliver the message to the team. This was for Sephiroth, by the way. But when I did, the page was accessed so many times that the server went down.

[02:19](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=139) Just from our dev team... Next, it's great for helping me to stay productive. Writing anything on a regular basis is a great form of personal training. Though, maybe that's the wrong way to put it, since it's an expected part of the daily job.

[02:38](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=158) Directors in particular are required to be highly productive, without exception. Another benefit is that it provides a bit of motivation to the team each day. If nothing else, I always try to vary my Daily Reports, so that a team that's continually working on the same thing can have some variety and stimuli for new ideas.

[03:03](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=183) The last benefit is that it helps track progress. When working with big teams, each person's efforts are just one part of the larger game dev process. This might make them lose sight of the game's bigger picture, so it's important that they see what other people are making.

[03:23](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=203) The Screenshot of the Day, for example, showed just how the game was coming along, and served as a kind of progress report for the entire team. That's what I had hoped it would accomplish from the start. Naturally, I sometimes posted separate pictures, too, as their own topics.

[03:40](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=220) Other people would also chime in with what they'd managed to finish that day, or the occasional personal matters. For people who post like that, it's a great way to get a feel for their personalities. This really contributes to an open team environment, so I can't recommend it enough.

[04:01](https://youtu.be/T6XgiBzYbzs?t=241) Another thing I'd set up when starting with a team was an "opinions board." This gave people an easy way to submit their suggestions and requests. This was effective too!
