[00:24](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=24) Just because someone's a director, producer, or some kind of leader, that doesn't mean it's their job to bark orders at the rest of the team. While they are in a position to ask for things as they guide a title toward the finish line, they're no higher up than anyone else.

[00:41](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=41) On the contrary, the people who provide ideas and proposals are powerless without others helping them out! It is true that the director stands farthest upstream in the production process. The instructions they give travel downward from that top position. But in actuality, they're making requests from the bottom up to the dev team members who'll be doing the actual work.

[01:05](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=65) It should be easier to conceptualize if you think of it like the relationship between customer and publisher. A publisher doesn't dictate that customers buy their game— the customer makes that decision themselves. The customer is downstream in terms of distribution, but upstream when it comes to whether they ultimately choose or value the game.

[01:27](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=87) It goes without saying that the "king" in this relationship isn't the publisher, but the customer. As for the dev team that's been hired by a company, everyone has a duty to do what the company asks, and nobody is higher or lower than anyone else in that. Neither the director nor their team members should act superior or subservient to anyone else, I'd say.

[01:50](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=110) And even if you were above someone, remember—nobody likes a demanding customer. No matter your position, you'll just end up being resented. So whether you're the boss or a core member of the team, you should always show respect to those around you. Everyone on the team should be working together to create a better product.

[02:11](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=131) And every member of your team is an ally. Even if you have the occasional disagreement, that doesn't make you enemies. As a general rule, I prefer to address my team members politely, both in name and speaking style.

Honestly, I'm always a little taken aback when I hear other people addressing new coworkers casually. On the other hand, I don't tend to write too formally in emails with external companies. Not even with higher-ups at Nintendo.

[02:43](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=163) This might come across a bit rude in some contexts, but I don't think anyone's had a problem with it... I hope!

[02:53](https://youtu.be/r8ZZlnvNvBY?t=173) Up is down, and down is up. Be sure to remember this, especially if you're in a leadership or directorial position.
