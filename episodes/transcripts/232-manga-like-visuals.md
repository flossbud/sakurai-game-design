[00:04](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=4) When I say "manga-like visuals," cel-shading techniques may come to mind— but let's put that aside. In Japanese manga and animation, there are lots of interesting ways in which visuals are expressed.

[00:20](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=20) Seeing a character's eyes through the frame of their glasses, for example. Or the way mouths are depicted from a side view. These techniques can of course be used for games, as well. The way Mega Man Legends did this was particularly impactful.

[00:36](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=36) When a character's profile is shown in cutscenes, their mouth moves to the side of their face. This is common in manga and anime. It's not a natural expression, per se, but it's a great fit for anime. This lets you show mouth movement by just swapping cels, without animating the jaw.

[00:54](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=54) Not only is it better for production, but it might even be easier to recognize the mouth's movements this way. Back to this profile shot from Mega Man Legends. I wonder what it would look like if we could see the model from the front...

[01:08](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=68) Well, maybe it's best not to dwell on it. Characters like Sonic have their mouths moved to the side like this, as well. It may be strange if you think about it too much, but it's a great way of expressing the character. Sometimes, striving for manga-like visuals will actually create more work when constructing the CG.

[01:27](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=87) Think of Tom and Jerry and the way in which they squeeeeze and stretch out of narrow spaces. Pulling that off well in CG can prove quite difficult. Maybe through something like shape animation. Just because manga-like visuals lack realism doesn't mean you should avoid using them— if anything, I recommend them.

[01:48](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=108) We may be better off welcoming the fun kinds of expression these methods can bring about. As a bonus, I'd like to share a story from my time supervising Smash Bros. Últimate. Fighters like Kirby and Jigglypuff originally had their mouths modeled like this. The left is a front view, while the right is a profile.

[02:23](https://youtu.be/U9lQpn67Wos?t=143) Now, this is how I requested the mouths look. The final models are very close to it. Here's the two side-by-side. An actual profile view of their mouths would look more like the top.
