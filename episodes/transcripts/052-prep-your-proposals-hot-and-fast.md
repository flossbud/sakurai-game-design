[01:19](https://youtu.be/1cFrEoiziDA?t=79) Don't underestimate the momentum that'll bring. The longer you let it sit, the less fresh it'll be. That's the attitude I'd say you should approach it with. Writing proposals is tough work. Their job is to show how a game is fun, and yet, they can often lack what end up becoming essential elements of the game.

[01:37](https://youtu.be/1cFrEoiziDA?t=97) For example, if you think about Monster Hunter at the proposal level, it's a game about hunting monsters and using their materials to make gear. At that stage, it doesn't sound especially novel. I imagine only a few of the game's best points showed up in its proposal.

[01:55](https://youtu.be/1cFrEoiziDA?t=115) In other words, polishing your game is also important to making it fun.

[02:04](https://youtu.be/1cFrEoiziDA?t=124) You can't have a game without first having a proposal. To get the folks who provide funding and talent to think, "We should definitely do this," try serving up proposals that are quick, snappy, and full of energy. And if your proposal gets rejected, don't convince yourself that the other party doesn't have taste.

[02:22](https://youtu.be/1cFrEoiziDA?t=142) It's your responsibility as the presenter to show off your game's appeal.
