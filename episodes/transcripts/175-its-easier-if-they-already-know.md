[01:06](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=66) Generally speaking, developers tend to avoid copying what other titles are doing, but at the same time, the industry has what you might call "de facto standards"— which I'd say you're better off using. By doing so, you're making the handshake between human and computer just a little bit warmer.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=85) You should also include a way to remap buttons, even if it makes things more complicated. Game designers used to think they'd already come up with the best controls, so nobody would need to change them— but players want the freedom to play how they like. And games should deliver that freedom whenever possible.

[01:50](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=110) Now, I'm asked on rare occasions to make Smash Bros. playable with the D-pad—but that's just not possible. The analog stick with its granular values is fundamentally different from a D-pad where the only values are 0 or 1. Back on topic, though.

[02:09](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=129) If, despite everything else, you simply can't set the controls how you want, there's no choice but to play using the controls you're given. You can't change the unchangeable.

[02:24](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=144) As an aside... There's a game called Chelnov— and when I first played it at an arcade, I remember thinking how strange it was. You pressed a button to walk forward, tilted the stick up to fire, or tilted it sideways to jump.

[02:40](https://youtu.be/ANerCiyfJjo?t=160) But it turned out, the person working at the arcade had simply wired the harness incorrectly. I can't help but feel that if game controls are too standardized, players might lose out on some of that unique fun.
