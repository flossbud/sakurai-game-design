[00:04](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=4) As I mentioned early on in this Graphics category, when making games, it's common for the sense of scale to not feel right. In other words, large objects end up looking too small. But let's put that issue aside for now and talk about how to create photos with a "miniature" look to them.

[00:28](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=28) First, start with a landscape photo with an overhead perspective. Blur the top and bottom. In other words, change the depth of field or focus. This helps make the photo look like it was shot from up close.

[00:40](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=40) Then, increase saturation. And decrease blue hues if possible. This will reduce any sense of aerial perspective. And it's done.

[00:48](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=48) A few easy steps, and it looks like a miniature. Simply put, this process can potentially make big things appear smaller. There were two parts to it. First, the shallow depth of field.

[01:03](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=63) This might seem like a handy way to boost atmosphere, but aside from the very specific locations it needs to be added, it's usually better to avoid it. Different people tend to focus on different parts of a composition, and trying to focus o something blurry can sometimes be physically uncomfortable.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=85) Take this shot from Sora's fighter reveal. Originally, the focus shifted from the background to the foreground, but I asked that this be removed. Next is saturation.

[01:39](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=99) Saturation refers to how vivid colors are, and is something that should be reduced on larger objects. That's because larger objects have aerial perspective. To put it another way, the closer an object is to you, the higher its saturation should be.

[01:52](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=112) You have to strike a balance.

[01:58](https://youtu.be/omeFOX21-wc?t=118) Even though getting the proper sense of visual scale seems relatively straightforward, it's something I have to give feedback for quite often. Just drawing each individual material's texture won't create cohesive visuals, so I recommend taking a step back and considering the final image as a whole.
