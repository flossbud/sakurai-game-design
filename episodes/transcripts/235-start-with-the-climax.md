[00:04](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=4) If you're going to make a game, consider kicking things off with a climactic scene right from the start!

[00:14](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=14) Of course, that's not to say you should put the last boss right at the beginning. Though you can if you want to. The intro sequence of a game is extremely important. Final Fantasy VIl's opening, for example, wouldn't necessarily feel out of place as the lead-up to the final dungeon.

[00:39](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=39) And yet, I feel as though many games out there tend to be slow starters. They flesh out the protagonist's backstory, the worid's customs, their relationships with their friends—all for a long time. In movies, as well, it's better to lead with a climactic event if you have one, then go back and show how you got to that point later on.

[01:00](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=60) With games in particular, players really want to take control and find out about your game— so it's no time for ambling along. Let them play, first thing!

[01:10](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=70) And if possible, show that the enemy forces mean business. Take Chapter 1 of Kid Icarus: Uprising. When you start the game, the door opens and you're thrust into an Air Battle. In less than a minute, you're introduced to your initial antagonist, Medusa.

[01:28](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=88) You don't fight her directly, and it's clear this is still just the first chapter, but details like Pit's connection to Palutena, the sky world and the surface world, air battles vs. ground battles, and other things you'd expect to lead with like Powers or the Underworld Army are glossed over in favor of a speedy start.

[01:51](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=111) If you think about Final Fantasy VII from a chronological standpoint, you might be tempted to start the story with why Cloud decided to join Avalanche. But instead, they kick things off in style. While it's okay to have events slowly unfold amidst the characters' daily lives, if you're going to do that, try to hand control to the player as soon as possible.

[02:18](https://youtu.be/eAd0pt-uv8g?t=138) In my opinion, you're best off bringing a narrative peak to the front of the story. If this doesn't work with the game's structure, at least try to keep the opening short. Even Phoenix Wright puts you into a trial at the very start. In short, try to get players up and at 'em right from the get-go.
