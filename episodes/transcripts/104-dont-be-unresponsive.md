[00:04](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=4) Game consoles have quite a lot of buttons. Even just the buttons you simply press amount to almost a dozen. For menus and other UI bits, I'd say it's better to have more than one button act as a confirm button.

[00:21](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=21) Even with simple action games that have only one or two functions to assign, I think it's completely okay to double up on buttons. It doesn't matter if the functionality is redundant— it's better to have buttons do something as opposed to nothing at all.

[00:38](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=38) Though of course, this doesn't mean that you have to assign functionality to every last button. When you play a game, information gets sent from your hands to the computer, then back to your eyes and ears. In that bidirectional transfer, the information coming from your hands is pretty vital, right?

[00:56](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=56) And everyone has their own way of playing. So if they send a signal— or press a button, in other words— wouldn't it be better to provide some kind of feedback instead of having nothing happen? If the player performs an action and the game doesn't give a reaction, that will feel a bit lacking.

[01:17](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=77) If the L Button also works as a confirm button or action button, you can play one-handed, too. I admit, there are some downsides, such as having to feel around for what button does what, but I'd still say that's better than having unresponsive controls.

[01:35](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=95) With cutscene sequences especially, I think it's better to have some kind of reaction when the player presses a button. That could be a prompt allowing the cutscene to be skipped, for example. Players press buttons because they want something from the game.

[01:53](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=113) It's also good to avoid ambiguous responses that don't clearly show whether a button was pressed.

[02:02](https://youtu.be/eia2IAmvtKI?t=122) I'm not saying that a lack of response is always a bad thing, but games are all about actions and reactions, and it's worth keeping this point in mind. By the way, did you notice this? It's a sticker that says “1-2."
