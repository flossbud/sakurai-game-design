And depending on who you ask, you'll get very different answers about what kinds of games are good or what parts are fun. [00:23](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=23) One person's trash is another person's treasure. Different strokes for different folks. We should try to accept that different people have different values and viewpoints. That said, there's one big trap to avoid— something that might make it so nobody loves your game.

[00:39](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=39) And that's... making it slow. That does include issues such as characters simply moving too slowly, but more importantly, I'm talking about things taking too long, or having long stretches with nothing to do.

[00:55](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=55) I'm really referring to how much time players spend not actually playing the game. Games are full of fun, interesting experiences, and I'd go as far as to say it's really bad to make players wait to get to those parts. You can, in some cases, use that slowness as a way to build suspense, but it may not land in this day and age.

[01:17](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=77) Even so, you shouldn't let your game drag. In practice, there will be times when you just don't have a choice. For example, no matter how much you optimize load times, they may not always get as short as you'd like. If that wait time feels like a mistake— like you're sitting around because someone dropped the ball in development— that's just irritating.

[01:41](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=101) All that said! You should reduce waiting time as much as you can. Think of it as trimming things down to give players a denser gameplay experience. Movies and cutscenes can serve their own purpose as a sort of palate cleanser.

[01:57](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=117) If anything, it's better to include them than to keep the player busy all the time. But if they're just needless downtime, try tightening them up. Leaner cutscenes tend to be a better fit for games nowadays. When I'm making a game, I pay attention to actual playtime— the amount of time the player has control.

[02:17](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=137) Out of the time spent with the game, how long is the player in control? How long are they not? And how much is just emptiness? Every second I'm not letting people enjoy the game or have fun is something I feel bad about.

[02:45](https://youtu.be/3qEKNxp_-Qs?t=165) This might sound like an exaggeration, but I'll say it anyway: To make players wait is a sin! Considering the scale of games these days, a bit of spectacle might be a necessity, but with a little trimming and slimming, you'll get right where you need to be.
