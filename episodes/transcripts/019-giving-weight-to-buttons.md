[00:03](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=3) Think about your time playing games. Have the buttons ever felt "heavy" to you, or harder to press? They have to me.

[00:13](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=13) I'm not talking about mechanical resistance like in the PS5's DualSense controller. I mean how the same physical button can feel either “lighter" or “heavier" depending on how the game is presented or designed. This might be harder to pick up on with an analog stick or in 3D gameplay, but consider the control pad on an NES controller.

[00:38](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=38) Depending on the game, moving your character around can feel radically different.

[00:47](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=47) Even just moving slowly or quickly can drastically change how buttons "feel" and other factors like inertia, jump physics, lag, and frame rate can also have an effect. Even with the same button presses, the response you see on-screen can make them feel so very different.

[01:06](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=66) I consider this a powerful tool in a game maker's arsenal— so try to pay attention to it! Video games are played by linking a person with a computer. The player reçeives information through their eyes and ears, then sends information back through hand-based input.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=85) You might assume both types of transfer are only one-way, yet, somehow, you can feel feedback with your hands. Take that into account, Take that intoaccount, and it might just change how you design your game.

[01:41](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=101) Take the fighters in Smash Bros., for example. They all differ in movement speed, vulnerability, and animation flair. Not only does this create the “push and pull” that makes for a fun versus match, it also makes for characters that feel wildly different in your hands.

[01:58](https://youtu.be/9qzUwoKT9T8?t=118) Did you know that each console has a slightly different analog stick, which we have to account for when developing games? Tilt sensitivity— the lowest value at which stick tilt is detected— is something we have to configure every time.
