[00:04](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=4) Do you enjoy running errands in video games? In other words, when the story sends you back and forth across the game world. With older games, I'd say most people didn't like it much, but that may not hold true with more modern games.

[00:18](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=18) Naturally, that's because games have grown better and more diverse. By including tasks like combat or foraging, they can provide fulfilment throughout a journey.

[01:09](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=69) Even if the errands are the same, there are surely better ways these ideas can be expressed. Now, I'd like to go over five criteria for better errands in games. Number one: fitting rewards and returns. Put simply, it's important to receive things that are useful in the game.

[01:29](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=89) Watching an enjoyable story unfold as you complete tasks can be a reward in itself, too. Number two: frequent moments of joy. Within even a single errand, try to include many pleasing moments throughout the experience. It can be enough for your combat or foraging to provide interesting rewards, but an unexpected twist could prove beneficial as well.

[01:57](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=117) Number three: the freedom to strategize. Will players take the safer, longer route? Or will they take the more dangerous shortcut? That might be an oversimplification, but it's good to give players space to formulate their own strategies. They'll feel better if they choose the path forward for themselves.

[02:21](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=141) Number four: a speedy exit once the deed is done. This includes tricks like whisking players back to town after they complete an objective. This may make more sense if you think of it like traveling, where the journey home isn't quite as exciting as the journey there.

[02:37](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=157) I'd say it's better to let players focus on the parts that are actually fun. Number five: a sense of future developments. Imagine an errand that asks the player to travel a short distance within town. Don't send players off with uninteresting tasks like, "Ġo buy me some meat!"

[02:56](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=176) You need to provide some mystery that ties into your world and setting. You could send the player that same distance, for example, to look for a girl no one has seen lately at a time when children have been going missing— and task them with investigating a house people have been hearing strange noises from.

[03:15](https://youtu.be/IM9P9NIKo10?t=195) Mechanically speaking, it's just another straightforward errand to set some programming flags, but if you're going to make players move around, there are plenty of better ways to package it.
