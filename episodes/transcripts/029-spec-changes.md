[00:14](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=14) When you change a game's spec, you end up scrapping work that's already done, and causing detours in development. That'll cost you money, and it might demotivate your team, so you really should try to avoid it.

[00:29](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=29) Still, even if you've laid out every detail of your game in advance, some things won't become clear until you actually sit down to make it. That is to say, if you want to make your game ready to show to the world, first you have to finish it— and if you try to envision a finished game right from the get-go, you might spend as much time writing documentation as you do actually developing the game itself.

[00:56](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=56) Plus, things might change depending on the direction the team has taken things. And even within the same team, different programmers can produce considerably different results. Schedule concerns, too, might force you into spec changes.

[01:10](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=70) It happens all the time, really. What you really want to avoid is having to change course because your game's not fun. Try to have the fun in mind straight from the planning stages. Creative works are a combination of a wide variety of elements.

[01:32](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=92) For example, to make a single enemy character move requires: planning, art design, modeling, animation, visual effects, programming, scripting, sound effects, and plenty more.

[01:44](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=104) If any of these upstream tasks get behind schedule, or if the schedule hits a snag somewhere downstream, that's going to halt development. You might change spec because technology and methodology has improved.

[02:15](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=135) In reality, you'll have to figure things out on a case-by-case basis. Don't dig in your heels— pushing forward with an idea that isn't working won't get you anywhere. Oh, and don't keep potential problems to yourself.

But don't underestimate the power of collective knowledge.

[03:05](https://youtu.be/bzWibA7izF8?t=185) Put all possible options on the table, then pick the best solution for your current predicament. You'll also need to make sure your solution works in both the short-term and the long-term. My style is as follows.
