[00:03](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=3) In certain ways, writing stories for games is greatly different than doing so for conventional media. I'd say it's possible to use those differences to your advantage to create something fresh, but let me start by walking you through four or so of these points.

[00:22](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=22) Number one. For those who want to play your game, story can sometimes get in the way. It's fine for people who want to see what happens next, but some games keep you stuck in cutscenes and don't let you actually play.

[00:36](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=36) With action games in particular, this can frustrate people. And skipping cutscenes will just make people worry about missing key story beats. Makes you feel like your story needs to be “skippable." Number two.

[00:48](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=48) The story generally unfolds from the main character's perspective. In other media such as comics, lengthy backstory exposition about comrades or villains is all but a given— but it's not often a good fit for games.

[01:09](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=69) Number three. In stories about fighting an enemy or opposing force, the main character almost always needs to emerge victorious. Have you ever won a battle in a game, only to feel the frustration of losing in the ensuing cutscene?

[01:25](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=85) Negating the player's achievements through story writing can cause some real stress. Number four. Your comrades can't die or be removed from the story. This is an effective story technique in other forms of media, but in games, it can throw off party balance or make the player lose valuable items— so there are certainly challenges here.

[01:49](https://youtu.be/ssvIEm_2mYM?t=109) The four examples I just outlined can surely pose problems in story writing, but you always have the option to disregard these theories and do what feels right. You may upset some of your players, but those feelings might end up making your game and its story even more memorable.
