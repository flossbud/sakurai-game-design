[00:04](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=4) When opinions are divided about design details or game specs, it's tempting to put things to a vote— but you should resist this temptation if you can. There's essentially no point to it.

[00:17](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=17) The main idea is this: the person with the big-picture idea of a project should be the one deciding its appropriate direction. That's the ideal. Putting things to a vote is democratic.

[00:27](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=27) And it's common to think if more people want something, it must be the right answer. But that's just not true for creative projects. Often an idea everyone was against in the planning phase turns out to be a lot of fun once it's actually developed for the game.

[00:44](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=44) In Metal Gear Solid 3, there's a unique system for patching up your wounds. But initially, apparently only four people on the team agreed with the proposal. If you've played the actual game, you'll understand how fun it can be to fix up the areas that are hurt.

[01:01](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=61) Ideas decided by majority end up being the safest ideas. In other words, the most mediocre. That can sometimes be the best choice from a team management perspective, but for a game or other creative work, having a key figure like a director be responsible for calling the shots usually turns out for the best.

[01:23](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=83) That said, the person wielding that power is expected to have a balanced perspective. If they just bark out irrational orders with paper-thin reasoning, you'll end up with something more like a dictatorship. For the person in charge to make the right decisions, they'll need the right information.

[01:41](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=101) Hence, it's best if their team shares everything that's on their minds, from problems to potential solutions. Anyone could make the wrong call when faced with a lack of transparency. Still, I do occasionally put things to my team for a vote. Here are some rough designs for the Mii Fighter's maid outfit.

[02:02](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=122) I personally thought it should be A or B, but decided to ask what my team thought, out of curiosity. It was interesting to see the different opinions people had.

[02:15](https://youtu.be/dP94K69NSVc?t=135) By the way, the option we went with... was A. I made a decision as director to color the socks white, though. That's because brighter extremities make it easier to see a character's movement.
