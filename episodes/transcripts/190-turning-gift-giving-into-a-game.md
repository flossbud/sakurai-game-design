[00:06](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=6) When I was commuting to an office, particularly before the COVID-19 pandemic, I made it a point to bring in gifts for the dev team pretty often. Sometimes I would get a game from a publisher, but since I had already bought it for myself, I'd end up with two copies.

[00:19](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=19) Or sometimes I just bought the same thing twice by accident. Or I bought something and didn't really use it. This included some expensive items like graphics cards or gaming monitors. Occasionally, I'd bring in merchandise from Smash Bros, events, too.

[00:35](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=35) I would give these things to my team members with some frequency. They'd make better use of them than I would.

[00:44](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=44) However, I never just handed things out at random. Instead, I made it into a sort of lottery process. First, I would ask who was interested. I made a list of what was available on the day's Daily Report page, and people would put their names down.

[01:00](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=60) Then, I would pick the winners based on a variety of rules. At times, I would just have them do a rock-paper-scissors tournament. Or they would battle it out in the Smash Bros. we were making. Or settle things in Samurai Kirby.

[01:17](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=77) Or we'd see who could manage to pull off a Kamikazee first. Or I'd pick the person who posted closest to 0 seconds. Of course, in that case, I would keep the rules a secret. I think making games and events out of these things served as a sort of icebreaker for the dev team.

[01:39](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=99) After all, if you're giving something away, the real winner is anyone who has fun.

[01:46](https://youtu.be/CB85OhWD9o8?t=106) By the way, in recent years, I’ve received a wide variety of birthday presents from my team members. I'm extremely grateful for that. It's proof that there's a good relationship between the director and the team.
