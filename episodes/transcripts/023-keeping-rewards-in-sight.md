[00:04](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=4) What motivates players to keep playing a game? Rewards and prizes are one obvious form of motivation, and I'd like to explore that a bit today. I do think it's a shame when a game doesn't keep you thinking about the next reward.

[00:22](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=22) You're looking at a Nihon Falcom game called Ys, which originally released for home computers in 1987. Though this is actually the TurboGrafx-16 version. The main game screen is a bit small, probably because drawing graphics to the screen was slow back then, and they needed a way to reduce processor load.

[00:41](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=41) This means there's plenty of space to put lots of information on-screen, but for now, take a look here. This shows your current EXP as well as how much you'll need to level up. A great feature, right?

[00:56](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=56) The number goes up with each enemy you defeat, which keeps you pushing to reach that next level. It shows how far you've come, and how far you have left to go. Keeping rewards in sight.

[01:10](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=70) Providing a clear objective. Both will serve to motivate players. Here's another title related to Nihon Falcom: a game called Faxanadu. There are only a few items in the shops, and they're all expensive.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=85) Bu once you can afford them, they give you a pretty massive boost. The player scrounges for money, thinking about how much more they need for the next big piece of gear— a real plus for the game.

[01:41](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=101) Games these days are generous with money, and usually have lots of different equipment, so providing this kind of fun isn't as easy as it used to be. Still, placing objectives within reach, and letting players feel the joy of completing them..

[01:58](https://youtu.be/KePsEzN-IAM?t=118) Well, that's an idea we can all learn a thing or two from. Looking back now, I wish I'd done more of it in my games, actually!
