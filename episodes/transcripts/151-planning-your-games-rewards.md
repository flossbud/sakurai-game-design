[00:04](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=4) When making a game, it's essential that you reward your players, but that's not going to be possible if you have nothing to give them. That's why you need to think hard in the planning stages about how exactly you're going to offer rewards.

[00:22](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=22) The most effective way to do this during gameplay is to simply make the player's character stronger. Examples of a simple numeriçal increase would be spénding currency to buy better equipment. or using experience to level up... or unlocking new abilities with skill points.

[00:40](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=40) Maybe an enemy drops a powerful item. Or maybe there's a treasure chest ahead with something valuable inside. Perhaps the player can fuse materials to create new equipment, items, or facilities. These kinds of inclusions make the player want to keep going.

[01:03](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=63) In Kirby games, the player never knows what copy ability they might encounter next, increasing their desire to continue on. You might also give a game depth by making it so that players can lose things they've collected. However, these approaches may work for some concepts, but not all.

[01:25](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=85) Smash Bros. is one game where they don't work as well. In a fighting game where characters grow stronger the further you progress, it might feel like the “real” game doesn't start until you've maxed out all of their levels. To share some examples of how exactly we do reward players, though...

One drawback was that we ended up putting a lot of work into these, thus inflating production costs. Stickers.

[02:30](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=150) In Super Smash Bros. Brawl, we included stickers for players to collect. By sticking them on the bottom of a trophy stand, you can power up the associated fighter. Since we didn't have to write descriptions or create models, we could include far more characters. Spirits.

[03:18](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=198) And remember the Coin Launcher? Music. With how much music we have, some of it ends up allocated as rewards. Still, some people would rather be able to enjoy the music from the very start, so we've cut back on this in recent years.

[04:03](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=243) That was before other game consoles had introduced their own achievement systems, but the functionality was similar. Fulfilling a condition in-game will break its panel and show you the unlock conditions for adjacent panels. Many panels bestow a reward for completing them, but this feature is more about giving players things to work towards rather than rewards.

[04:34](https://youtu.be/44ONFHla-sw?t=274) You could say that game objectives are rewards in their own right, and that motivation and reward are simply two sides of the same coin. It can be difficult to strike a balance and not go overboard with these kinds of systems, but a game without any rewards or new developments is a drab game, I'd say.
