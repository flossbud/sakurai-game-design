[00:45](https://youtu.be/FfPN4ZGgBpo?t=45) But when you start using 3D polygons in a perspective view, the collision points become more difficult to recognize. Meaning if you don't set your terrain elements apart from non-terrain elements, the player won't know where they are.

[01:02](https://youtu.be/FfPN4ZGgBpo?t=62) I think it's fair to say that the Smash Bros. backdrops are pretty elaborate. But when our artists put too much work into the areas without collision— making those areas stand out— the crucial terrain with collision runs the risk of getting lost.

[01:18](https://youtu.be/FfPN4ZGgBpo?t=78) I usually tell them, “your backdrop's outshining your terrain." Make the objects with collision stand out— and give the backdrop less emphasis. Though, this isn't so easy when you're also trying to make the stage look good. One simple solution could be to apply aerial perspective to anything that's not terrain, to reduce its presence.

[01:43](https://youtu.be/FfPN4ZGgBpo?t=103) But if that's the only thing you do... Well, the stage elements with collision are clearer, but it really hurts the overall look. A game's visual appeal is important, too.

[01:58](https://youtu.be/FfPN4ZGgBpo?t=118) Some might say visuals are irrelevant to gameplay, but that won't matter if nobody buys the game. It's all about striking a balance, though I'd say it's best to give gameplay the slight edge.
