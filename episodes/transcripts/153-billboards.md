[00:04](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=4) A "billboard" is a flat object that always directly faces the camera. Most of the time, these are two-polygon meshes.

[00:14](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=14) In Super Mario 64, these iron balls that roll down the slope are actually made using billboards. Each one is just a single image that doesn't have any animation. Billboards are used like this for King Bob-omb as well.

[00:35](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=35) His eyes, crown, and more are displayed as 3D models, while the main body underneath is a billboard.

[00:48](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=48) Now, if you're wondering why I'm talking about this in an Effects video.. it's because particles and other similar effects are generally displayed using billboards. Let's stop this explosion and rotate the camera. You can see how it's just flat images.

[01:06](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=66) Though of course, we still need extra touches like sparks. 2D objects have a relatively low rendering cost. And billboards are just perfect for intangible effects such as flashes of light. By the way, all of the food items in Smash Bros. have traditionally been billboards.

[01:26](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=86) That's why they look a little strage when you rotate the camera around them. You could use a “Y-axis billboard," which makes the image face the camera based only on rotation around the Y-axis, but misusing this for effects can sometimes leave you with images that feel a bit too skinny.

[01:45](https://youtu.be/_o29OOOarPY?t=105) With how detailed games of today tend to be, it's nearly impossible to tell when a game is using billboards. Awareness of them has lessened as a result, so I wanted to touch on them here today.
