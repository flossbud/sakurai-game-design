# 071 · Do We Really Need Enemies?

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atqS7SImuBc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/071-do-we-really-need-enemies.md)

## In one sentence
You can take enemies out of a game, but not the stress they provide: build every loop as stress → relief → growth → progress, and if there is no combat, find another source of stress.

## Key ideas
- It's worth questioning conventions such as "games need enemies to defeat." Things we expect usually exist for a reason, and asking why shows you what the reason is.
- Most games create fun by first applying stress and then relieving it. An obstacle is stress. Removing it is relief, the feeling of a weight being lifted. The reward you get (an item or experience that makes you stronger) is growth, which brings satisfaction and excitement for what comes next. The next obstacle then becomes easier, which is progress.
- Relief, growth and progress are what actually make the game fun, but they're hard to set up without the initial stress. So games really do need obstacles.
- Undertale can be finished without defeating enemies, but it still has plenty of stress. On the Pacifist route you overcome enemies indirectly, which may be *more* stressful. Relieving that extra stress feels extra rewarding, and players expect greater progress for it.
- Stress comes in many forms. In adventure games the hero and friends may be in peril. Needing to pay for things is stress and relief too: an expensive item gives you something to save up for and look forward to.
- A mining game where you get infinite resources but have no way to use them would be pointless.

## Do this
- Map your core loop as four steps: stress (obstacle), relief (obstacle removed), growth (a reward that makes the player stronger), progress (the next obstacle becomes possible). For each, write what in your game supplies it, and fix any step that's missing.
- If you're cutting or reducing combat, replace enemies with another source of stress: characters in peril, environmental obstacles, or costs the player must save up for.
- Give every resource the player collects a use, including at least one expensive goal worth saving toward.
- When an obstacle is harder or more stressful than usual (like a non-violent solution), pay it off with bigger relief and more progress.

## Avoid
- Removing obstacles entirely. With no stress there's no relief, growth or progress.
- Resources that pile up endlessly with nothing to spend them on.

## Examples from the episode
- **Undertale — Pacifist route:** no enemies need to be defeated, yet overcoming them indirectly creates even more stress and a bigger sense of reward.
- **Adventure games:** characters in peril are stress, and finding a way out is the relief.
- **Hypothetical mining game:** infinite resources with no use would be pointless. Spending is part of the stress-and-relief loop.

## Quote
> To summarize, it's possible to take enemies out of games if you want, but you still need the stress and relief they provide. (02:56)
