# 106 · Posing Suggestions

**Category:** Animation · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LsSF_H-W-w8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/106-posing-suggestions.md)

## In one sentence
Develop an eye for cool poses by physically handling an action figure—breaking symmetry and straight lines, angling the head and limbs, and shifting the balance off-center until it looks good—because if the fundamental pose is off, no finishing touch will save it.

## Key ideas
- "Bundodo," the Japanese word for zooming an action figure around by hand, may be a good game for animation designers. Unless you're going for realistic motion, poses should look cool, and handling a physical figure teaches you a lot about what makes a pose cool and which angle works best.
- With a figma of Pit, the plain standing pose isn't exciting. Adjusted, it looks "pretty dashing."
- His posing tips:
  - Avoid left-right symmetry, and don't face straight forward.
  - Lower the chin and angle the head so you don't get right angles.
  - Don't let the torso, hips and legs follow the same lines (an "S" curve).
  - Angle the figure to show the scarf's flow, and let cloth billow.
  - Square the shoulders and raise them a little if you can.
  - Set the arms at different angles, bend them with the silhouette in mind, and keep them close to the torso.
  - Bend the wrists slightly forward rather than straight in line with the arms.
  - Shift the center of gravity off the middle.
  - Turn the feet slightly outward and keep them at different distances from the camera.
  - Give the wings a sense of physicality.
- In the end there's no formula or logic to posing. It's about finding what looks good, and poses that imply motion add even more to consider.
- Smash Bros. artwork often ranks high in image searches for characters. It's polished right down to the finishing touches, but it all comes down to posing: if the basic pose is off, it won't look right.

## Do this
- Keep a posable figure at your desk and practice posing it (and swooshing it around) to train your eye for angles and cool silhouettes.
- For a standing pose, break symmetry, turn the body off the straight-on view, lower the chin and tilt the head so no right angles show.
- Give the torso, hips and legs different directions to form an "S" curve, and shift the weight off center.
- Square and slightly raise the shoulders, put the arms at different bent angles close to the torso, and tip the wrists slightly forward.
- Turn the feet slightly outward at different depths from the camera, and pose cloth (scarves, skirts) and wings so they show flow and physical weight.
- Lock the base pose before polishing details, then judge the result by eye rather than by checklist.

## Avoid
- Symmetrical, front-facing, straight-standing poses.
- Straight wrists and arms held far from the body.
- Polishing renders or details while the basic pose is still off.

## Examples from the episode
- **Pit (figma figure):** the default standing pose vs. an adjusted pose that applies all the tips above.
- **Super Smash Bros. — official character artwork:** high-ranking in image searches and polished down to the finishing touches, but its quality rests on posing.
