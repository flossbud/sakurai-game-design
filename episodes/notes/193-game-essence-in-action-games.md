# 193 · Game Essence in Action Games

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EzwCc2_8OzU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/193-game-essence-in-action-games.md)

## In one sentence
Every action-game verb (jumping, dashing, melee, shooting, dodging, parrying, hiding) should pair a danger with an opportunity, so understand why each one's risk and reward works instead of just copying it.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai defines game essence as taking risks and earning rewards. In every action below, danger and opportunity are two sides of the same coin. Thinking about the meaning behind each kind of game essence makes it more likely you'll find something even more fun, so go beyond mere imitation.
- **Jumping:** the closer you get to a ledge before jumping, the bigger the gap you can clear, but the easier it is to fall off.
- **Dashing and running:** you move faster, but your character may be harder to control, you may lose stamina, or you may run straight into an enemy or hazard.
- **Close-range attacks:** if you can only hit enemies up close, getting close means they can hit you, but it also lets you attack.
- **Firearms:** you can attack one-sidedly, but you use up resources like ammunition, and often the longer a weapon's range, the less ammo it has. Enemies may shoot back, and finding a good firing position is its own risk and reward.
- **Dodging:** evades an attack right before it lands, with downsides such as other actions being briefly harder to do. Parrying extends the idea: by waiting until the moment an attack is about to hit, you turn massive risk into massive reward.
- **Hiding:** you can't attack while hidden, so lowering the risk of being found also lowers the possible reward. You can still take risks, such as waiting for an enemy to come close.

## Do this
- List every core action in your game with its risk and its reward. If an action has no downside, add one, such as a dash that reduces control, costs stamina or can carry you into hazards.
- Make jump distance depend on how close to the edge the player takes off, so the longest jumps require risky edge timing.
- Tie weapon range to resources, for example giving longer-range weapons less ammo, and make positioning matter because enemies can fire back.
- Give dodges a short window where other actions are harder to do. Make parries require timing right at the moment of impact, and reward them heavily.
- Let hiding reduce both risk and reward, while still allowing bold plays like waiting for an enemy to come close.
- When you borrow a mechanic from another game, first write down why its risk and reward work, then build your version from that reason.

## Avoid
- Copying actions from other games without understanding the risk and reward that make them fun.
- Actions that give their benefit with no matching danger.
