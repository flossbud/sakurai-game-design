# 160 · Super Smash Bros. Ultimate

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RDrNWAHaKfI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/160-super-smash-bros-ultimate.md)

## In one sentence
Pick the bold concept that your current production situation uniquely makes possible (Ultimate's "Everyone is here!" was only viable because the team and toolset carried over), then plan exhaustively, rebuild the fundamentals for the new feel, and design within hard limits on labor, storage and secrecy.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai was asked to make a Smash for Switch before the Switch was even announced. It was the last mission he received from Mr. Iwata. The core concept was "Everyone is here!" Earlier games in the series were each made by an entirely new team. Brawl and Smash for 3DS/Wii U changed company and team completely, so no assets or know-how carried over, and differing hardware meant rebuilding almost everything. Smash for and Ultimate were both made by Bandai Namco Studios, in the same office, with many of the same members and an intact dev environment, on similar Wii U and Switch architecture. That made bringing back every fighter feasible. Only seven past fighters were missing from Smash for and its DLC: Pichu, Snake, Young Link, Ice Climbers, Wolf, Squirtle and Ivysaur.
- Each fighter has its own rightsholders and costs a great deal of money and effort, so new Smash games usually drop old fighters to make room. Sakurai saw this as the one chance to include everyone. To keep the dev team together, he finished the Ultimate proposal on the same day the final Smash for 3DS/Wii U video presentation went out. The proposal ran over 200 pages of new features and adjustments. Not everything made it in; it even mentioned VR support, before Nintendo Labo existed.
- New fighters drew on a past fan survey with about 1.8 million valid votes, not counting repeat votes from the same person. At the start of development Sakurai listed an average of 70–100 potential changes per fighter, covering graphics, audio, moves, animation and more, not just mechanics and specs.
- Tempo: it was raised from Brawl to Smash for, then raised again for Ultimate, because players knew Smash better and this was a home console. It still stays slower than Melee, sitting between Melee and Smash for. Jumps and knockback were sped up too. Raising the tempo meant none of Smash for's parameters could be reused; that work has to be redone for every game.
- Stages and storage: about 100 stages, or 300 counting the Battlefield and Omega forms, but fewer brand-new stages than before, because designing them takes too long. The cartridge capped the ROM at about 16 GB, less in practice, about half the Wii U's 25 GB discs. Sakurai feared he'd have to turn the music library into free downloadable content, but compression fit all of it into a quarter of the space it took in Smash for.
- Solo content: a Subspace Emissary-style mode (spotlight movies, side-scrolling stages and enemies for every fighter) was impossible at this content volume. Without Smash for's customization and power-up systems there was nothing to build fun around there, Trophies were cut for labor cost, and a Melee-style Adventure Mode is "just one versus battle after another". Spirits replaced Trophies: you can use their power, you earn them by winning battles that recreate their source games using Smash's borrowed characters, worlds and sounds, and you can level them. World of Light, a story that starts with loss, wraps those battles in one mode. Players can brute-force it with strong Spirits, which is left in their hands like a difficulty setting, but being overpowered earns fewer rewards. More Spirits were added as new games released.
- Release and DLC: announced March 2018, "Everyone is Here!" revealed that June, released that December. Joker (Persona 5) was announced on December 6, the day before launch, with Piranha Plant as a bonus. COVID-19 forced remote work in the middle of DLC development, which raised worries about leaks, made alignment and playtesting harder (Sakurai's lunch-break versus tests had to run online and hit connection trouble) and delayed many games industry-wide. All DLC still shipped by the end of 2021, after three years, almost as long as the base game took. Sakurai thinks it would be hard to push the series any further, and he started this channel with his free time afterward.

## Do this
- Before choosing a sequel's headline concept, take stock of what carries over (team, office, tools, assets, hardware architecture) and pick the ambitious idea that this continuity makes uniquely affordable.
- Have the next project's proposal written before the current one wraps, so the team can move straight on instead of scattering.
- At kickoff, list concrete adjustments for every character or major asset across all disciplines (graphics, audio, moves, animation, specs). Sakurai averaged 70–100 per fighter.
- If you change the game's tempo, plan to retune every gameplay parameter from scratch, including jumps and knockback, instead of porting the old values.
- When a big solo mode is out of budget, build one from your game's existing strengths (Spirits reuse borrowed characters, worlds and sounds). Let players over-power themselves as a self-chosen difficulty, but cut rewards when they do.
- On size-limited media, test compression on your biggest asset sets (like music) early, before you decide to cut content or move it to a download.

## Avoid
- Carrying old tuning values into a game whose speed has changed.
- Solo modes that are just a string of versus fights. Sakurai says nobody would be satisfied with that now.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — "Everyone is here!":** made feasible by keeping Bandai Namco Studios' team and dev environment from Smash for. Only seven missing veterans had to be rebuilt.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — tempo:** faster than Smash for, slower than Melee, with jumps and knockback sped up and all parameters reworked.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Spirits / World of Light:** a cheaper replacement for Trophies and a Subspace Emissary-style story. Spirits are earned in battles that recreate their source games, can be leveled, and give fewer rewards when you're overpowered.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — 16 GB cartridge:** compression fit the music library into 1/4 of Smash for's space.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate DLC — Joker and remote work:** Joker announced the day before launch, then three years of DLC finished under pandemic remote work.
