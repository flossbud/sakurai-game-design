# 034 · Making a Living Making Games

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_-WyivvfibM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/034-making-a-living-making-games.md)

## In one sentence
The quickest way into making games for a living is to make a game yourself: finish it, get people to play it, learn from their reactions, and show originality when you apply.

## Key ideas
- Most people who ask how to break into the industry just want to make a living doing what they love. Sakurai doesn't criticize that; everyone starts that way, including him.
- Setting aside specialized roles like artists and composers, the quickest (if not only) way in is to make a game yourself, with Unity, Unreal Engine, C, JavaScript, visual programming languages or "Maker" software.
- Even aspiring designers will need to think like a programmer at times, and making a game shows you what problems come up and how to solve them.
- When starting out it's fine to mimic an existing game; making anything at all is a feat.
- Once you're satisfied, let people play it: friends, free online distribution, or selling it if you're confident (which also gives some PR experience). Find out what they think, then improve the game or carry the lessons into your next project.
- Send the game with your job application, even if you don't meet every listed requirement; companies are always looking for people who can make fun games. But an imitative idea won't help, because many people want to make games, so let your originality shine.
- Some people prefer to work alone or in very small teams; a solo developer keeps all the profits. Even a small first game is a vital step, and if it doesn't work out, at least you learn it may not be your path.

## Do this
- Start a small game this week in any tool you can get (Unity, Unreal Engine, C, JavaScript, a visual programming language or "Maker" software), even if you want to be a designer rather than a programmer.
- For a first project, recreate or riff on an existing game if that helps, and focus on finishing it.
- When it's done, have friends play it or release it free online, ask what they think, and either improve it or apply the lessons to your next game.
- Before applying to studios, make a game with an original idea and send it with your application, even if you don't meet every listed requirement.

## Avoid
- Submitting an imitative game idea as your showcase.
- Holding off on applying because you don't meet every job requirement.
