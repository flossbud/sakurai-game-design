# 114 · Explain Ideas to Everyone at Once

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CafmzxRBo4Y) · [Transcript](../transcripts/114-explain-ideas-to-everyone-at-once.md)

## In one sentence
Give instructions and feedback to every relevant team member at once in a shared session, instead of relaying through one person, because it costs some meeting time but greatly improves understanding and visibility.

## Key ideas
- On Kid Icarus: Uprising, Sakurai would explain something to a planner, the planner would explain it to the programmers, and what came back was sometimes the polar opposite of what he wanted. The team came from many different work cultures, so his usual method sometimes gave poor results.
- From Smash for 3DS/Wii U onward, everyone involved had to attend whenever he gave instructions or feedback. When he supervised Inkling's special-move spec docs, for example, the planners, programmers, modelers, animators, effect designers, sound designers and their team leaders all came, where before only the planner had heard it.
- **Setup:** a large room near his desk with a huge monitor for the build or his PC screen. Around 30 people on average and up to 50 on big days, all pulling up chairs. Anyone could sit in, even from unrelated teams. He used a mic so everyone could hear and took questions at the end.
- Separately, fighter modelers, artists, stage modelers, animators and others held their own group review sessions. Seeing everyone's feedback, not just their own, helps people learn and grow. He had used that part on earlier projects too.
- More meetings can look wasteful, but this worked very well. Everyone understood his ideas and goals much more clearly, anyone unsure knew what to do whatever their role, and visibility improved on a large project where it's often hard to know what others are doing. It only works if the meetings stay quick. The team seemed to appreciate it.
- Aside: he kept the room stocked with sweets and snacks he bought himself (lots from Bourbon Foods). He stopped when the pandemic moved work remote.

## Do this
- When giving feedback on a feature, gather every discipline that touches it (planner, programmers, modelers, animators, VFX, sound, and their leads) in one session instead of briefing only the planner.
- Show the actual build or your screen on a large display, use a mic so everyone hears, and save time for questions at the end.
- Leave these sessions open so people from other teams can sit in.
- Run regular group reviews within each discipline (modelers, animators, etc.) so everyone sees the feedback others get.
- Keep each session brisk so the extra meeting time stays worth it.

## Avoid
- Relaying a director's intent through a chain of people (director → planner → programmers), where it can come back reversed.
- Letting group feedback meetings drag on. The system only works if they stay speedy.

## Examples from the episode
- **Kid Icarus: Uprising — relayed instructions:** explanations passed through a planner sometimes came back as the polar opposite of what was intended.
- **Super Smash Bros. — Inkling's special-move spec review:** every involved discipline and its leaders sat in on the supervision session (these sessions drew about 30 people on average, up to 50).

## Quote
> By having team members see the feedback everyone was getting—not just their own— they could all learn and grow. (02:06)
