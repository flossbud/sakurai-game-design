# 257 · No Masterpiece Has Bad Audio

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fqTlWgfCL0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/257-no-masterpiece-has-bad-audio.md)

## In one sentence
Treat music and sound effects as essential rather than secondary, because audio is what players remember first and what hits them in the heart, and no masterpiece has bad audio.

## Key ideas
- After experiencing a lot of games, Sakurai believes no masterpiece has bad audio, and he grows more convinced the more games he plays.
- Apart from very old games with no audio at all, even titles with nothing more than simple noise-based sound effects had well-constructed audio.
- Some masterpieces have bad graphics, and some even lack coherent game essence, but he can't think of a single masterpiece with bad audio design. "Audio" here means both music and sound effects.
- Taste in music and sound effects varies, so you can't definitively say a piece of audio is or isn't well made. But think back: when you remember a game you really enjoyed, the music and sound effects are often the first things that come to mind.
- More than the game itself, its visuals or its characters, audio is what hits you right in the heart, so it's not something to take lightly.
- Possible exceptions: mobile games, handheld games played with the sound down, or play in surroundings where you couldn't hear the game. You can still enjoy those. But when you can listen without constraints and the audio is good, it boosts your enjoyment that much more.

## Do this
- Give music and sound effects the same priority and schedule as graphics, instead of leaving them for the end.
- Even with limited sound technology or simple effects, construct every sound deliberately; even old games with only simple noise-based effects had well-constructed audio.
- List the games you loved and note which music and sound effects come to mind first, and use that as the bar for your own audio.

## Avoid
- Taking audio lightly or treating it as something to bolt on after the "real" game is done.

## Examples from the episode
- **Games with only simple noise-based sound effects:** still had well-constructed audio.
- **Mobile games and handhelds played muted:** the possible exception, still enjoyable without sound, though good audio would have added more.

## Quote
> Even more than the game itself, its visuals, or its characters— audio is what hits you right in the heart. (01:33)
