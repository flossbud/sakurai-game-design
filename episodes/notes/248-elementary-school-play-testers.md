# 248 · Elementary School Play Testers

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aACUZ769djE) · [Transcript](../transcripts/248-elementary-school-play-testers.md)

## In one sentence
If you can get permission, have elementary school children play your game in a relaxed, fun setting and quietly watch them, because their honest reactions expose control problems and boredom that adults hide.

## Key ideas
- An old method that "yielded great results": the dev team's children, relatives and their friends came in to play during development. Snacks and a casual mood made it feel less like play testing and more like a fun gaming session with friends.
- Compared to "jaded adults," children always give honest reactions: where the controls are hard for them, and what they get bored with.
- Sakurai didn't ask them questions. He stood back and watched how they responded to the game.
- The sessions also lifted the dev team. When you're pushing through especially tough work, moments like this drive you forward.
- They stopped doing it. Social media, the team's wish for higher confidentiality, and greater concern for children's well-being made these sessions hard to arrange. Still, if you can get permission, he says it's absolutely worth doing.
- Anecdote: playing the original Smash Bros., one child shouted "Whoa! Look how far he flew!" at a Falcon Punch's knockback. He may have been the first member of the public to land a Falcon Punch.

## Do this
- Get the needed permissions (parents, company confidentiality), then invite young children, such as staff members' kids and their friends, to play a current build.
- Make it feel like play, not a test: let them come with friends, provide snacks, and keep the setting casual.
- Watch from a distance instead of interviewing them. Note where they struggle with the controls and where they lose interest.
- Share the kids' reactions with the team, especially during hard stretches, as a morale boost.

## Avoid
- Asking children directly what they think instead of observing what they do.
- Running sessions without permission, given today's confidentiality and child well-being concerns.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. (original) — Falcon Punch:** a child playtester shouted with excitement at how far it knocked someone back, the kind of unfiltered reaction these sessions capture.

## Quote
> Compared to us jaded adults, children are the purest of the pure! When they're playing a game, you'll always get their honest reactions. (00:31)
