# 089 · Audio as Fiction and Non-Fiction

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Ns03q8noEA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/089-audio-as-fiction-and-non-fiction.md)

## In one sentence
Choose deliberately between exaggerated and realistic sound. Exaggeration sells impact in games like fighters, which are nothing like real fighting, while realistic sounds can supply information that simple graphics can't.

## Key ideas
- If 2D or 3D fighting games used truly realistic sounds, he doubts they'd be as popular. Real punches would just sound like a bunch of slaps, which feels lacking.
- Street Fighter II's impact sounds are incredibly fake. They remind him of old Showa-era yakuza films. Culturally, Japan has always leaned into exaggerated sound effects, even outside anime.
- In both film and games, the more realistic the visuals, the stranger exaggerated sounds feel.
- If you don't remember from the start that fighting games are nothing like real fighting, you'll drift far from the game essence you want.
- Realistic sounds have benefits too: they can give meaning to things that would otherwise have none. Minecraft's surprisingly realistic sounds fill in missing information. When building, the different "thunk" sounds help you picture what you're building and how, and despite the simple cube graphics you can feel the weight of wood and stone.
- Sound can be exaggerated or realistic. Finding the right balance for your game makes its world feel more substantial.

## Do this
- For each category of sound, decide its job: selling impact (exaggerate it) or conveying material and information (make it realistic).
- Exaggerate combat hit sounds instead of using real recorded impacts, then compare the two side by side to hear how weak the realistic version is.
- In games with simple or abstract visuals, give each material its own realistic sound (wood vs. stone, for example) so players can feel weight and texture the graphics don't show.
- Check how exaggerated your sounds are against how realistic your visuals are. The more realistic the visuals, the more exaggerated sounds stand out.

## Avoid
- Literal realism in combat audio. Real punches are just slaps.
- Forgetting that a fighting game is not real fighting, which pulls you away from the game essence you want.

## Examples from the episode
- **Street Fighter II — impact sounds:** wildly fake yet effective, in the tradition of exaggerated Showa-era film sound.
- **Realistic fighting-game sounds (demo):** what fighters would sound like with real impacts, which is lacking.
- **Minecraft — building sounds:** realistic sounds convey what you're building and the weight of wood and stone despite simple graphics.
