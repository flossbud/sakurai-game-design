# 178 · What is “Play”?

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5uooUqdXXs) · [Transcript](../transcripts/178-what-is-play.md)

## In one sentence
Use Caillois's four elements of play (competition, chance, imitation and vertigo) as a checklist: build your core around clever competitive play, then add chance, real-world imitation and visceral sensation on top.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai summarizes Roger Caillois's 1958 book *Man, Play and Games*, which is often cited on what makes games fun and defines four elements of play:
  - **Agon (competition):** sports and competitive games.
  - **Alea (chance):** gambling and randomization.
  - **Mimicry (mimesis, imitation):** imitating something else, including simulator and job-based games.
  - **Ilinx (vertigo):** think of a roller coaster or a high-speed scrolling game.
- The four don't cover everything games offer, but breaking play down this way is important, and fun too.
- What Sakurai calls game essence, or push and pull, is mainly agon. In his view, the winner isn't decided by raw physical advantage but by clever plays.
- His games use the other three as well:
  - **Alea:** randomized features, such as randomly appearing items that can sway the outcome.
  - **Mimicry:** raises the density and richness of a game's information. Floors look hard, windups look powerful and hits sound painful, because that's how those things are in reality.
  - **Ilinx:** makes a game feel viscerally good, like knocking an enemy back after a tough struggle, or the fear of falling.
- His own view is that play is like training that gives us energy for daily life. It's doing something and having fun, plus everything that grows out of it and draws on body and mind: exercise, reflexes, insight, intellect, emotion, rules and society. It brings intangible rewards without feeling hard the way real training does, and people even use it to recover.
- Doing things purely to entertain ourselves, with no link to productivity, is "anything but meaningless". Those varied experiences help us work out how best to live.

## Do this
- List where each of the four elements appears in your game and decide which one is the core. For a push-and-pull action game, that's agon.
- Tune competitive play so clever plays (reads, timing, positioning), not raw stat or size advantages, decide who wins.
- Add a measured dose of chance that can swing results, such as items that appear at random.
- Make things look and sound like their real counterparts so they carry information: floors that look hard, windups that look powerful, hits that sound painful.
- Build moments of visceral sensation, like a big knockback payoff after a tough exchange or real danger and fear around falling.

## Examples from the episode
- ***Man, Play and Games* (Roger Caillois, 1958):** the source of the agon, alea, mimicry and ilinx framework.
- **Roller coasters and high-speed scrolling games:** examples of ilinx.
- **Sakurai's own games:** randomly appearing items (alea), hard-looking floors and painful-sounding hits (mimicry), knockback after a struggle and the fear of falling (ilinx).
