# 040 · Super Smash Bros. Melee

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLRRNsAVl-8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/040-super-smash-bros-melee.md)

## In one sentence
A sequel's concept can simply be "power up everything": use the new hardware's headroom to upgrade every element, and add the special touches that set the game apart.

## Key ideas
- Most of Sakurai's concepts are logical, solving a problem, but Melee's was simple: power up Smash Bros. by leaps and bounds. Fighters, stages, graphics, effects, animations, sounds, content, play feel, items and bonus features all got a massive upgrade.
- The N64 was very limiting: a fighter in the original Smash Bros. was about 300 polygons, lowered to about 200 with three or more fighters, about as low as home console fighters go (hence Samus's triangle legs). Fighter textures were limited to 64x64 pixels, sometimes 32x32.
- The GameCube had vastly superior specs and was much easier to develop for. Melee was also his first optical-disc game.
- Streaming playback let him use music recorded with real instruments, so he used orchestral arrangements for several tracks. That led to the PRESS START concerts, which ran ten years, and the many composer collaborations in later Smash games.
- It was his first game with a CG movie; with no spare time, he gave up his New Year's holiday to draw the storyboards.
- The Trophy system worked around the limit on fighters: it brought in other characters from across Nintendo's history, with background info as fan service. Creatures Inc., among others, helped get the project out the door.
- "Is this game worth the immense labor?" He asks himself this too, but most creative works have to do what they can to stand out from the crowd, so he worked to make it extra special. Melee became the top seller among all GameCube titles.

## Do this
- When a sequel moves to stronger hardware, list every element (fighters, stages, graphics, effects, animation, sound, content, play feel, items, bonus features) and set an upgrade target for each.
- Use what the new platform makes possible (Melee used streaming audio for orchestral, real-instrument music) to do something the old one couldn't.
- When you can't make every character playable, add a lighter-weight collection, such as trophies with background info, to bring more of the franchise in as fan service.
- Decide which touches will make your game feel extra special and set it apart (Melee's were orchestral music and a CG movie), and commit to them.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. (N64) — fighter budget:** about 300 polygons per fighter (about 200 with three or more), 64x64 or 32x32 textures; Samus's legs are made of triangles.
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee — music:** streaming playback enabled orchestral arrangements with real instruments.
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee — CG movie:** his first; he drew the storyboards over his New Year's holiday.
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee — Trophy system:** brought characters from across Nintendo's history in as collectibles with background info.
