# 123 · Daily Report Suggestions

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T6XgiBzYbzs) · [Transcript](../transcripts/123-daily-report-suggestions.md)

## In one sentence
As a director, post a daily internal report (a screenshot plus 3–5 varied topics, open to comments), because it builds trust, gets read where email doesn't, and keeps the whole team seeing the big picture.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai started writing a Daily Report for his team during Smash Bros. Brawl and wrote one every day he worked, even on days that were supposed to be off. It was internal only. His public "Screenshot of the Day" on Twitter grew out of the daily screenshot he shared with the team.
- Format: a screenshot at the top, then three to five topics, such as project news, what he was working on, checklists and issues to fix. Sometimes he had to be stern. Other times he gave impressions of games he played, talked about current events or shared his thoughts. Team members could comment on each topic and talk with each other there.
- Benefit 1, getting known: he doesn't belong to a larger company and has no team of his own, so he joins existing teams as an outsider. Taking direction from an unknown person doesn't feel good; after reading his reports, he isn't a total stranger.
- Benefit 2, sharing information: team-wide emails often go unread, but a report with something new every day is something people keep up with on their own. When he announced he'd reveal the next fighter (Sephiroth) there at 5 p.m., so many people opened the page that the server went down, from the dev team alone.
- Benefits 3 and 4, productivity and motivation: writing regularly is good training, and directors must be highly productive without exception. He also varies the reports so a team working on the same thing day after day gets variety and stimulus for new ideas.
- Benefit 5, tracking progress: on big teams, each person's work is one small part and they can lose sight of the big picture. The screenshot served as a progress report for the whole team. Others posted what they had finished that day, or personal news, which shows their personalities and creates an open team environment.
- He also set up an "opinions board" when joining a team, an easy way for people to submit suggestions and requests, and found it effective.

## Do this
- Post a short internal report every workday: one current screenshot at the top, then 3–5 topics (news, what you're working on, checklists, issues to fix).
- Allow comments on each topic so the team can reply and talk to each other.
- Mix in non-work topics (a game you played, current events, your thoughts) so each report feels fresh and the team gets to know you.
- Put important announcements in the daily report rather than a mass email, since a page with something new every day actually gets read.
- Invite team members to post what they finished that day so everyone sees the overall progress.
- Set up an "opinions board" where anyone can easily submit suggestions and requests.

## Avoid
- Counting on team-wide emails to spread important information; they often go unread.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Brawl:** where the Daily Report habit began.
- **Screenshot of the Day (Twitter):** a public version of the daily internal screenshot.
- **Super Smash Bros. — Sephiroth reveal:** announced to the team through the Daily Report, which drew so many visits that the server went down.
