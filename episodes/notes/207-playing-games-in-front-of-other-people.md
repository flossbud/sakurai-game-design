# 207 · Playing Games in Front of Other People

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6pnwcuP78g) · [Transcript](../transcripts/207-playing-games-in-front-of-other-people.md)

## In one sentence
When you demo your game, plan your play so it shows as much of the content as possible in close matches, and imagine presenting your work even if you never will, because doing so organizes your thinking.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai plays games in front of people a lot, "perhaps more than any other game director." In each "Mr. Sakurai Presents" DLC announcement for Smash Bros. Ultimate, he presented and played the new character himself.
- He also plays at events. Playing on stage can be hard if the play environment isn't set up properly. At the Japan Game Awards he plays a game he didn't make on stage at the yearly Game Designers Award, and he has done that for over ten years.
- His rules for "Mr. Sakurai Presents" demos: use every one of the character's special moves and its Final Smash, keep matches relatively close, and show as many regular moves as possible. The goal is "as much quality footage as I can."
- You can't demo a game unless you understand its content.
- Showing people a game directly is probably the most effective form of presentation.
- Planning a presentation helps you organize your own thoughts: "The teacher becomes the student, so to speak."
- Even if showing off your work feels unthinkable, imagining that you are presenting it, and picturing how people and customers would react, can reveal new insights.

## Do this
- Before a demo, write a checklist of everything the footage must show (for a fighter: every special move, the super move and as many regular moves as possible) and tick items off as you play.
- Keep demo matches relatively close instead of one-sided.
- Check the on-stage play setup before any live demo, since a badly set-up environment makes playing on stage hard.
- Learn the game's content thoroughly before you demo it.
- Rehearse a presentation of your current work in your head, picture how players and customers react, and note what you would change.

## Avoid
- Demoing a game whose content you don't understand.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — "Mr. Sakurai Presents":** Sakurai played every DLC fighter himself, using all of its special moves and its Final Smash in close matches.
- **Japan Game Awards — Game Designers Award:** he has played a game he didn't make on stage every year for over a decade.
