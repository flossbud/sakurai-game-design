# 191 · Don’t Put Decisions Off

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NaWuNgpYDi4) · [Transcript](../transcripts/191-dont-put-decisions-off.md)

## In one sentence
Make decisions on the spot instead of saying "later", because every minute of waiting leaves the team blocked, but stay flexible enough to correct a call once you see it was wrong.

## Key ideas
- When a meeting can't reach consensus, many managers say, "I'll have to decide on this later!" Sakurai's advice is: please don't.
- Decisions should generally be made on the spot. That may not be possible if the responsible people aren't there, but if someone with the authority is present, push them to decide. If you are that person, decide quickly.
- A director decides the direction of nearly every part of development. Each decision is a fork that sends the team down a long production path, so one wrong choice can waste many hours of work. That's a huge responsibility.
- He understands wanting to be careful, get second opinions and figure things out as you go, but there often isn't time for that hesitation. A quick, firm decision helps more of the team than you might think, and every minute they wait is a minute the issue stays unresolved.
- The rest of the team should help make decisions possible, because the decider needs accurate information. If the resources aren't reliable, the decision won't be either.
- Don't put decisions off, but stay flexible. If you notice a mistake, don't stay the course just because it was decided; leave room to correct it. Decisions set the project's trajectory, so frequent slight adjustments keep it on the right path.
- He tries to decide as soon as a team member comes to him, and researches right away if he needs more information. "Later" is too late.

## Do this
- Make sure someone with the authority to decide is in each decision meeting, and push them to decide before the meeting ends.
- If you're the decider, make the call as soon as a team member brings you an issue. If you're missing information, look it up right away instead of postponing.
- When you bring a question to a decision-maker, bring accurate, reliable information so the decision can be sound.
- When you discover a decision was wrong, correct it with a small adjustment instead of sticking to it because it was already made.

## Avoid
- Ending a meeting with "I'll have to decide on this later."
- Waiting for extra second opinions when the team is blocked and there's no time.
- Holding to a known mistake just because the decision is already made.
- Giving the decider unreliable information.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sakurai's own practice:** he makes a call as soon as a team member comes to him, and does the needed research on the spot.

## Quote
> When it comes to making decisions, it's best to think that "later" is too late! (02:06)
