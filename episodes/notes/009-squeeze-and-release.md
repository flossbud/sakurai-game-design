# 009 · Squeeze and Release

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TYh5SJb5gWk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/009-squeeze-and-release.md)

## In one sentence
Put players under stress with enemies, obstacles or puzzles, then release it, and reward their success with choices that help them handle the next, bigger squeeze.

## Key ideas
- Games stress you out in order to give you relief, and that is "a core element of what makes games fun." Stress alone isn't fun, but heavy pressure followed by sudden release brings great satisfaction.
- A game with no stress might seem more satisfying on paper, but anyone who plays games knows it "probably wouldn't be too much fun." Stress is unpleasant, but "that's no reason to weed it out entirely."
- The stress can be an enemy, an obstacle or a puzzle. All it has to do is get in the way of the player's progress.
- Releasing the stress is only the first step. After a challenge, reward the player with "new ways to more effectively relieve stress": experience points or new skills to face stronger foes, or currency for better equipment.
- Progress is a reward in itself, but "providing the player with choice can really boost that 'game essence.'"
- The flow: apply stress, allow release, prepare players for what's next.
- "Don't just mimic the things other games are doing." Ask why they do them, and your chances of making something fresh go up.

## Do this
- For each section of your game, name its source of stress (enemy, obstacle, puzzle) and its moment of release. If a section has no clear squeeze, add something that gets in the player's way.
- After each cleared challenge, give a reward that improves the player's ability to handle future stress, such as XP toward a new skill or currency for gear.
- Build choice into those rewards (which skill, what to buy) instead of offering only linear progress.
- Before borrowing a mechanic from another game, write down why it works there, and keep it only if that reason applies to your game.

## Avoid
- Removing stress entirely to make the game more comfortable.
- Copying other games' mechanics without understanding why they exist.

## Examples from the episode
- **Generic RPG-style rewards — EXP, new skills, currency for equipment:** rewards that prepare the player for stronger foes.

## Quote
> Stress on its own isn't fun, but if you really squeeze a player, then whoosh! set them free, it can bring great satisfaction. (00:18)
