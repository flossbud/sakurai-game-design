# 155 · Special Crossover! #1

**Category:** Special · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6QH1cQWmXlY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/155-special-crossover-1.md)

## In one sentence
Play the foundational arcade games of 1978–1987 yourself. Many building blocks of modern games started there, and their scoring rules show how to tempt players into taking risks.

## Key ideas
- **The setup:** A crossover with Arino Kacho, host of *Retro Game Master* (first aired 2003, over 300 titles featured). Sakurai's playful objection as a viewer: the show doesn't play arcade games, and "you just can't talk about gaming history without including arcade games in the mix." He planned "The Space Invaders Decade," 25 arcade games from 1978 to 1987 played in a row, to show how much arcade games evolved in a short 10-year span. This part covers 9 of them, through Moon Patrol.
- **A built-in handicap:** Both play the same game at once. Arino can keep playing until game over, while Sakurai fails if he's hit even once, and they move on as soon as either fails. Sakurai says they don't have to frame it "as a competition with one winner and one loser." He didn't practice, and hopes "the merciless nature of these old arcade games will be part of the fun."
- **Motion sickness aside:** The subtitles don't say who is speaking. The guest gets sick driving through buildings in 3D games. The creator of Gran Turismo, asked how not to get sick in VR, reportedly said "Drive better," and there's some truth in it: indoors everything is close and seems to rush past you, so look into the distance at a focal point where things are largely stationary.
- **Greed as design:** Several of the games make the high-scoring play also the risky one:
  - **Galaxian:** flagships are worth more if you destroy their escorts first.
  - **The maze game with fleeing blue ghosts:** more points the more ghosts you eat in one go, so "it's really tempting to get greedy."
  - **The digging game with rocks:** inflating enemies works, but crushing them with rocks is better, and "it's easy to get greedy with the rocks and end up getting hit instead."
  - **Space Invaders:** if you count your shots, there's a way to get 300 points from the UFO.
- **Space Invaders (1978):** Attack "cleanly," one column at a time starting from the ends, and intercept rather than chase. The fewer invaders remain, the faster they move side to side, so if that happens while many columns are left, "they'll be on you in no time."
- **Firsts and building blocks named in the commentary:**
  - **Galaxian:** smooth, analog-feeling movement where earlier games moved digitally, plus color.
  - **Asteroids:** vector scanning, where a single beam like an oscilloscope draws lustrous wireframes, with smooth, remote-control-car-like handling.
  - **Crazy Climber (1980):** two-stick climbing, one of the arcade games that "felt far ahead of their time." Tilt the sticks down to brace yourself when a flower pot is about to hit you.
  - **Scramble:** horizontal scrolling, separate shot and bomb buttons the player chooses between, and a fuel gauge refilled only by destroying "FUEL" tanks. It laid the groundwork for side-scrollers like Gradius. Its bombs fly sideways before dropping, so they land farther away than expected. Backing up moves at the scroll speed, so you can't retreat.
  - **Donkey Kong:** maybe not the first jumping action game, but "certainly the most famous in the genre." Jumping "wasn't much of a thing" in the earlier titles they played.
  - **Moon Patrol:** possibly the first parallax scrolling (distant layers move slower), unusual for heading horizontally toward an actual goal, with smooth tire motion over the terrain. It had a continue screen misspelled "countinue."
- The takeaway voiced at the end: "You can see how all these clever ideas built on top of each other."

## Do this
- Play the foundational games of your genre yourself and note which mechanic each one introduced (scrolling, separate weapon buttons, resource gauges, jumping, parallax).
- Add greed-based scoring: pay escalating points for chaining a risky action (eating several fleeing enemies in one power-up, destroying escorts before the leader, crushing enemies with a dropped object instead of the safe attack), so the player chooses how much risk to take.
- Use a draining resource that forces engagement, like a fuel gauge refilled only by destroying fuel targets, so players can't hang back and play safe.
- Let danger rise as a result of the player's own progress (Space Invaders' survivors speed up as their numbers drop), so the order in which players clear threats becomes a strategy.
- For play between people of very different skill, use asymmetric fail conditions (one player plays to game over, the other loses on the first hit) and present it as shared fun rather than a winner-and-loser contest.

## Examples from the episode
- **Space Invaders:** clear one column at a time from the ends, because survivors speed up. Counting shots sets up 300 points from the UFO.
- **Galaxian:** smoother, analog-feeling movement and color. Flagships score more if escorts go first.
- **Asteroids:** vector-scan wireframe graphics, smooth controls, a warp button.
- **The ghost-eating maze game** (not named in the subtitles): escalating points for eating fleeing blue ghosts in one go, plus a cherry bonus.
- **Crazy Climber:** two-stick climbing, windows that slam on your fingers, bracing against flower pots.
- **Scramble:** shot and bomb buttons, a fuel gauge, bombs that land farther than expected, no retreating.
- **Donkey Kong:** jumping and the hammer. Barrels won't come down a ladder while your hand is on the girder, but trust that too much and you get clipped. One stage wasn't in the NES version.
- **The rock-dropping digging game** (not named in the subtitles): crushing enemies with rocks beats inflating them, but greed gets you hit.
- **Moon Patrol:** parallax scrolling, holding right to accelerate, balancing forward and backward speed to dodge UFOs, a six-wheeled buggy, and the misspelled "countinue."
