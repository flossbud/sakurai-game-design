# 148 · More Game Essence Means Less Broad Appeal

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ML5wdEWpTuA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/148-more-game-essence-means-less-broad-appeal.md)

## In one sentence
Pick your target audience first, then set how much risk your rules carry. More risk means more thrill but less broad appeal, so a beginner-focused game should deliberately hold back.

## Key ideas
- Game essence and risk/reward make a game fun and exciting, but too much game essence can reduce broad appeal. You can always add rules to boost push and pull, but overcomplicated rulesets are hard to grasp. "Sometimes, it's important to hold back," depending on the game's aims.
- **Super Mario Bros.:** Built on NES-era standards, and some parts strike Sakurai as a bit too difficult. Its heart is getting right next to enemies and turning that extreme risk into a counterattack, so risk and reward sit very close together.
- **Kirby's Dream Land:** When an enemy approaches, the risk is still relatively low, and Kirby can inhale it before the risk gets very high. Kirby can take up to six hits. Spitting the enemy out is an attack with even greater range than the inhale. Kirby can fly whenever you want, which helps avoid the steep penalty for falling into a pit.
- **The thought experiment:** Shrink Kirby's inhale range, make him lose a life after one hit, remove flight, or add more inertia to the controls. That would likely raise game essence and could give a good amount of push and pull, depending on balance. But it would lower broad appeal.
- **Risk and reward scale together:** Balanced correctly, they "scale in almost perfect proportion." A more intense risk brings a more intense joy when you overcome it, and low risk brings low reward. So the low risk of the beginner-focused Dream Land has a downside: it is less fun. That doesn't mean it doesn't work as a game.
- **Difficulty is perceived differently:** A minor risk to an experienced player can be "a massive brick wall" to someone less experienced. "We should be kind to beginners." Dream Land was built around that idea, so even if it is less thrilling than Mario, it "accomplishes exactly what it set out to do." Some "super casual" players who only play simple mobile games may find even Dream Land too hard, and Sakurai suspects that audience is an extremely large business opportunity.
- Be aware of your target audience, and let it follow from your game's design and concept. There's no one right answer: understand how game essence arises, then use that to hone in on a target.

## Do this
- Write down your target audience before tuning, and set the risk level of your core rules to match it.
- List your game's risk dials (threat-neutralizing range, number of hits the player can take, escape tools such as unlimited flight, control inertia) and adjust each deliberately: tighten them for more thrill and expert appeal, loosen them for beginners.
- Accept that lowering risk lowers reward: a low-risk, beginner-friendly design will feel less intense, so judge it by whether it serves its intended audience, not by thrill alone.
- Before adding another rule to increase push and pull, check whether it makes the ruleset harder to grasp for your target players, and leave it out if so.
- Judge difficulty from a less experienced player's point of view, not your own. A risk that's minor to you can be a brick wall to them.

## Avoid
- Piling on rules to boost game essence until the ruleset is hard to grasp.
- Assuming everyone perceives difficulty the way you do.
- Writing off a low-risk beginner game as a failure because it's less thrilling. It may be doing exactly what it set out to do.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Mario Bros.:** getting right next to enemies and turning extreme risk into a counterattack. Some NES-era parts are a bit too difficult.
- **Kirby's Dream Land:** inhale neutralizes enemies before the risk gets high, six hits of health, a spit attack with more range than the inhale, and flight at any time to avoid pits.
- **Hypothetical harder Kirby:** smaller inhale, one-hit death, no flight, more inertia. More game essence, less broad appeal.
- **"Super casual" mobile players:** may find even Dream Land too difficult, and are a potentially huge audience.

## Quote
> Risk and reward, when balanced correctly, should scale in almost perfect proportion. (02:00)
