# 165 · Odds and Ends of Supervising

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wKQ2v9kno9I) · [Transcript](../transcripts/165-odds-and-ends-of-supervising.md)

## In one sentence
Supervise by showing, not telling: give concrete visual feedback (retouched images, diagrams, side-by-sides with the source) on everything players will see, with special attention to readability, collision matching animation, and staying true to the original's feel.

## Key ideas
- Since remote work began, Sakurai's supervision has been mostly over email, so text-based, but "showing tends to communicate more clearly than telling", so he often retouches or draws art himself. Every issue shown here was fixed for release, and it's "just a tiny part" of his feedback (many images couldn't be shown because they contain debug UI). He supervises banners, commercials and streamed videos too.
- Readability comes first: Steve's block had to stay visible through the Castlevania stage's vignetting. The Minecraft stage at night was brightened, with more light around the torch and less in the surroundings, like the original game. Stage cracks needed fixing for contrast, and display priority had to keep the fighter in front. The P8 Enderman costume was so hard to see he pitched it as a game of "find the Enderman". Fighters in Mishima Dojo's shadows were hard to see, which a desaturated image showed more clearly. The dojo's battlefield platforms were hard to make out, and the Pneuma costume was too dark.
- Collision and animation must agree. A common issue is "attacks that look like they should hit, but don't", and the fix touches both collision and animation. Steve's grab arc couldn't catch nearby opponents, so its parabola was pulled closer. Pyra's forward aerial felt like it kept hitting after the collision ended, so the collision time was shortened. Mythra's slash had to line up with a diagonal "otherwise, the hit itself just won't feel right". Sacred Arrow's blade pointed straight sideways "would go straight through opponents", so she raises it slightly.
- Animation needs weight and impact. Pyra's walk shifts her weight forward and improves her silhouette. When Kazuya landed, his wings retracted in just two frames, so frames were added for impact. The wings should catch and release wind with more force and flutter harder where the punch couldn't be seen. Devil Kazuya keeps a bat-like silhouette with more energy, and a too-rounded wing silhouette was sharpened. A fighter hit by a Final Smash should enter his damage animation immediately.
- Faces and fine detail: Pyra's smile should turn up at the corners instead of being a simple arc. Kazuya's eyes and eyebrows were pulled up to look angrier, and his eye glint in the reveal made stronger and sharper. Mythra's low-poly blade tip was touched up, and Foresight leaves an afterimage at the dodge's center point.
- Honor the source without copying it: "I don't want us to just copy the originals, but honor the feeling and do what works best." Kazuya's knocked-down pose follows his original game, and his Final Smash was brought closer to it ("it's not always best to match things directly"). CG movie shots were pushed toward FFVII: Advent Children, starting by lowering saturation. The Doom Slayer Mii costume's palette was compared against the original art, and reference screens were taken to recreate the original gameplay in reveals.
- Scrutinize the whole environment for plausibility: "It's important to take a top-down look and scrutinize everything." The Cloud Sea's clouds moved unnaturally and the stage rocked too much. Its windmills once turned against the wind. Dojo ceiling debris lacked presence and was too bunched together. Sakurai also asked for moon shafts through the broken ceiling, moonlit ridge lines, mountain detail adjusted from the bottom up, and more detail on the sides where the background shows.

## Do this
- Give art feedback as a retouched screenshot or quick paintover next to the original, with one line on what to change and why, instead of a text-only description.
- Check readability by desaturating a screenshot. If characters in shadow, platforms or costumes disappear, raise their contrast or relight the scene (more light near light sources, less in the surroundings).
- Whenever an attack looks like it should connect but doesn't, or keeps hitting after the swing ends, fix both sides: adjust the hitbox shape and timing and the animation's arc or pose until they agree.
- Hunt for motions too fast to register (wings retracting in 2 frames on landing) and add frames so they land with impact. Make damage reactions start immediately.
- When adapting a character from another game, put the original's screenshots or art next to yours and match its feeling (palette, saturation, signature poses) instead of copying it literally.
- Do a "top-down" sweep of every stage for physical plausibility: windmills turning with the wind, debris spread naturally, light shafts where the ceiling is broken, motion that isn't excessive.

## Avoid
- Text-only feedback on visual problems that one image would make obvious.
- Hit visuals and collision that disagree, in either direction.
- Letting characters or key objects blend into dark, shadowed or vignetted backgrounds.
- Copying source material literally instead of honoring its feel.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Ultimate — Steve (Minecraft):** grab parabola brought closer, block kept visible in vignetting, night stage lit around the torch like the original.
- **Smash Ultimate — Pyra/Mythra:** forward walk weight, shortened forward-aerial collision, the hardest blade trail (flames combined with the Photon Saber), upturned smile, Sacred Arrow blade raised, Cloud Sea windmills fixed.
- **Smash Ultimate — Kazuya:** angrier brows, more frames on the wing retraction, sharper wing silhouette, Mishima Dojo readability check with a desaturated image.
- **Smash Ultimate — Sephiroth / FFVII:** sharper sword contrast in his artwork, camera-angle sketches for the Highwind's escape from the Northern Cave, CG movie shots pushed toward FFVII: Advent Children by lowering saturation.
- **Event banners:** cleaner edges and rim lighting, a wider field of view, tears that look "rough and ripped", radial action lines.
