# 157 · Special Crossover! #3

**Category:** Special · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zfh0oq-_nJQ) · [Transcript](../transcripts/157-special-crossover-3.md)

## In one sentence
Borrow the tools classic arcade games used to welcome and balance players (a no-penalty "trial time", difficulty and resource settings that work as handicaps, clear attack tells), and check that your co-op mode doesn't make two players worse off than one.

## Key ideas
- One host remembers a PS2-era Tokyo Game Show demo called Densen, where you used a wire hanger to hook onto power lines and zoom around. It never came out. Sakurai explains that many games shown at trade shows were still mid-development, so cancellations were "relatively commonplace".
- Space Harrier (1985) felt far ahead of its time: its graphics, its feeling of speed and its moving cabinet. Sakurai turns on its "trial time" for Arino: for a set period you can die as much as you want. It was a real feature of the game, and many arcades kept it on for their customers.
- Handicaps from settings: The Genji and the Heike Clans had no lives setting, so Sakurai gave himself the hardest difficulty and the fewest candles (set in place of lives) and gave Arino the most candles and the easiest difficulty. That evened out a match between unequal players.
- Co-op can backfire. In two-player Darius the ships get in each other's way and there are only enough power-ups for one ship, "which actually makes it harder". In arcade Contra, the two-player scroll point sits far forward on the screen, which makes it tough. Sakurai also found analog sticks made precise ship control harder than a standard eight-direction controller, and he kept drifting diagonally by accident.
- Readable threats and risk-reward: in Punch-Out!!, an opponent's eyes turning yellow tells you it's time to dodge. Space Harrier's dragon only takes damage to the head, and Darius's boss only when its mouth is open. In Darius, if you hang back on the left you run out of shots, and getting close "lets me fire more shots without waiting". One player complains they can't hear the game audio, so they have "no idea when the enemies are firing".
- Power and execution over time: in the original Street Fighter (1987), one hadouken took 40% of your health but was hard to perform. Sakurai's first thought on playing SFII was "Wow, they made the hadouken weak!" Ninja Gaiden's final boss Jaquio has three forms, which was punishing and, Sakurai says, what made it fun.
- Art and hardware: Punch-Out!!'s see-through wireframe hero was reportedly drawn on graph paper by Shigeru Miyamoto, and its large, well-drawn boxers scaling up and down as they attack had real impact. The NES couldn't do the see-through effect, so the hero was made tiny instead. The first Kunio-kun used realistic proportions, unlike the cartoony later games. The series covered 25 games, and the hosts note that once color arrived, games changed "at a breakneck pace".

## Do this
- Offer newcomers a "trial time": an opening window or optional mode where deaths cost nothing, so first-timers can learn before the normal rules apply.
- Expose difficulty and resources (lives, health, enemy aggression) as separate settings per player, so a strong player can handicap themselves in the same match as a weaker one.
- Playtest co-op specifically for "the second player makes it harder" problems: power-ups budgeted for one player, players blocking each other, and a camera or scroll point that leaves the trailing player behind.
- Give each dangerous enemy attack a visual tell that players can react to, like Punch-Out!!'s yellow eyes, and don't rely on audio alone to signal incoming fire.
- Reward aggression mechanically. For example, limit shots on screen so players who move in close can fire again sooner (Darius).

## Avoid
- Splitting single-player resources or camera space between two players without retuning them (Darius power-ups, Contra's scroll point).
- Signaling enemy attacks only through sound.
- Assuming an analog stick gives the precision an eight-direction game was tuned for.

## Examples from the episode
- **Space Harrier — "trial time":** a period of free deaths that many arcades left switched on. The dragon can only be damaged in the head.
- **The Genji and the Heike Clans — handicap:** hardest difficulty with the fewest candles for Sakurai, easiest with the most for Arino. The game also has jokey touches, like a top that Sakurai says is a reminder to "expect the unexpected".
- **Darius — doubleplay and co-op:** three screens combined into one. Sakurai controls both ships himself. Two-player is harder because of shared power-ups and the ships getting in each other's way.
- **Contra (arcade) — two-player scroll:** the scroll point sits far forward, and it's a horizontal scroller on a vertical screen.
- **Street Fighter (1987) — hadouken:** 40% of your health per hit and hard to input. SFII made it weak.
- **Punch-Out!! — tells and scaling:** yellow eyes mean dodge, a see-through wireframe hero, large scaling characters. The NES version shrank the hero instead.
- **Fantasy Zone — shop system:** a side-scrolling shooter set apart by its shop, with beautiful graphics and cheerful sound. Sakurai recommends buying Big Wings (speed) and Twin Bombs.
