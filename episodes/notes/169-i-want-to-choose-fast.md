# 169 · I Want to Choose Fast!

**Category:** UI · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-_3ZnSiwlnc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/169-i-want-to-choose-fast.md)

## In one sentence
Make choosing fast: question the input devices and menu conventions everyone takes for granted, and treat every new device as a chance to speed up selection, without burying beginners under expert-only shortcuts.

## Key ideas
- Nintendo has sometimes asked Sakurai for suggestions on new hardware features. Since the GameCube and Smash Bros. Melee, he has repeatedly suggested a scroll wheel (then called a jog dial) on the controller.
- His reasoning: most mice now have one, and anyone who has used a web browser knows how useful it is. Of all the inputs in games, menu selection is the one he most wants to be quick, and a wheel would let him "zip right to my choice and be done in a flash." It would also help anywhere you pick one thing from a group.
- He wanted the wheel to feel "clicky", because games are about play and that tactile feedback makes gameplay more immersive. He understands why it was passed on, though: scroll wheels are bigger than you'd think.
- Today's menus often mean tapping the analog stick over and over, which he isn't satisfied with. He prefers the D-pad, which "feels nice and clicky".
- The real point is to re-examine what we take for granted. Whenever new devices are adopted, he thinks about how to use them for UI, and he still wants logical improvements to how controller menu navigation feels.
- The trade-off: the more advanced the shortcut options, the less inexperienced players use them, and the UI can lose broad appeal, "so it's a tough line to walk." Still, new devices are new opportunities.

## Do this
- Count the inputs it takes to reach your most common menu choices, and cut steps for the most frequent ones.
- Support D-pad navigation in menus with crisp one-step movement, instead of relying on repeated analog-stick taps.
- Give each step of menu movement a distinct, "clicky" feel so scrolling feels tactile.
- When your platform has an unusual input (a wheel, touch, gyro), prototype using it to jump through lists faster.
- Layer shortcuts on top of a simple path beginners can follow, so speed for experts doesn't cost broad appeal.

## Avoid
- Menus that make players tap an analog stick repeatedly to reach an option.
- Shortcut systems so advanced that only experts use them, leaving a UI with narrow appeal.

## Examples from the episode
- **Nintendo GameCube / Super Smash Bros. Melee — scroll wheel proposal:** Sakurai's repeated suggestion of a clicky jog dial for fast menu selection, which was understandably passed on because of its size.
- **Analog stick vs. D-pad in menus:** stick taps feel unsatisfying, while the D-pad feels clicky and better.
