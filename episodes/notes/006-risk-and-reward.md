# 006 · Risk and Reward

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXqEykD5Ub4) · [Transcript](../transcripts/006-risk-and-reward.md)

## In one sentence
Build your core fun from risk and reward placed close together in the right amounts, so the moment of greatest danger is also the moment the player can win something.

## Key ideas
- "Game essence" is a game's "push and pull," or "risk and reward": take risks to get rewards. Strategy is the same thing seen from the player's side. Game essence is "the fun of managing risks and getting rewards" (the mechanics), and strategy is "how you manage those risks to get rewards" (the player).
- In Space Invaders, the cannon and the invaders only shoot straight up and down. Far apart, nobody can hit anybody ("no risk, no reward"). As the cannon moves under an invader, the risk of being hit rises and the reward comes within reach.
- Because invaders move sideways, their real attack range is diagonal. Chasing an invader from behind puts you in danger with no shot. Intercepting from the front carries the same risk with the target in your sights. "Intercept invaders, not pursue them."
- The cannon's width "is key to game balance because it determines how great your risk is." It's more than visual design, and a different size could change how deep the game is. Mechanics that change your risk, like hiding halfway under a bunker, matter just as much. A three-way laser "might look cool" but probably wouldn't be good design, unless enemies also shot diagonally or ammo were limited.
- The "Wall of Death" (or "Nagoya Attack"): in the final moments, with invaders down near the bottom, one mistake means Game Over no matter how many lives you have, but their beams start below the cannon's hitbox, so the cannon can't be hit. "Ultimate risk, ultimate reward." It's probably a bug, but a brilliant one.
- "You can't be a chef if you can't discern taste." Developers need to be able to sniff out what makes games fun. In Super Mario Bros., risk rises as Mario nears a Koopa Troopa. The jump that dodges it is also the chance to stomp it, so "When we're at the highest possible risk— that's the only time we can beat the Koopa Troopa." Harsh misses and tricky midair control add to the thrill. Kicking the shell earns points or a 1-Up, but the shell can bounce back and hurt Mario.
- Game essence is only one part of a game's identity. His theory: "As you increase 'game essence,' you reduce a game's broad appeal." He made the first Kirby deliberately simpler to reach a wider audience.

## Do this
- For each core interaction, map how risk and reward change with distance and position. Make sure the spot where the player can score is also a spot where they're exposed.
- Treat the player's hitbox size as a balance setting, not just art. Tune it to control how risky it is to get close.
- Add ways for players to adjust their own risk, such as partial cover, so strategy has room to emerge.
- Before adding a strong option (spread shots, auto-aim), check whether it removes the risk. If you keep it, counterweight it, for example with enemies that cover the new angles or limited ammo.
- Attach new risk to rewards. A kicked shell that pays out points but can bounce back is the model.
- Decide how much push and pull your audience wants. Dial it down for broad audiences, as with the first Kirby.

## Avoid
- Strong options that remove risk with no counterweight, like a three-way laser in Space Invaders.
- Treating character or hitbox size as purely visual.
- Assuming more game essence is always better. It narrows a game's appeal.

## Examples from the episode
- **Space Invaders — closing in on an invader:** risk and reward rise together as you approach.
- **Space Invaders — diagonal attack range:** pursuing is danger for nothing; intercepting is the best strategy.
- **Space Invaders — cannon width and bunkers:** the cannon's size and partial cover set how much risk you take.
- **Space Invaders — Wall of Death / Nagoya Attack:** beams start below the cannon's hitbox, making it invincible at the moment of highest stakes.
- **Super Mario Bros. — stomping a Koopa Troopa:** the only chance to win comes at peak risk; the kicked shell is a reward that carries risk.
- **Kirby's Dream Land:** made deliberately simpler to appeal to a wider audience.

> Risk and reward must be in close proximity, and in the right amounts. (08:05)
