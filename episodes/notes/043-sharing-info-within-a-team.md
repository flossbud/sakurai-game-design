# 043 · Sharing Info Within a Team

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40ryMOr36fw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/043-sharing-info-within-a-team.md)

## In one sentence
Keep every team member connected to the whole game with regular shared information (a daily report, a daily screenshot and periodic team presentations) so nobody feels like a cog.

## Key ideas
- This episode opens the Team Management category.
- On large games individuals lose sight of what they're making, like someone making one screw without knowing it becomes a car. Working without the larger picture is stressful, so Sakurai tries to remove that stress by encouraging the team to share information.
- Daily Report: a page where he posts progress, news and anything else that comes to mind, with a comment field so the team can talk to each other. Some posts are just for fun, others cover development and his thoughts on the project, almost like a development newspaper.
- Screenshot of the Day: one development screenshot posted daily to the Daily Report. It became the daily screens he posted on Twitter, but internally it includes work in progress unfit for the public. The team enjoyed writing captions in the comments, a nice way to unwind.
- Project Presentations: every few months each team (graphics, sound, animation, visual effects, UI and more) presents its work. Aligning schedules and finding a venue is a hassle, but it's worth it: everyone gets a broader view of the project instead of feeling like a cog, and each member's work will eventually be seen by many people.
- Better information flow keeps the team from getting stuck in a rut. Creatives have to keep producing, so give them motivation and a sense of accomplishment along the way; on a team, no one should work alone.

## Do this
- Start a daily report page where the lead posts progress, news and thoughts on the project, with a comment field everyone on the team can use.
- Post one in-development screenshot to that page every day, including work in progress not fit for the public, and invite the team to caption or comment on it.
- Every few months, have each discipline (graphics, sound, animation, VFX, UI, etc.) present its work to the whole team.
- Block out the time and book the venue for those presentations even though they're a hassle to organize.

## Avoid
- Leaving people to work without a view of the larger project, like cogs in a machine.
- Skipping team presentations because scheduling and venues are a hassle.

## Examples from the episode
- **Making a single screw for a car:** the worker can't picture what it becomes, which is how team members on large games can feel.
- **Screenshot of the Day:** the internal routine that he repurposed for his daily screens on Twitter.

## Quote
> When you're part of a team, no one should be working alone. (03:02)
