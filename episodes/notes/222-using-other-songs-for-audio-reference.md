# 222 · Using Other Songs for Audio Reference

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=heFZ6qQXfDw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/222-using-other-songs-for-audio-reference.md)

## In one sentence
Give your composer a concrete existing song as a starting point, but don't let you or them get fixated on it, because a new piece has to do more than imitate the reference.

## Key ideas
- A concrete example is very useful when a composer wants to know where to start. Pointing to an existing piece that fits the game is a fast way to communicate a direction.
- First danger: the director gets fixated on one piece and closes their mind to other options. Then everything sounds like a cheap imitation.
- Second danger: the composer gets fixated. It is hard to make something similar yet different, and composers are often unsure which parts of the reference to keep and which to change. Sakurai calls this a surprisingly common way to lose sight of the goal.
- A reference can be about a feel, not its most famous element. God Hand's "Gene's Rock-a-Bye" was requested in the style of The Ventures. The band's well-known "tika-tika" guitar riff wasn't what the director had in mind and was left out, yet the track still captures the surfer sound.
- Another option is to license the existing song as-is when it fits perfectly. Ore no Shikabane wo Koete Yuke uses the song "Hana": the game is about a family cursed with a two-year lifespan who pass their hopes to later generations, so a song about flowers blooming and wilting fits. The game's designer, Mr. Masuda, negotiated the rights personally.
- Working from a reference can save work or create more of it, in music and elsewhere. Either way, stay flexible.

## Do this
- When briefing a composer, attach one concrete reference track and write down which quality you want from it (e.g., "the surfer feel") and which signature elements not to copy (e.g., "not the famous riff").
- Before you lock in a reference, list two or three other musical directions, so the reference doesn't become the only thing you can imagine.
- Judge drafts by whether they serve the scene, not by how closely they match the reference.
- If an existing song fits the game's theme exactly, consider negotiating the rights to use it as-is instead of commissioning a soundalike.

## Avoid
- Fixating on one reference piece until every draft sounds like a cheap imitation of it.
- Handing over a reference with only "make it like this", which leaves the composer guessing what to keep and what to change.

## Examples from the episode
- **God Hand — "Gene's Rock-a-Bye" (first stage):** composer Mr. Takada was asked for the style of The Ventures. The iconic guitar riff was left out, but the track still has the surfer sound.
- **Ore no Shikabane wo Koete Yuke — "Hana":** an existing song licensed as-is because its theme of blooming and wilting flowers matches a family with a two-year lifespan. Designer Mr. Masuda negotiated the rights himself.

## Quote
> Either way, a new creation should do more than just recreate what someone else has already made, so try to stay flexible however necessary. (02:32)
