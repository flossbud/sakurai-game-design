# 144 · Keep Your Internal Pressure High

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UVnLW47cpFk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/144-keep-your-internal-pressure-high.md)

## In one sentence
While you're writing a proposal, don't talk about it or post about it. Hold the "internal pressure" in, pour it into the document, and consult others only once it's finished.

## Key ideas
- When you're creating something from scratch, like a proposal, "keep your lips sealed until it's finished." Resist the urge to share and keep writing down what's inside you.
- **Internal pressure:** Think of the air that inflates a balloon, or a pressure cooker: pressure that tries to escape. A proposal must be inflated inside your mind. The point is not to make it bigger but to increase its power and make the content stronger.
- Just talking about ideas can make you feel fulfilled, and that releases the pressure, so the ideas lose their power. Keep ramping it up instead, and make the content "denser and richer."
- Consulting others, soliciting ideas, finding weak points together and group brainstorming all have their place. But a proposal needs force: it must show how much you want it. Sakurai says a design document written without sharing a word with anyone "will burn a good bit hotter" than one compiled from a brainstorming session.
- So consult after the proposal is finished. "First, you should face inward to really hone ideas on your own."
- The same applies to social media. Posting ideas or progress vents the pressure.
- Picture how people will react when you pitch the proposal. During development, imagining players' reactions and the parts they might love gives you the strength to push on.

## Do this
- When you start a proposal or design document, don't discuss it with anyone or post about it until the first complete draft is written.
- Whenever you feel the urge to tell someone about the idea, write it into the document instead.
- After the draft is finished, show it to colleagues to find weak points and gather new ideas.
- Hold off on posting work-in-progress ideas or progress on social media while a project is taking shape.
- While writing and later while developing, picture specific reactions (how the people you'll pitch to, and later players, will respond, and what they'll love) and use that to keep going.

## Avoid
- Talking through an unfinished proposal. The sense of fulfillment drains its force.
- Using a compiled brainstorm in place of a proposal you've built up under your own pressure.
- Venting ideas or progress on social media mid-creation.

## Examples from the episode
- **Metaphor — balloon:** the air pushing outward to inflate it is the internal pressure.
- **Metaphor — pressure cooker:** pressure that tries to escape, which you hold in.
