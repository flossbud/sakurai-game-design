# 171 · The Power of Suggestion

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QI39AHhZNcY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/171-the-power-of-suggestion.md)

## In one sentence
Motivate yourself by imagining something closing in on you. Treat a deadline as a pursuer to outrun rather than a goal you'll reach eventually, without actually leaving the work until the last day.

## Key ideas
- In middle school Sakurai came last in foot races, until one day he won. At the starting line he imagined a horrifying machine over three meters tall, with rotating blades and hammers, bearing down on him and shaking the ground with every step, close enough "to take my legs off". He ran faster than the wind and broke the tape. "In conclusion: running away is always faster."
- He admits it sounds ridiculous, but "you can't underestimate the power of suggestion."
- Frame of mind matters in anything you do. Instead of setting a target to head toward, give yourself something to run from. A deadline or other endpoint closing in can be surprisingly effective.
- At work, instead of thinking you can finish whenever, tell yourself you only have a few hours left and you'll really get things done. That doesn't mean waiting for the last day of summer break to do your homework.
- When he was losing races, he was running "as if I were chasing my own shadow", and that wasn't good enough. How well this works depends on the person and situation.

## Do this
- Before starting a task, give yourself a short, self-imposed time limit ("I have only a few hours left for this") and work as if it's real.
- Frame upcoming milestones as something approaching that you need to get ahead of, not a finish line you'll get to eventually.
- Set the imagined deadline well ahead of the real one, so the pressure produces focus rather than last-minute cramming.

## Avoid
- Treating work as something you can finish "whenever".
- Waiting until the real last day (like summer homework) to start.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sakurai's middle-school foot races:** imagining a three-meter machine chasing him took him from last place to first.

## Quote
> instead of setting a target or a destination you're heading toward, give yourself something to run from. (01:14)
