# 002 · Masahiro Sakurai's Creative Works

**Category:** Special · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=frNXwWqZpkI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/002-masahiro-sakurais-creative-works.md)

## In one sentence
Be able to sum up each game in one line that says who it is for or what its hook is, the way every one of Sakurai's works is captioned here.

## Key ideas
- The episode is a montage of title cards for his works with one caption each. It ends with "THE END (Next Game TBD)". The subtitles name only a few titles; the rest appear on screen.
- Kirby games: "A game that was tailor-made for newcomers to introduce them to gaming" (Kirby's Dream Land, per ep. 4). "A fun romp for beginners and pros alike that introduced the power to copy enemy abilities" (Kirby's Adventure, per ep. 11). "A puffed-up collection of 7+ game modes, each with its own unique story and rules" (Kirby Super Star, per ep. 20).
- Smash Bros.:
  - The first is "A classic for 4-player competitive play. Rack up damage, then smash foes off the screen!"
  - Its sequel has "more than double the characters and stages."
  - Brawl was "The first Smash Bros. to support online play" and "sold over 12 million units worldwide."
  - A portable Smash where "No corners were cut" was developed alongside the Wii U version.
  - "An even bigger Smash Bros." added 8-player battles, amiibo support, and DLC characters and costumes.
  - The latest brings back "The entire series roster."
- Other works:
  - A Weekly Famitsu column, the magazine's longest-running by a game developer, published for nearly 20 years.
  - A "battle-centric racer" where you "Press A to play," released around the same time as a Kirby anime.
  - "An interstellar puzzle game where you eliminate blocks by launching them into space" (his block-puzzle game Meteos, per ep. 7).
  - Orchestral concerts of game music from Japan and abroad.
  - LCD toys for raising virtual beetles and battling them over infrared.
  - "A handheld shooter that combines land and air battles," with online play.
- A pattern in the captions (an inference from the format, not something he states): each work is pitched in one line naming either its audience ("newcomers," "beginners and pros alike") or its signature mechanic ("copy enemy abilities," "smash foes off the screen").

## Do this
- Write a one-line pitch for your game that names its audience or its core hook, and check that the hook could fit in a caption.
- Lead the pitch with the audience when accessibility is the point ("tailor-made for newcomers"), and with the mechanic when the hook is new ("introduced the power to copy enemy abilities").

## Examples from the episode
- **Kirby's Dream Land — target audience:** captioned as "tailor-made for newcomers."
- **Kirby's Adventure — copy ability:** captioned as being for "beginners and pros alike."
- **Super Smash Bros. — core rule:** "Rack up damage, then smash foes off the screen!"
- **Super Smash Bros. Brawl — online play:** the first Smash with online play, over 12 million units sold.
