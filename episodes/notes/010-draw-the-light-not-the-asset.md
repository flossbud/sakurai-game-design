# 010 · Draw the Light, Not the Asset

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FuAtKjEuck8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/010-draw-the-light-not-the-asset.md)

## In one sentence
Don't call an asset finished once it's modeled and textured; finish it by showing the light it reflects in its environment, using cheap techniques like colored light, hue variation and atmosphere.

## Key ideas
- His most common comment to artists, especially background artists: "Don't draw the asset, draw the light it reflects."
- Everything we see is light reflected off objects into our eyes. "It's not as simple as seeing a red thing as red or a blue thing as blue."
- For a 3D tree you'd texture the trunk brown and the leaves green, but you "shouldn't stop there." A powerful machine might add detail with shaders or post-processing, but you rarely have that luxury. "The less we process an object, the more of it we can put on screen," so simpler techniques are better when possible.
- A forest of the same tree cut and pasted many times doesn't look great, though it's usable. He improves it in three steps:
  - Colored light shining down creates contrast within the group, and a spray brightens the spots that should get more light.
  - Slight variations in hue give the piece more impact, because light scatters as it reflects.
  - Atmosphere adds depth and distance. It's "a bit more complicated than just adding fog for aerial perspective."
- A brightness filter in the direction of sunlight also helps, though that goes beyond asset creation.
- Art duties may be split across several people, but "one way or another, avoid just drawing the asset and calling it finished." The final product should express the light the asset reflects in its environment.

## Do this
- When reviewing a background, check whether you can see where the light comes from and what color it is. Flat, evenly colored objects mean the work isn't done.
- Add colored light falling across groups of repeated assets to create contrast, and brighten the spots that should catch more light.
- Vary the hue slightly across copies of a repeated asset, such as trees in a forest, instead of using identical colors.
- Add atmosphere for depth, treating aerial perspective more carefully than a flat fog layer.
- Prefer cheap techniques over heavy per-object shaders or post-processing, so you can fit more objects on screen.
- Make lighting part of an asset's definition of done, even when modeling and lighting are handled by different people.

## Avoid
- Calling an asset finished with only its base colors (brown trunk, green leaves).
- Copy-pasting identical assets with no variation in lighting or color.
- Relying on expensive shaders or post-processing when the hardware budget is tight.
