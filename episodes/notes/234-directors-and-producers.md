# 234 · Directors and Producers

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=isEMP5_Eems) · [Transcript](../transcripts/234-directors-and-producers.md)

## In one sentence
Give a project one director who owns the content and concept, and a producer who owns money, staffing and marketing, and make sure both are working toward the same quality goal.

## Key ideas
- People outside the industry confuse the two roles, and Sakurai has been called a producer, though he is a director. Whether a company says "director" or "kantoku" (the Japanese word for a film director) comes down to company culture. Boundaries vary by company and project, so he describes the general split.
- The director makes the final call on almost all of the game's content and is usually responsible for its concept. Just as a film director can be separate from the script, a game designer sometimes produces the concept, but that's hard without a grasp of the game's core appeal, so it's probably best if the director comes up with the ideas.
- A team usually has one director, because the role is about setting the game's direction and you don't want split opinions or unclear goals. If you divide the role, be extremely clear about who has the final say.
- Producers manage the team and handle marketing. They work with the company on financing, management and staffing; the director is one of the roles a producer assigns. They weigh development costs against projected sales to make the project as cost-effective as possible, and they handle marketing because a good game doesn't matter if no one knows about it.
- Producers are often the ones interviewed because directors are very busy and essential to the team, so the producer handles publicity for them.
- The split isn't strict. Some producers are prolific sources of ideas, and some directors do marketing. Sakurai often takes on a producer's role, since fighter reveal movies are also marketing.
- Directors usually work on one project at a time; producers often oversee several. Directors are responsible for one game's quality, while producers watch all the company's projects. The two can clash, but Sakurai says Nintendo trusts him so much there is almost never tension. Both want a quality product.

## Do this
- Name a single director with the final say on content. If you split the role, write down exactly who decides what.
- Have the director own the concept, since shaping it is hard without a grasp of the game's core appeal.
- Give a producer the budget (development cost versus projected sales), staffing and publicity, so the director can focus on the game.
- Agree early that both roles share the quality goal, and build enough trust that differences of opinion don't turn into standoffs.

## Avoid
- Several directors with no clear final say, which leads to split opinions and unclear goals.
- Handing the concept to someone who doesn't grasp the game's core appeal.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Bros. — fighter reveal movies:** Sakurai, as director, effectively does a producer's marketing work.
- **Sakurai and Nintendo:** Nintendo trusts him so much that differences of opinion rarely cause tension.
