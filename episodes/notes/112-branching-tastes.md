# 112 · Branching Tastes

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=50Khnf80P7M) · [Transcript](../transcripts/112-branching-tastes.md)

## In one sentence
Choose your target audience from a top-down view of the market rather than your own tastes, remembering that tastes branch apart with age, so the broadest market is the "trunk" of younger players while a niche "branch" can still breed devoted fans.

## Key ideas
- "People who play games" covers very different habits: buying physical console games, playing only one social game, location-based games played while walking, competitive shooters night after night, VR, fitness games, an MMORPG with friends almost every day, only indie games on PC. All fans of games, with completely separate styles. In the NES days, by comparison, everyone tended to play the same kinds of games.
- Nintendo's top sellers sell enormous numbers worldwide and top Japan's charts. Quality is part of it, but they don't use cutting-edge hardware or tech, and every modern console is capable. A big reason, Sakurai says, is that they're hits with younger generations.
- **The tree metaphor:** as we age, experiences, cultures and the people we meet split our values into narrower and narrower branches, but the trunk, where young people are, is broad. In a world of diverse tastes, the market is broadest before everyone finds their own branch. If the things we loved when young have era-appropriate successors, that enthusiasm passes to the next generation.
- The real point: when choosing which of these many viewpoints to target, don't rely on your own likes and dislikes. Take a top-down view of the market.
- Broad-appeal games, orthodox games and ongoing series become hits easily, probably because they match values many players hold dear. The tips of the branches are niche markets. Aiming squarely at one can make its players fall in love with your game, so not every game has to target the trunk.
- The more aware you are of the market, the better you can target it.

## Do this
- Before you settle on an audience, map the current player segments (physical console buyers, social-game players, location-based, competitive shooter, VR, fitness, MMO, PC indie) and decide deliberately which you're serving, rather than defaulting to your own taste.
- For maximum reach, design for the broad shared tastes of younger players, the trunk, using orthodox, widely appealing design.
- If you go niche, commit fully to one branch and aim to make its players fall in love with the game.
- For a series, plan era-appropriate successors so players' childhood enthusiasm can pass to the next generation.

## Avoid
- Picking your target audience based only on your personal likes and dislikes.

## Examples from the episode
- **Nintendo's best sellers:** they top charts without cutting-edge tech, largely because they're hits with younger players (the trunk).
- **NES era vs. today:** everyone once played similar games; now playstyles have branched into separate niches.

## Quote
> The more aware you are of the market, the better you'll be able to target it— and that's really the main point here. (03:51)
