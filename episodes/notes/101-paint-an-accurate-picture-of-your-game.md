# 101 · Paint an Accurate Picture of Your Game

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tb6GJUvJGpM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/101-paint-an-accurate-picture-of-your-game.md)

## In one sentence
Make your title, box art, screenshots and trailers show what your game actually feels like, because a misleading image misses the players who would love it and disappoints the ones it fools.

## Key ideas
- Until people play, they judge a game by its title, box art, screenshots and promo videos. Demos put a big strain on developers, so there's no guarantee one will exist, and the title and visual media have to carry the message.
- The big no-no: however much you want to surprise players, don't paint a picture that doesn't represent your game. It's like buying a banana that tastes like an apple. In the bigger picture, the player's impression of the game should be "one and the same" with the fun inside it.
- Titles and content aren't always decided with marketing in mind. Still, if the image and the reality don't match, you fail to reach the people the game would resonate with, and you disappoint the people who were misled into buying it.
- Surprises are fine, as long as the overall picture matches how the game actually feels.
- When coming up with a title, think about how people might shorten it. "Super Smash Bros." becomes "Smash," which may help people remember it.
- His biggest box-vs-content gap is the Japanese Kirby Super Star: a simple, fancy wooden-box cover for a vibrant game, an idea from Shigesato Itoi that worked partly because Kirby's design is inherently simple. (The Super Famicom discount sticker on the front spoiled the effect a little.) The cover looks unrelated, but it gets straight to the heart of what they were going for. Marketing isn't always direct.
- Once a game is in stores, cover art is crucial for drawing interest. There's a time and place for subverting expectations, but showing the game for what it truly is works better.

## Do this
- Before release, compare your title, cover, screenshots and trailer with the actual moment-to-moment play and ask whether a buyer's expectations would match the fun inside. Fix whatever doesn't line up.
- When choosing a title, list the short forms players are likely to use and pick a name that shortens to something memorable.
- If you go indirect or subversive with packaging, make sure it still captures the heart of the game, as Kirby Super Star's wooden-box cover did.
- Treat cover art as a main way to win new players on storefronts, and give it the attention that deserves.

## Avoid
- Misrepresenting the game in marketing for the sake of surprise ("a banana that tastes like an apple").
- Marketing that reaches the wrong audience and leaves misled buyers disappointed.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — title:** shortens naturally to "Smash," which makes it easier to remember.
- **Kirby Super Star (Japanese release) — box art:** a simple wooden-box design proposed by Shigesato Itoi that looks unrelated to the vibrant game but captures its heart.

## Quote
