# 215 · Final Output

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGPwDINsVbU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/215-final-output.md)

## In one sentence
On powerful hardware, judge visuals by the final composited frame (post-processing, blended hues, reflections) rather than by individually polished models, and consider giving post-processing its own specialist.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai mostly makes games for lower-powered hardware, so he may be "a bit late to this discussion." On high-end PCs and consoles, though, what he calls the "final output" matters more than ever.
- A rendered image isn't shown immediately. It is first stored in the framebuffer, where characters, backgrounds and other details are drawn, shaders are applied and rendering happens. Only once the whole image is drawn is it sent to the screen. Post-processing at this stage is key to quality graphics.
- In Smash Bros. Melee each object on a stage has its own distinct color. The same stage in Smash Bros. Ultimate blends many hues together, and subtle additions such as reflections off the water improve the final output.
- With more powerful hardware and more awareness among artists, final output can keep getting better.
- The topic is full of technical terms and methods, but the general idea is simple: "it's no longer enough to just focus on making your polygonal characters look good— what matters now is how everything comes together."
- It is easy to get caught up in small details, so step back when you can.
- He expects post-processing specialists to become the norm and greatly raise the overall quality of game visuals. Any team would want one.

## Do this
- Review and approve art in the final rendered frame with all post-processing on, not in isolation in a model viewer.
- Use post-processing and lighting to blend hues across the scene so objects share one look instead of each sitting in its own flat color.
- Add subtle touches such as reflections off water that tie elements together in the final image.
- Step back from asset-level detail regularly and judge the whole screen.
- On high-end projects, make one person responsible for post-processing and final output.

## Avoid
- Polishing individual characters and props while ignoring how the whole frame comes together.
- Getting lost in minor details.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee vs. Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — the same stage:** Melee's objects each have their own distinct color, while Ultimate blends hues and adds reflections off the water for a better final output.

## Quote
> Put simply, it's no longer enough to just focus on making your polygonal characters look good— what matters now is how everything comes together. (01:38)
