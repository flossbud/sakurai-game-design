# 026 · Fun Beyond Game Essence

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dq7X8-lB0is) · [Transcript](../transcripts/026-fun-beyond-game-essence.md)

## In one sentence
Game essence (push and pull, risk and reward) is only one source of fun, so identify what else your game's fun rests on, strengthen it, and add risk and reward where it fits.

## Key ideas
- A game's fun is more than its "game essence." Sakurai lists seven kinds of games that don't rely purely on it, and stresses that the categories are blurry and overlap.
- Games that are fun to control: the world in the screen is free from the laws of nature. Making control alone fun takes apt design choices and the skill to sense and fine-tune how the controls feel (RC cars tap into the same fun).
- Adventure games and visual novels, and cinematic or story-focused games: the fun is in watching a story unfold, but a compelling story alone isn't enough; the magic comes from stories that take advantage of interactivity. Story isn't game essence, but it enhances it: it keeps players pushing onward to face risks, and fleshed-out characters and worlds create a visceral connection.
- Licensed games are judged on whether fans get an enjoyable experience in the franchise's world, not on fun alone; there's little point critiquing them unless you approach them as a fan.
- Real-life simulations: as a game approaches real life, the player perceives more than is shown. A train simulator calls a handful of simple sound effects (doors clattering, feet shuffling) and it feels like a world exists beyond the screen.
- Building and crafting is fun on its own, but mechanics like dying from a fall, building defenses against enemies, or braving danger for materials bring in risk and reward. Crafting overlaps with real-life games because both are "playing pretend," and he calls Animal Crossing an even better example of this than Minecraft.
- Rhythm games tap the primal joy of rhythm, and get more fun the better you know a song. Strict timing adds risk: in beatmania a hair late is a miss, and a looser Good/Bad system would be less risky and possibly boring. He left sports games off the list because real sports already contain push and pull, and controlling the team you root for intensifies the ups and downs.

## Do this
- Name the main source of fun in your game (game essence, control feel, story, fandom, simulation, creation or rhythm) and put your polish budget into that source.
- If controlling the character is the fun, reserve time to sense and fine-tune how movement feels, not just to add content.
- In story-driven games, build the story around interactivity and use story beats and characters to push players onward into the next risk.
- In simulations, add a handful of simple, well-chosen sound effects (doors clattering, feet shuffling) that imply activity beyond what's on screen.
- In building and crafting games, layer in risk and reward: fall deaths, enemies you must build defenses against, or dangerous trips to gather materials.
- In timing-based games, keep judgment windows strict enough that being slightly late counts as a miss, so there's real risk.

## Avoid
- Assuming a compelling story alone is enough to make a good game.
- Critiquing a licensed game without approaching it as a fan.
- Loosening timing judgment so much that there's little risk, which can make the game feel boring.

## Examples from the episode
- **Ace Attorney:** a story-based game with more push and pull, blurring the line between genres.
- **Train simulator — arriving at a station:** doors clatter and passengers' feet shuffle; a few simple sound effects make it feel like a world exists beyond the screen.
- **Minecraft and Animal Crossing:** crafting games that overlap with "playing pretend"; Animal Crossing is the stronger example of that overlap.
- **beatmania:** being even a hair late on a note counts as a miss, which keeps risk in the game; a looser Good/Bad system might be boring.
- **Sports games:** left off the list because real sports already have push and pull, and controlling your favorite team intensifies the ups and downs.
