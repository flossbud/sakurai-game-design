# 041 · The Fiend's Cauldron

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ai7pBxHAcvU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/041-the-fiends-cauldron.md)

## In one sentence
Let players bet resources on higher difficulty for bigger rewards and ease off automatically after failure, but make sure newcomers understand how to turn the difficulty down.

## Key ideas
- Instead of Easy/Normal/Hard, Kid Icarus: Uprising uses the Fiend's Cauldron at the start of each stage. The default Intensity is 2.0, and pouring hearts (the game's currency) into the cauldron raises it.
- Higher Intensity makes enemies more aggressive, with higher attack and defense, so you need preparation and strategy. Clearing the stage earns more hearts plus stronger weapons and powers. Failing spills some hearts out of the cauldron; the difficulty is lower when you continue, but having lost hearts makes worthwhile rewards harder to get.
- You can also spend hearts to lower Intensity. At 0.0 you're nearly invincible, an almost guaranteed win.
- Benefits: a big risk earns a big reward, and it's a novel take on difficulty. Difficulty drops automatically after failure, so players rarely get stuck, and it stings less than bluntly asking whether you'd like to switch to Easy. Players don't have to play the whole game on Easy, which keeps the experience from feeling unsatisfying.
- Faults: it made the game feel too hardcore; a game constantly pushing you to your limits isn't "casual." The land stages were also too big with far too many enemies, and players couldn't respawn where they died. Game essence, push and pull, and risk and reward make a game "gamelike" but can make it less approachable, more so on the small 3DS screen.
- It wasn't clear enough that setting Intensity below 2.0 lowers difficulty, and newcomers needed that most; there wasn't time to introduce it to beginners properly.
- Modern games let you change difficulty anytime without restrictions; he agrees that's necessary but wishes strong play could still be rewarded. Smash for Wii U/3DS and Ultimate use a similar mechanic in some single-player modes, but the points matter less there than hearts did in Uprising.

## Do this
- Supplement or replace fixed difficulty levels with a stake: let players spend in-game currency to raise difficulty in exchange for proportionally bigger rewards (more currency, better items).
- On failure, automatically lower the difficulty for the retry and take part of the stake as the cost, so players rarely get stuck and never have to be asked to switch to Easy.
- Let players pay to lower difficulty all the way down to a near-invincible setting so no section can block progress.
- Teach newcomers explicitly that going below the default lowers difficulty, rather than only advertising the risky high end.
- Check that the content around the system (stage size, enemy counts, respawn points) supports it, so the whole game doesn't feel too hardcore.

## Avoid
- Bluntly asking failing players whether they want to switch to Easy Mode.
- Making struggling players play the whole game on Easy.
- Assuming beginners will discover how to lower the difficulty on their own.
- Letting constant risk and reward make the game feel too hardcore to approach.

## Examples from the episode
- **Kid Icarus: Uprising — Fiend's Cauldron:** default Intensity 2.0; hearts raise or lower it; failing spills hearts and lowers the difficulty; 0.0 is nearly invincible.
- **Kid Icarus: Uprising — land stages:** too big, with far too many enemies, and no respawning where you died, so not all the pieces meshed.
- **Super Smash Bros. for Wii U/3DS and Ultimate — single-player modes:** a similar mechanic, but the points matter less than Uprising's hearts.
