# 053 · Sora Ltd.

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VVhGCtCNGHg) · [Transcript](../transcripts/053-sora-ltd.md)

## In one sentence
If you go independent, keep company management from eating your creative time: Sakurai runs Sora Ltd. as a near-staffless contracting company, directs at partner studios one project at a time, and is paid only from sales to share the publisher's risk.

## Key ideas
- Sora is a *yūgen-gaisha*, a type of limited company that can no longer be founded after a 2006 law change. Sakurai kept that status anyway "because it's more fun," and he has no plans to sell stock.
- He founded Sora to make contracting with other companies easier. It employs almost no game production staff, and he isn't hiring or accepting inquiries (so please don't send resumés).
- His reasoning: creators who go independent usually form a company, hire a handful of staff and start a game, then get bogged down in the management needed to keep employees paid, leaving no time for the game itself. He chose to be a "wandering director" instead.
- At a partner company he works a set schedule as if he were employed there, and once a directing job starts, his standard procedure is to focus on that project alone.
- His contracts usually pay nothing during development; his income comes from game sales after release. If the project falls through, he earns nothing, even if the failure wasn't his fault ("Truly a high-risk job"). External contractors are usually paid a monthly development fee, which helps with taxes and lets companies retain workers, but the publisher sees no profit until release, so going without a fee shares that risk with them.
- He sometimes wishes he had more employees or wonders about scaling up to a full studio, but he handles those needs in other ways and is happy with the niche he has carved out.

## Do this
- Before going independent and hiring a team, estimate how much of your time payroll and company management would take away from making the game.
- Once you take on a directing job, commit to that one project rather than splitting your attention.
- If you want to share a partner's risk, consider pay tied to sales after release instead of a monthly fee, but only if you can absorb earning nothing should the project be cancelled.

## Avoid
- Hiring staff by default when you go independent, then losing your creative time to company management.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sora Ltd. — contract model:** no pay during development and income based on sales, compared with the usual monthly fee paid to outside contractors.
