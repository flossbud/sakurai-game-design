# 054 · Listen in Various Environments

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WUy4lTUhj80) · [Transcript](../transcripts/054-listen-in-various-environments.md)

## In one sentence
Check and tune your game's sound on headphones, PC speakers and above all a cheap TV, where most players will hear it, and never sign off on audio from big studio speakers alone.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai always compares sound in several environments: headphones, then PC speakers, and perhaps most importantly a cheap television.
- TV speakers tend to be small and lose low frequencies, so sounds hit less hard. But that's the most common setup, so it's how most players will hear the game.
- A soundproofed studio with great speakers matters for getting the sound right, but if you rely too much on that pristine environment, your details may be inaudible on everyday speakers. Set up varied listening environments and solve the problems in each. It's arduous and can pull you away from the delicate audio you wanted, but it's necessary.
- Once you check sound this way, buying new headphones gets hard: if your reference changes, it's tough to stay consistent with everything you checked before.
- The Switch's built-in speakers are worth checking but sit low on the list, because they already sound crisp and clear, and many handheld players play with the sound off.
- Don't take the tuning so far that you stray from your creative vision; aim for a balance that works across environments.
- The Smash Bros. sound that gave the team the most trouble was Ryu's impact sounds. Copying the original game's audio wasn't enough; they wanted to capture how it sounded coming out of the arcade cabinet, which took lots of trial and error.

## Do this
- Set up at least three listening setups (headphones, PC speakers and an inexpensive TV) and compare every important sound on each before approving it.
- Pay special attention to sounds whose punch comes from low frequencies, and make sure they still land on TV speakers that drop the bass.
- Keep the same reference headphones and speakers for the whole project so your standard doesn't shift partway through.
- Never finalize the mix on big studio speakers alone.
- When recreating a sound from another game, match how it sounded through its original hardware (e.g. an arcade cabinet), not just the raw audio file.

## Avoid
- Finalizing sound only on studio monitors.
- Changing your reference headphones partway through a project.
- Tuning for weak speakers so hard that you lose your creative vision.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — Ryu's impact sounds:** the hardest sound; the goal was to capture how it sounded from the arcade cabinet, not just copy the original audio.
- **Nintendo Switch — built-in speakers:** clear already and lower priority, since many handheld players play muted.

## Quote
> More than anything, I recommend not solely using big studio speakers to finalize sound. (01:46)
