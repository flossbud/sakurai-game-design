# 233 · In-Game Cutscenes

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VZRh0P40RAM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/233-in-game-cutscenes.md)

## In one sentence
Use cutscenes where they add emotion, clarity or a breather, but respect the player's time: keep them tight, always let players skip them (credits included), ideally add fast-forward, and use quick-time events with great caution.

## Key ideas
- Modern games can render so well in-engine that pre-rendered cutscenes are sometimes a step down, with lower resolution and frame rate.
- Long stretches of fairly irrelevant cutscenes make Sakurai impatient. But long cutscenes aren't good or bad in themselves: they can tell a story more clearly or build emotional investment, and subtle, meaningful moments are hard to recreate during gameplay.
- In fast action games, cutscenes can give players a breather. Those games usually include areas without enemies anyway, so cutscenes needn't be a high priority.
- Cutscenes are sometimes the selling point, a reward for playing well. But they end up all over the internet now, so it's hard to treat them as a reward.
- Story-focused games have it tough. In-engine dialogue scenes can usually be skipped line by line, but most cutscenes can't, which is off-putting. Letting players skip ahead or fast-forward would be friendlier, since people now watch videos slightly sped up on DVRs and YouTube.
- Players should always be able to skip cutscenes, including the credits. Even on a first playthrough, it may not be their first time seeing the scene.
- Quick-time events (button presses during a cutscene) seem to be quite unpopular. Sakurai suggests extreme caution.

## Do this
- Make every cutscene skippable, including the credits, even on the first playthrough.
- Add fast-forward or skip-ahead inside cutscenes, like line-by-line skipping in dialogue scenes.
- Trim cutscene length wherever the content isn't relevant, and save long scenes for emotional or story beats gameplay can't carry.
- In fast action games, use enemy-free areas or short scenes as breathers rather than leaning on long cutscenes.
- Don't make cutscenes the main reward for good play, since players can watch them online anyway.
- If you add quick-time events to cutscenes, test them with players very carefully before committing.

## Avoid
- Long stretches of relatively irrelevant cutscenes.
- Unskippable cutscenes or credits.
- Quick-time events added only to keep people on edge.

## Examples from the episode
- **In-engine dialogue scenes:** usually skippable line by line, unlike most cutscenes.
- **DVRs, YouTube and streaming services:** viewers are now used to watching videos sped up.
