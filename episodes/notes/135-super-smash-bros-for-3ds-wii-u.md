# 135 · Super Smash Bros. for 3DS / Wii U

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeLrIXCOUw0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/135-super-smash-bros-for-3ds-wii-u.md)

## In one sentence
When you build one game for two platforms, share the mechanics, animations and balance, give each version modes that use its own hardware strengths, and treat a stable frame rate as non-negotiable, cutting content until you hit it.

## Key ideas
- **Why both at once:** Smash had never been on a handheld, which gave a 3DS version its own appeal, but the 3DS's processing speed and graphics were a challenge. The Wii U was an HD console whose main offer was power, so it was hard to do something truly new with it alone. Sakurai framed the split as the "one" (a handheld, one player per machine) versus the "many" (a home console). Mechanics, animations and balance could be shared to an extent, so building both at once "wouldn't actually mean double the work."
- **The weaker hardware set the limits:** Ice Climbers (two characters controlled at once) was too much for the 3DS. How fast fighters fly off-screen was limited, and even made slower than before, so the game stayed readable on the 3DS's LCD screen. Overall game speed sits between Brawl (laid-back) and Melee (frantic).
- **Platform-specific content:** 3DS stages draw on handheld games and Wii U stages on home-console titles. StreetPass is 3DS-only and 8-Player Smash is Wii U-only. Smash Run (3DS) gives players five minutes to collect power-ups before a final showdown. Its rules come straight from Kirby Air Ride's City Trial, and because each player has their own screen, everyone can explore a huge map freely. Smash Tour (Wii U) is a board-game-style group party mode.
- **"Variable character strength" as a framework:** Smash Run and Smash Tour both work by changing character parameters. Once that framework existed, more features could sit on top of it: Character Creation (equippable items, adjustable attack power and speed, three options for each special move), customizable Mii Fighters, and amiibo, which train CPU fighters that gain battle experience. It isn't suited to hardcore matches that need a level playing field, but it hits the mark for broader, party-style play.
- **Parameter tuning was finally delegated:** On Smash, Meteos and Kid Icarus: Uprising, Sakurai had entered every parameter himself. He says that, with all his supervision, approvals and marketing work, he "should never have been handling parameters." Combining his Smash know-how with Bandai Namco Studios' (BNS) own experience produced an efficient pipeline, so a small team of planners took over tuning, backed by more play testers to gauge versus balance. On Ultimate he stopped entering data and only supervised.
- **Technical bar:** The 3DS version supports four-player matches and stereoscopic 3D at a stable 60 FPS, with items, Pokémon and Assist Trophies all in. Many 3DS games ran at 30 FPS or dropped 3D. Sakurai saw 60 FPS as "an absolute must" and pared things down until it worked.
- **Updates and reveals:** These were the first Smash games with online updates, so balance could change after release and paid DLC fighters could be added. The cost was a longer schedule: release was fall to winter 2014, but development only wrapped in spring 2016. Fighter reveal videos (Villager, Mega Man, Wii Fit Trainer) grew into bigger, flashier events from here on. BNS's involvement was announced right as the project began (June 2012) to head off speculation about Sakurai being seen at their offices.

## Do this
- Before building a game for two platforms, write down each platform's unique strength (e.g., every player having their own handheld screen vs. many players around one TV) and give each version at least one mode built on it. Keep mechanics, animation and balance data shared so the second version isn't double work.
- Let the weakest target set shared limits: cap launch and movement speeds so action stays readable on the smallest screen, and cut features (like a two-character fighter) the weaker hardware can't run.
- Declare a stable 60 FPS a hard requirement early, and pare content and effects on each platform until it holds, rather than dropping to 30 FPS or cutting a headline feature.
- Build a parameter layer that can change character strength (stat boosts, equipment, alternate versions of special moves). Use it to power party modes, customization and trainable AI, and keep it out of modes that need a level playing field.
- Get the director out of raw data entry: invest in a tuning pipeline that lets a small team of planners enter parameters, add play testers to check versus balance, and keep the director in a supervising role.
- If people will speculate about a partnership anyway (e.g., an external studio), announce it at the start of the project.

## Avoid
- A director who enters all the parameters personally while also handling supervision, approvals and marketing.
- Treating post-launch DLC and balance updates as free. They stretch the schedule until the project feels like it will never end.
- Using variable character strength in modes meant for serious, level-playing-field competition.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Run (3DS):** a five-minute power-up hunt across a huge map, followed by a final showdown. Rules taken from Kirby Air Ride's City Trial, made possible by each player having their own screen.
- **Smash Tour (Wii U):** a board-game-inspired party mode, also built around power-ups.
- **Ice Climbers:** controlling two characters at once was too heavy for the 3DS.
- **Launch speed:** knockback flight was made slower than before to stay readable on the 3DS LCD.
- **amiibo:** scan a figure to get a CPU fighter that learns as it fights. amiibo are essentially the only exception to Smash's ban on merchandise beyond free giveaways and limited editions.
- **3DS tech:** four players, stereoscopic 3D and a stable 60 FPS with all items, Pokémon and Assist Trophies included.
- **Fighter reveal videos:** Villager, Mega Man and Wii Fit Trainer. Reveals became larger events from this game through Ultimate.
