# 067 · You'll Forget the Hardship, But the Game Will Endure!

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPSFfqxpVJk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/067-youll-forget-the-hardship-but-the-game-will-endure.md)

## In one sentence
Don't take the easy way out during development: the hardship fades, but the game and its mistakes last, and every player multiplies the effect of your effort.

## Key ideas
- Game development is hard work, "almost like stacking small stones one at a time," especially with computers that don't always do what you want. Still, if it meant more people played his game, Sakurai would put up with any hardship.
- If lots of people play your game, the hard work pays off. This isn't about making a huge hit; any game probably reaches more than tens or hundreds of people. If 1,000 people play, the impact is 1,000 times your hardship, and the same holds for 10,000 and beyond. Many things in the world are made on this principle, and we all support each other.
- If you take the easy way out and make too many compromises, you'll regret it later: "You'll forget the hardship, but the game will endure!"
- He has forgotten the difficulties of individual projects, but never the parts he got wrong. In the ending of Kirby's Adventure, the screen shakes when it was supposed to flash white, and he still remembers that slip.
- Smash Bros. Ultimate has sold over 29 million copies, about the population of Australia (people, not households), and the whole series is roughly the population of the UK. For him, those numbers sweep the hardship away.
- He isn't saying workers should go through needless hardship. Once you're set up to work efficiently, do everything you can to serve your customers, and remember that real people will be buying what you make.

## Do this
- Before accepting a compromise to save effort, ask whether you'll regret it after release, since flaws stay in the game permanently.
- Estimate how many people will play, and remember that every improvement is multiplied by each of them.
- Make your workflow efficient first, then put the effort you save into serving players rather than into needless hardship.
- Check that key presentation moments, such as the ending's effects, are implemented exactly as specified (a shake where a white flash was intended is the slip Sakurai still remembers).

## Avoid
- Taking the easy way out and making too many compromises.
- Needless hardship for workers, as opposed to efficient, focused effort.

## Examples from the episode
- **Kirby's Adventure — ending:** the screen shakes where it was supposed to flash white, an error Sakurai has never forgotten.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — sales:** over 29 million copies, about the population of Australia; the whole series is roughly the population of the UK.
