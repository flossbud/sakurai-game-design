# 249 · Never Get Into Fights

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kgrvdeLBR8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/249-never-get-into-fights.md)

## In one sentence
Treat every coworker as someone you'll work with again: never fight, badmouth people or leave on bad terms, because the game industry is small and that behavior comes back to you.

## Key ideas
- Game developers often move between teams, whether they're temps or not. A coworker on your team today could be your coworker on a different team later.
- If you fight with teammates, cause them problems, act selfishly, leave the company on bad terms or abandon a project to jump to the next job, one of those old colleagues may join your fresh start, sometimes years later on an unrelated project. The industry is much smaller than you might think.
- Don't speak ill of people either. If you treat someone poorly they won't think highly of you, and you'll find yourself excluded. Unless you leave the industry entirely, it almost always comes back to haunt you.
- Directors at other companies sometimes ask Sakurai what he thinks of someone from his team who is interviewing with them. He never speaks poorly of anyone or reveals anything confidential, but these questions do get asked.
- This isn't "grow up and deal with it." Speaking your mind often benefits everyone. But you're responsible for the atmosphere at your workplace, and focusing on unpleasant people can pull your attention away from the supportive people who cause no issues.
- It's fine to distance yourself from a situation that doesn't suit you, and fine to give up-front constructive feedback. Just don't think poorly of people, because that dislike comes straight back to you. Teams should be striving together toward one common goal.

## Do this
- Before you leave a team or company, finish or hand off your work properly instead of abandoning the project, and leave on good terms.
- When something bothers you, raise it directly as constructive feedback about the work, not as complaints about the person behind their back.
- If a situation or person doesn't suit you, step back from it rather than escalating into a fight.
- Deliberately put your attention on the supportive teammates instead of dwelling on the difficult ones.
- Act as if any colleague could be your teammate again, or could be asked about you by a future employer.

## Avoid
- Fighting with teammates, speaking ill of people, acting selfishly, or leaving on bad terms.
- Abandoning a project midway to dive into the next job.
- Holding on to dislike for people.

- **Reference checks:** directors from other companies ask Sakurai about former team members who are interviewing with them.
