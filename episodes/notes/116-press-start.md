# 116 · ♪ PRESS START

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zJcPrrjFNFw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/116-press-start.md)

## In one sentence
Treat game music as worth presenting on its own: when you curate a showcase, keep the performance in the spotlight, and size each edition so you can keep it going alongside your development work.

## Key ideas
- For 10 years starting in 2006, Sakurai presided over PRESS START, a once-a-year "omnibus" game music concert drawing from many titles. Most shows were in Tokyo, with others in Yokohama, Osaka, Nagoya, Shanghai and Paris. The committee also included conductor Taizo Takemoto, Nobuo Uematsu, Shogo Sakai and Kazushige Nojima.
- The idea came up in 2002, when Final Fantasy and Smash Bros. Melee each had their own orchestral concert led by Takemoto. The five of them went out together and talked about a concert that would broaden the reach of game music. Company AZA (production partner) and Enterbrain (sponsor) made it real.
- Koichi Sugiyama had held his own omnibus game music concerts, often sponsored by game makers. Sakurai went to one: hearing Donkey Kong Country music live made him half expect Donkey Kong to walk on stage, and showed him how powerful live music can be. The opening song was from Kirby Super Star. He respects Sugiyama for raising the status of game music at a time when many soundtracks weren't seen as suitable for orchestras.
- Process: choose songs first. Sakurai was the only committee member familiar with lots of games, so he brought many samples to meetings, where they listened and narrowed them down. Arrangers then orchestrated and scored them, followed by rehearsal and performance. Game footage was used only occasionally, because video after video would distract from the point of the concert: the performance.
- Nearly every show sold out. Each program had 14–16 pieces, about 60 songs counting medleys. Researching and pitching more than that, including songs that didn't make the cut, would have been too much while he was deep in game development. Licensing prevented CDs, which limited how many people could hear the music.
- They planned a 10-year run from the start and saw it through. By the end, game music concerts had become common in Japan, so the series had done its job. He believes live music can stir emotions you can't find elsewhere and recommends going to a concert.
- The rest of the video is concert footage with no narration: The Legend of Zelda (Yuzo Koshiro Version) and a Xenoblade Chronicles medley.

## Do this
- When curating music (or any showcase) from many games, gather far more candidates than you have slots and narrow them down as a group by listening together.
- In a live music event, use game footage only now and then. The performance is the point, and constant video pulls attention away from it.
- Before launching a recurring side project, fix its total run (PRESS START planned 10 years) and its per-edition size (14–16 program pieces, about 60 songs) so it stays manageable next to your main job.
- Go hear game music performed live, to experience what it can do for an audience.

## Avoid
- Playing video after video during a concert; it distracts from the performance.

## Examples from the episode
