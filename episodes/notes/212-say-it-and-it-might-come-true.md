# 212 · Say It, and It Might Come True

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sbEFYooj0Y8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/212-say-it-and-it-might-come-true.md)

## In one sentence
The words you use shape your attitude and your actions, so stop broadcasting defeatist thoughts, turn negative feelings into concrete questions about how to improve, and take on challenges fired up.

## Key ideas
- Saying "It'll rain tomorrow" or "I'll be in the Olympics" won't change much. Negative statements are different: "I'm running out of money," "There's barely any point in working," "Life's just not fun." Each time you think one, and especially when you say it out loud or post it on social media, it becomes "a little more true."
- Positive statements ("I'm gonna be rich!", "My work has purpose!") might change your fortunes, but Sakurai says framing it as fortune sounds like "occult gibberish," and that isn't his point.
- His real point is that your thoughts and words set your attitude toward life. You can't see the effect, which is exactly why you shouldn't take it lightly.
- He isn't saying a negative outlook is always bad, and this isn't a positive-thinking pep talk. It's fine to have those feelings, and a negative outlook can be a bigger opportunity for change than a positive one.
- Use a bad situation as motivation. If your work feels pointless, ask how to make it better. If you aren't enjoying life, ask how to change things. That kind of attitude points you in a clearer direction.
- The words you speak tend to set the course of your actions. When you take on a challenge, meet it head-on and get as psyched up as possible. "Those who act are the ones who win," and telling yourself "Do it! Come on! Go!" already starts you moving.

## Do this
- Notice when you voice or post defeatist statements about your work, and stop putting them out loud or on social media.
- Rewrite each negative thought as a question about what to change. "My work feels pointless" becomes "What are some ways I can make it better?"
- Before a hard task, psych yourself up out loud ("Do it! Come on! Go!") and start right away.

## Avoid
- Repeating negative ideas out loud or on social media, which makes them feel more true.
- Mistaking this for forced positivity. The aim is to use negative feelings as fuel, not to deny them.

## Examples from the episode
- **Negative self-talk:** "I'm running out of money." "There's barely any point in working." "Life's just not fun."
- **Turned into questions:** "If your work feels pointless, what are some ways you can make it better?"

## Quote
> What I mean to say is, your thoughts and words establish your attitude toward life! (01:07)
