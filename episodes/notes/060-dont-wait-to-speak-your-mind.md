# 060 · Don't Wait to Speak Your Mind

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f3UjN6cCBYY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/060-dont-wait-to-speak-your-mind.md)

## In one sentence
Raise concerns about the game or the team the moment you have them, together with a proposed fix, because only during development can anyone still do something about them.

## Key ideas
- People with different skills, standpoints and tastes notice different problems, in the game's design and in how the team is managed. Sometimes they simply dislike something.
- Hiding your dissatisfaction and announcing it after the project ("Actually, this is how I felt the whole time!") is no good. Whether or not it would have changed anything, it's too late: it only satisfies your ego, upsets teammates and adds nothing to the project.
- Share a thought the moment you have it. Unhappy players can only complain; they can't fix anything. A developer can, so being on the team puts you in a hugely advantageous position. Don't assume nobody will listen; make your thoughts visible to the people who can act on them.
- It's okay to say "This isn't good," but it's better to offer a possible solution, with the problem and solution as a set. That builds a constructive environment. If the team keeps things as they are, they'll have reasons, and hearing them helps you accept the decision. The issue may even resolve itself while you think it over, and your solution might end up helping many players.
- On recent projects Sakurai set up "opinion boards" where anyone can write what they think about how development is going. He replies with his own thinking or passes items to the relevant teams. Things sometimes get lost, and some people still won't speak up, but hearing everyone matters.
- Quick course corrections make goals easier to reach, so weigh pros and cons and decide fast. On the listening side, be flexible; a broader viewpoint and deeper understanding help you reach solutions quickly.

## Do this
- When something about the game or the way the team works bothers you, raise it now with someone who can change it, not in the postmortem.
- Present every complaint as a package: the problem plus at least one proposed solution.
- Set up an opinion board anyone on the team can post to about how development is going, and have the director reply to posts or forward them to the responsible team.
- If the team decides to keep things as they are, ask for their reasons and listen to them.
- When you're on the receiving end, respond flexibly and decide quickly so the project can course-correct early.

## Avoid
- Saving up your objections until the project ends.
- Assuming nobody will listen, and staying quiet because of it.
- Saying only "This isn't good" without suggesting a way forward.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sakurai's recent projects — opinion boards:** an open place for team members to post opinions on development; he comments or routes them to teams.
