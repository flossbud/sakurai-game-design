# 258 · A Director’s Job is to Trim

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MS2Tf3nXhZE) · [Transcript](../transcripts/258-a-directors-job-is-to-trim.md)

## In one sentence
A director creates and also trims, cutting excess and steering the project back on course when plans slip, and that only works if team members report accurate information, including trouble, as early as possible.

## Key ideas
- A director is the person who sets the course. In practice that means a wide range of decisions, and considering and acting on the best solution in both the short and long term, even when not every solution will make players happy.
- Plenty of things don't go to plan: hardware limits, delayed deadlines, a growing workload and more. The director is there to anticipate the damage, offload the heavy or flammable cargo, and get things back on course.
- Many people imagine compromises come from battles between cost-conscious producers and directors focused on quality and fun. Some companies may work that way, but it's ultimately faster when the director recognizes and addresses the problems, because a director not only creates but is also responsible for trimming the excess. Just about any finished product is the result of trimming and hard work.
- To trim well, you need accurate information as soon as possible. Every piece of information feeds those decisions, and if you act on an incorrect premise, everything you do will be incorrect.
- The biggest problems come when a task has a scheduled deadline and a team member keeps struggling with it, then says at the last minute that they can't do it. Had they come to the director during the process, an appropriate solution could probably have been found, and the time spent hesitating wouldn't have been lost.
- Build a cooperative relationship within the team so the baton can be passed quickly, and always give accurate information, even when you're having trouble.

## Do this
- As director, own the cuts. When hardware limits, deadlines or workload slip, decide yourself what to drop or scale back rather than waiting for a producer to force it.
- Set a team rule that anyone stuck on a task reports it right away, with an honest status, well before the deadline.
- Check that the information behind a decision is accurate before acting on it.
- When someone reports they're stuck, treat it as a baton pass and quickly reassign, re-scope or find another solution.

## Avoid
- Struggling silently on a task and admitting you can't do it only at the last minute.
- Treating scope compromises as a fight between the producer and the director.
- Making decisions on incorrect premises.
