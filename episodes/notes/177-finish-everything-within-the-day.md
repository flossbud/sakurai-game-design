# 177 · Finish Everything Within the Day

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j1qLM0yfOuY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/177-finish-everything-within-the-day.md)

## In one sentence
Answer every review request the same day it arrives, first come first served, so you never stall your team, and set aside a fixed block of time when you do no reviews at all.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai reviews nearly every part of his team's work because he's responsible for how the game turns out. With hundreds of people on a team, that means a lot of reviews and change requests.
- His process: once production work is finished, the team member sends an email titled "Review Request". He doesn't use instant messaging or ticketing systems for this, because each has its own drawbacks.
- He handles requests first come, first served. He checks the request, gathers the materials he needs, does the review, and explains any changes to everyone involved.
- He gets a lot of these emails every day, and no matter how many pile up, he always replies within the same day. In practice he answers as soon as an email arrives, or right after the review he's already doing.
- Why same-day matters: the longer you take, the longer team members wait. They have other work, but delaying a key team member's decision can put their whole team behind schedule. Quick replies also make the second and third rounds of review easier, and putting replies off just makes the next day more miserable.
- Review emails arrive in scattered bursts, sometimes none and sometimes a deluge, which can break a director's day into erratic pieces even though directors have plenty of other work. His fix is a set timeframe, like mornings or after a cutoff point, when he stops reviewing and works only on his own tasks.

## Do this
- Have team members request reviews by email with a fixed subject line such as "Review Request", sent once the work is actually finished.
- Work through review requests in the order they arrive, and reply to each one the same day, ideally as soon as you finish the review you're currently on.
- When a review needs changes, explain them to everyone involved in that work, not only the person who asked.
- Block a fixed daily period (for example mornings, or everything after a set cutoff time) with no reviews, and use it only for your own tasks.

## Avoid
- Letting unread review requests pile up in your inbox overnight.
- Sitting on a key team member's decision, which can push their whole team behind schedule.
- Leaving yourself open to review requests all day, which chops your schedule into unproductive pieces.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sakurai's supervision workflow:** email-only "Review Request" messages, handled first come first served and answered the same day, with a protected block of time for his own work.
