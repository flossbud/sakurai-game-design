# 163 · Fighter Reveal Videos

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K_z28RqWDU4) · [Transcript](../transcripts/163-fighter-reveal-videos.md)

## In one sentence
If players will share your movies online anyway, make showcase videos that aren't tied to game progress, produce them like film (plot, storyboards, animatics, then final CG and sound), and push every shot bolder than "good enough".

## Key ideas
- In Brawl, Sakurai made movie sequences showcasing each fighter, partly to present Smash's unique crossovers in a concrete, charming way and partly as a reward for pushing on through the Subspace Emissary adventure mode. They cost a lot of money and effort, but people posted them online before the game was even out, including overseas media using pre-release copies, so they weren't much of a reward. He stopped making movies that cut in during gameplay: "It's a tough time to be making story-driven games."
- Since people will share movies, he wanted something everyone could enjoy that wasn't tied to game progress. That became fighter reveals, which started officially with Smash for 3DS/Wii U. They've made 37 of them, each split into CG segments and gameplay segments.
- CG pipeline: Sakurai writes almost every plot himself (Pyra and Mythra's was left to Monolith Soft). A plot works like a planning document, laying out how each cut is shown and how characters behave, and often when the music comes in. He then meets the production staff, such as the CG studio, to agree on details. Storyboards come next (Digital Frontier has expert storyboard artists), with frequent adjustment meetings from then on. Animatics are rough edits made by moving and stretching the storyboard art, and at that point "the video's structure and length are essentially set". Previz then weaves in CG (Min Min's background stayed a flat image even in the final video). After that comes production: models and art swapped in, backgrounds, effects and blur, up to "final" quality. Once the video starts coming together, sound is produced, using the game's sound effects as a base.
- The CG models are touched-up revisions of the game models, so they hold up at higher resolution and suit CG rendering.
- Gameplay segments are recorded at places like Bandai Namco Studios and Mario Club by Smash dev team members, using debug and replay features to find the best camera angles. Sakurai checks the footage repeatedly and the structure is gradually brushed up.
- His most frequent notes: when you think something's good enough, zoom in and go bolder; tweak the angle of view to transform the layout; add more interactions with other fighters; balance serious and comical; sync footage to the music. The original rightsholders check every reveal and have generally been very pleased, and the videos became a global sensation that encouraged the team.

## Do this
- Assume any in-game movie will be uploaded at or before launch. Don't make it the payoff for progression; turn showcase movies into marketing that everyone can watch.
- Write each trailer's "plot" like a planning document: every cut, what each character does, and where the music comes in, before anyone starts animating.
- Lock structure and running time with animatics (storyboard art moved and stretched into a rough edit) before committing to expensive CG.
- Build replay and debug-camera tools so gameplay footage for trailers can be re-shot from the best angle instead of captured live.
- Give every trailer shot a polish pass with these checks: zoom in and go bolder, change the angle of view, add interaction between characters, balance serious and comic beats, cut to the music.
- Make higher-detail revisions of game models for pre-rendered CG, and build trailer sound from the game's own sound effects.

## Avoid
- Spending heavily on in-game movies as progression rewards when they'll be posted online anyway.
- Settling for "good enough" framing on a shot.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Brawl — per-fighter movies in Subspace Emissary:** meant as rewards, but leaked online via pre-release copies, so in-gameplay movies were dropped.
- **Super Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U onward — fighter reveals:** 37 reveal videos, with plots by Sakurai, storyboards from Digital Frontier's artists, and gameplay captured with debug and replay tools.
- **Pyra and Mythra — plot by Monolith Soft:** the only reveal whose plot Sakurai didn't write.
- **Min Min — flat background:** the background stayed a flat image even in the final video.
