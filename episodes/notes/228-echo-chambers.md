# 228 · Echo Chambers

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuI0zoKIURY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/228-echo-chambers.md)

## In one sentence
Notice when you're only hearing agreement about your ideas, designs or reviews, and stay objective, while still keeping the conviction to push your game in its own deliberately "unbalanced" direction.

## Key ideas
- On social media, an echo chamber is when your views keep being reinforced. You post an opinion, many people agree, and you feel sure you're right, even though plenty of people disagree. "Like attracts like," and you end up in a world closed off from other views.
- Social media is built around communities you're interested in, so this is bound to happen. Taken too far, you lose sight of problems that need solving or start causing trouble for others.
- It happens with design ideas and with reviews, almost every day.
- Sakurai turns it on the viewer: whatever you think he meant by this video, finding someone who agrees will make you feel validated, and letting in only those opinions is an echo chamber too. He says he is just stating facts.
- An echo chamber can affect a product. You may play things safer or push them further, and if you can't stay objective, the game ends up unbalanced.
- Yet sometimes going in your own "unbalanced" direction is what feels best, and it beats being swayed by other people. Listen to your team's concerns and adapt, but a unique vision gives a game distinctive strength. In the end, strike a balance.

## Do this
- When a design idea gets nothing but praise, deliberately ask people outside that circle (other teams, players of other genres) what is wrong with it.
- Before acting on reviews or social-media reactions, check whether you're only seeing the voices that agree with you.
- Sort feedback into team concerns you should adapt to and outside pressure that would just make the game more ordinary, and protect the deliberate quirks that make your game distinctive.

## Avoid
- Letting in only the opinions that validate you.
- Being swayed by others until the game loses its own distinctive direction.

## Examples from the episode
- **Social media — posting an opinion:** a flood of agreement makes you feel right, though many people who disagree simply aren't showing up.

## Quote
> You might play things safer, or you may push it further— and in either case, if you can't stay objective, your game will surely end up unbalanced. (02:12)
