# 108 · Super Smash Bros. Brawl

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dXpFTHtlmCo) · [Transcript](../transcripts/108-super-smash-bros-brawl.md)

## In one sentence
Build a sequel's concept around what its platform and audience need—for Brawl, online play and a single-player mode of appropriate length—while slowing the controls for casual players and giving every character individuality.

## Key ideas
- At E3 2005, the day after the Wii was announced, President Iwata asked Sakurai, by then freelance, to make Smash for Wii. Iwata later told him that if he'd declined, they might simply have re-released Melee and its 26 fighters as-is.
- The concept had two key parts: online support and a single-player mode that would take an appropriate amount of time to complete.
- **Online:** the Wii had online built in, so supporting it was essential. Sakurai personally thinks online and Smash aren't a great fit. Much of Smash's appeal is becoming the champion among your friends, and online competition can make people lose confidence. The game also needs fully synchronous communication, a major disadvantage compared with the asynchronous kind in games like Mario Kart, so it works best with players close by (your own region, not just your own country), and lag makes it choppy. Still: "If my job is to do it, I'll do it." When it worked, his reaction was "Wow, it's actually possible."
- **The Subspace Emissary:** cutscenes, a story, a map, sticker power-ups and a cinematic ending. It aimed to give every character a moment in the spotlight instead of only being tiny fighters knocking each other around. It was almost another whole game. He hoped to outsource it but couldn't find a suitable contractor, so everything except the cutscenes was built in-house, and the team passed 100 people for the first time. Fighters needed separate jump and movement parameters for this mode because their Versus specs didn't work there, which gave two parameter sets per fighter, all tuned by Sakurai.
- **New features:**
  - **Final Smashes:** an item could have provided a powerful attack, but each fighter got their own unique move to boost individuality.
  - **Assist Trophies:** brought in characters from other Nintendo games, and summoning them on the spot adds push and pull.
  - **My Music:** each generation has its own Mario, and its own music, so one song per world felt wasteful. It also let him assemble a dream team of composers.
  - **Guest fighters (Snake, Sonic):** didn't change how he worked. From his point of view Nintendo is an "outside company" too, so he approaches original creators, explains his goals and looks for a middle ground.
- **Speed:** Melee was so fast that it was hard to master and too hard on new players. It was great for top players and spectators but harsh on casuals, and even gave some competitive players tendinitis. The Wii targeted casual players, Brawl had to be playable on a sideways Wii Remote, and the higher the input density, the more online frame skips stood out. So Brawl's speed was a little slower and tuned toward casual play.
- **Testing and results:** Brawl was the first time Smash used playtesters, only four, and not professional-level players; Sakurai still tuned all fighter parameters himself. Brawl is the second best-selling Smash (excluding the combined 3DS/Wii U sales).

## Do this
- Anchor a sequel's concept to a few key pillars the platform and audience demand (for Brawl: online support and a single-player mode of appropriate length).
- If characters move from one mode to a very different one (versus arena → side-scrolling adventure), give them a separate set of jump and movement parameters for that mode, and budget time to tune both sets.
- When a feature could be a generic item, consider making it unique to each character instead, as with Final Smashes, to build individuality.
- When targeting casual players, simpler controllers or laggy online play, slow the game a little and lower the input density it demands.
- Offer several music tracks per world and let players choose (as My Music did), so each generation can hear the version they grew up with.
- When working with outside IP, explain your vision to the original creators and look for a middle ground between their game and yours.

## Avoid
- Keeping a hardcore, high-input-density control scheme when your audience is casual, since it's too harsh on new players and can even physically strain experts.
- Dropping characters into a new mode with specs tuned for another mode.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee → Brawl — game speed:** Melee's speed was hard on newcomers (and gave some players tendinitis), so Brawl was slowed and tuned for casual play.
- **Brawl — Subspace Emissary:** a story mode with a map, stickers and a cinematic ending; fighters got separate jump and movement parameters for it.
- **Brawl — Final Smashes:** a unique super move per fighter instead of a generic power item.
- **Brawl — Assist Trophies:** summoning characters from other Nintendo games on the spot adds push and pull.
- **Brawl — My Music:** multiple tracks per series so each generation's favorites are represented.
- **Mario Kart — online:** asynchronous communication, which handles online better than Smash's fully synchronous play.
