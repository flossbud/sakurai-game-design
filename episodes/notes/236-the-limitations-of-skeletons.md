# 236 · The Limitations of Skeletons

**Category:** Animation · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q77TZaISg1Y) · [Transcript](../transcripts/236-the-limitations-of-skeletons.md)

## In one sentence
Don't let convenient tools like realistic skeleton rigs decide what your animation can do; great animation goes beyond real-world limits.

## Key ideas
- CG characters are rigged with a "skeleton" made of bones and joints. In FK (forward kinematics) you set the shoulder, then the elbow, then the wrist, as real limbs move from the base out. In IK (inverse kinematics) you place the hand and the shoulder and elbow angles follow, which makes editing easier and helps plant limbs on the ground. Both are long-standing fundamentals.
- A joint's range of motion is naturally limited to 180 degrees. Sakurai wants to bend joints further, past a straight arm, to give animation more oomph.
- Joints that bend backward make a skeleton harder to use; for example, IK joints may start bending the opposite way you'd expect.
- There are fixes, like adding joints or building out the skeleton. On Smash Bros. they couldn't go that far because of processing load and the need for animators with cartoon-focused sensibilities. He suggested it at the start of development, but it is still unsolved.
- Pushing beyond common sense is what makes animation great; he wants work that breaks from convention.
- The lesson goes beyond skeletons. If you're too tied to convenient systems, it's hard to step away from them, and you set mental limits on what's possible. Creation is about freedom, so push your ideas past those perceived limits.

## Do this
- In key attack poses, try extending limbs past straight for extra oomph, and check whether your rig allows it.
- If your style needs cartoon exaggeration, design the rig for it at the start of development (extra joints, an extended skeleton) and budget for the processing load.
- Brief or hire animators with cartoon sensibilities when the style calls for exaggeration.
- Use IK for ground contact and easier editing, but watch for joints flipping the wrong way when you extend their range.
- Regularly ask which "impossible" ideas are only limits of a convenient tool.

## Avoid
- Letting a standard tool's convenience set the limits of what you think is possible.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Bros. — skeleton rigs:** Sakurai proposed exaggerated joints that bend past straight at the start of development, but processing load and a shortage of cartoon-minded animators left it unsolved.

## Quote
> In any case, I'd say that pushing beyond common sense is what gives animation its greatness. (02:21)
