# 198 · Competition and Abundance

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fWWwELXIAEw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/198-competition-and-abundance.md)

## In one sentence
If you release games commercially, treat every game players could spend their hours on, new or old, as your competition, and let gratitude for that abundance fuel your work.

## Key ideas
- More people than ever make games, for work or as a hobby. Sakurai says there is no harm in quitting if you want to, because plenty of others are "raring to create." He counts himself among them.
- The competition between individuals and teams is fierce. Games take many hours to play through, so every developer is competing for the time players put in.
- Making a game you personally find fun is fine, but a commercial release means facing the competition. You compete with past titles as well as the latest ones, and subscription services make that competition with the past unavoidable.
- From the player's side this abundance is wonderful. You can choose freely, play only what you want, and pick the best games in each genre you like. Those games exist only because someone made them.
- Sakurai knows how hard development is, so he is deeply grateful for every game. Games and services take far more labor than people think, and he is amazed at how little we pay for that enjoyment.
- Many creators were moved by other games and started making their own. Something one person struggled to create can bring another person joy. The worlds we have exist because people braved the competition and supported each other.
- Making games himself gave Sakurai a deeper understanding of why games are fun.

## Do this
- Before you commit to a commercial project, list the games your target player could spend the same hours on: current releases, older titles and subscription-catalog games. Write down why that player would pick yours instead.
- Measure your game against the best games in its genre, because players with plenty of choice pick from the top of each genre.
- When you feel like giving up, remember the field will carry on without you. Keep making games because you want to, and use the work to deepen your sense of why games are fun.

## Avoid
- Assuming that because you think your game is fun it will find players. A commercial release competes with every game, old or new, that wants the same player time.

## Examples from the episode
- **Subscription services:** they put older catalog titles right next to new releases, so a new game is forced to compete with the past.

## Quote
> Remember, you're not only competing against the latest titles, but those created in the past as well. (01:09)
