# 205 · Making the Imaginary Feel Real

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bVEPp5n-ysE) · [Transcript](../transcripts/205-making-the-imaginary-feel-real.md)

## In one sentence
A game world is a massless mirage, so control every asset's texture, response to light, weight, use and placement, along with invisible things like airflow, until the world feels real.

## Key ideas
- Modern game worlds can look better than reality, but to a creator they are "mirages. Just decoration." Without collision, things pass straight through the terrain. Without programming, a dropped object won't even fall. They are just polygons, "flavorless and without mass."
- Players don't notice this while playing, because visuals, audio, programming, parameters and other parts work together to create a sense of authenticity. Sakurai calls all of this "expression." He filed the episode under Graphics but says it applies to every discipline.
- Graphics artists especially should keep this in mind. When making a table with things on it, don't stop at something that merely looks like a table. Consider and control its texture, how it catches the light, its weight, how it is used and where it is placed.
- A regular table and a miniature table have the same shape. Presentation and arrangement are what decide whether the world feels real.
- Look at the big picture, and "try to make the imaginary feel real."
- Airflow is easy to overlook because we normally can't see it. In Smash Bros., environmental wind makes things flutter, and explosions create slight gusts of wind around them.

## Do this
- For each prop, decide more than its shape: set its texture, how it catches light, how heavy it should feel, what it is used for and where it sits, then check those choices in context.
- Use presentation and arrangement (lighting, surroundings, placement) to establish scale, so a full-size object can't be mistaken for a miniature.
- Add environmental wind so cloth, foliage and loose objects flutter, and add small gusts around explosions.
- Review scenes as a whole, with visuals, audio, physics and parameters together, instead of approving assets one at a time.

## Avoid
- Modeling something that only looks like the object, without considering its mass, its response to light or its context.
- Forgetting invisible forces such as airflow.

## Examples from the episode
- **A table with things on it:** to feel real it needs texture, light response, weight, usage and positioning, not just a table shape. A miniature table has the same shape and differs only in presentation.
- **Smash Bros. — wind:** environmental wind makes objects flutter, and slight gusts appear near explosions.

## Quote
> Take a look at the big picture, and try to make the imaginary feel real. (01:55)
