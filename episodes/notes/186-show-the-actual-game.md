# 186 · Show the Actual Game!

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-4oPgNbnJCI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/186-show-the-actual-game.md)

## In one sentence
Every game you sell needs a trailer, and that trailer should get to real gameplay as fast as possible, not spend its opening on logos, cinematics and story.

## Key ideas
- If you're selling a game, a trailer that actually shows the game in action is really important. Nearly every game has a promotional video now, and Sakurai frankly won't buy one without a trailer unless there's a very good reason. Treat making one as an absolutely essential step.
- A common failure is a trailer whose opening movie or character intros keep going, so it takes forever to reach actual gameplay. Some trailers, like ones on Steam pages, show no gameplay footage at all. Please don't do that.
- Popular series and famous creators can sometimes get by with only a teaser. If that isn't you, and especially for a new IP, and even more for an indie title, show gameplay as soon as possible.
- So many promos open with logos or long introductions that Sakurai has almost made a habit of skipping the start of trailers. A trailer exists to show what the game is about.
- Story and world can come later. However much work you put in, a trailer will never be on the level of a film, so hurry up and show what makes your game appealing.
- Another exception: opening on footage that clearly illustrates the game's systems, or on a tech demo that truthfully shows how the game will run, may be fine. Either way, don't beat around the bush.
- As he's said before, presentations are all about speed. Get to the point quickly so people understand quickly.

## Do this
- Treat a gameplay trailer as a required deliverable for any game you sell, including the one on your store page.
- Cut logos, long opening movies and character intros from the start of the trailer so actual gameplay appears as soon as possible.
- Open with what makes the game appealing and save story and world details for later in the trailer or for other materials.
- If the trailer doesn't open on raw gameplay, open on footage that clearly shows the game's systems, or an honest tech demo of how the game really runs.
- Watch your trailer as a stranger would, and trim anything that delays showing the game.

## Avoid
- Store-page trailers with no gameplay footage at all.
- Long cinematic intros before any play is shown.
- Relying on a teaser alone for a new IP or an indie game.
- Trying to compete with films on cinematics instead of showing your game's appeal.
