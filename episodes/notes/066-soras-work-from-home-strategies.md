# 066 · Sora's Work-From-Home Strategies

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXudgEHF78M) · [Transcript](../transcripts/066-soras-work-from-home-strategies.md)

## In one sentence
When a team moves to remote work, survey everyone early and quickly buy what they lack (desks, chairs, reference monitors), because most problems can be fixed with equipment, though productivity will still drop.

## Key ideas
- Because of COVID-19, almost all ongoing Smash Bros. Ultimate development went remote in April 2020. That was hard for such a secretive project, but it was necessary, so they moved quickly.
- Soon after the switch, Sakurai surveyed the whole team. **Positives:** no commute, better room temperature and eating habits, no waiting for the restroom.
- **Common problems:** no proper desk or chair (some people sat on the floor at low tables); no reference monitor, meaning a standard TV for reference use; slow or unreliable internet (slow apartment connections, and encryption slowing everything down); no in-person playtesting, which hit the playtesting team hard and moved Sakurai's daily lunchtime test matches online; and too little social contact, which was hard to avoid.
- Many issues could be solved by buying equipment, and the lack of a desk and chair would surely cause health problems. Sakurai set up a supplementary budget through Sora Ltd.: any Smash team member could request what they needed. They sent a product page (e.g. from Amazon), Sora ordered it and had it delivered, and they kept it with no returns needed. The purchases covered desks, chairs, monitors, monitor arms, room dividers, webcams, headsets, HDMI and other switchers, side tables and soundproof mats.
- The team mixed Bandai Namco Studios, other hired developers and temporary staff (Sakurai himself was technically temporary, contracted through Sora). If each company had bought its own equipment, the procedures, applications and approvals would have eaten up time, so Sora paid for everyone. It worked out great, and everyone was happy.
- Remote work continued to the end of development. They were lucky to already be on DLC; pandemic delays are real. His impression as director is that productivity fell by about 30%, but you have to stay flexible and find a new way forward.

## Do this
- Survey the whole team shortly after any big change in how you work, and ask what's working and what isn't.
- Make sure every remote worker has a proper desk and chair to avoid health problems, plus a standard reference TV or monitor for checking output.
- Set up a fast equipment process: staff send a product link, one central budget buys it and ships it to their home, and they keep it, which skips approval chains spread across several companies.
- Plan in advance for losing in-person playtests (move regular test matches online) and for slowdowns from home internet and encryption.
- When scheduling remote work, assume a productivity drop (Sakurai estimated about 30%).

## Avoid
- Letting each partner company run its own purchasing and approvals for remote equipment, which eats time.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — remote development from April 2020:** a team survey, Sora's supplementary equipment budget, and working remotely through the DLC phase to the end of development.
- **Sakurai's daily lunchtime test matches:** moved online once the team was remote.

## Quote
> But just like with every situation, you need to be as flexible as you can, and ready to find a new way forward. (03:58)
