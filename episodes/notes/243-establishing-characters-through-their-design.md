# 243 · Establishing Characters Through Their Design

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PDjdquhVHU4) · [Transcript](../transcripts/243-establishing-characters-through-their-design.md)

## In one sentence
Establish a character with an exaggerated, readable silhouette and strong poses, and make sure everyone working on them understands the character's core essence and pushes it further.

## Key ideas
- The Mii Fighter Nakoruru costume shown to Sakurai during Smash Bros. Ultimate development was based on her look in The King of Fighters XIV and matched it, but it wasn't quite right. The revised costume was clearly Nakoruru. It didn't just copy her classic design; it kept newer features too, such as the patterns in the red trim.
- Nakoruru first appeared in 1993's Samurai Shodown and was hugely popular. Her form is emphasized and dynamic, especially her hair, ribbon, gloves and legs, which gives her a clear silhouette.
- Sonic is similar: a large head and skinny arms and legs with just the right amount of exaggeration, which marks him as his own character.
- Sakurai knows several ways to make an imaginary character look "cool" but finds them hard to explain. The one he'd stress most is the silhouette: the form should be exaggerated even in solid black. If it's ambiguous, unreadable or hard to tell apart from other characters, it isn't good enough.
- Poses work the same way. Depending on how they're posed, the same character can look and feel completely different.
- Always be aware of how well established your characters are. The people assigned to a character need to understand its core essence and draw it out further. One person can't do this alone; the whole team has to make the characters shine.

## Do this
- Fill each character in solid black and check that the silhouette is exaggerated, readable and distinct from everyone else in the cast.
- Exaggerate signature features (hair, ribbons, gloves, head size, limb thickness) instead of rendering them at realistic proportions.
- Review each character's key poses and rework any that don't express the character's personality.
- When adapting an existing character, combine the classic design with selected newer details rather than copying one source version literally.
- Write a short statement of each character's core essence and share it with everyone who works on that character.

## Avoid
- Silhouettes that are ambiguous or hard to tell apart from other characters.
- Matching a reference design accurately but missing what makes the character feel like themselves.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Bros. Ultimate — Mii Fighter Nakoruru costume:** the first version matched her KOF XIV look but felt off; the revision captured her and added newer details like the red-trim patterns.
- **Samurai Shodown (1993) — Nakoruru:** emphasized hair, ribbon, gloves and legs make a clear silhouette.
- **Sonic:** a large head and skinny but well-exaggerated limbs.

## Quote
> Their form should be exaggerated, even when they're just solid black. If the silhouette is ambiguous and unreadable, or impossible to distinguish from other characters, it's not good enough. (01:38)
