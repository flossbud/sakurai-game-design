# 001 · About This Channel

**Category:** Special · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7M-DnOIeasA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/001-about-this-channel.md)

## In one sentence
Use outside design lessons as a small boost on top of your own experience, skill and way of thinking, and keep the shared goal in view: making games a little more fun.

## Key ideas
- The channel's goal is to "help make games around the world a little more fun." It mainly targets game developers and people who want to become one, but it is meant to work for viewers with no development experience too.
- Why YouTube: lectures at game schools reach only so many people, and conferences like GDC or CEDEC are good for technical topics but "aren't always the best forums for discussing game design theory." A channel reaches more people than a lecture, a conference or a book.
- He calls the lessons a "boost." The real foundation is the viewer's own experience, skill and way of thinking, and "your mileage may vary."
- He covers very basic concepts on purpose ("keeping things simple is for the best"), including things the industry treats as common knowledge. He avoids deep technical detail because every dev environment is different and trade secrets must be kept.
- Format: episodes of 2–5 minutes, because "the longer I talk, the harder it'll be to retain everything." Each video has Japanese and English versions, footage comes mostly from classic games, and he may show Smash Bros. development builds and his own design documents (cleared with Nintendo, though Nintendo isn't involved in the channel).
- There are no mid-video ads. He pays editors and translators, so the channel loses money the longer it runs, and he calls it "an investment in the game industry." He hopes it leads to more game creators and therefore more fun games.
- Players benefit too: learning the mechanics behind games can deepen their enjoyment.

## Do this
- When teaching or briefing design ideas, break them into bite-sized units (he targets 2–5 minutes per topic), because people retain less the longer you talk.
- Explain the basics, including what your field treats as common knowledge, so newcomers can follow.
- Use any design advice, this series included, to sharpen judgment you build by making and playing games. Don't treat it as a substitute for that judgment.
