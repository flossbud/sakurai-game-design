# 007 · Game Development Isn't a Game

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jlFkxfkICO8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/007-game-development-isnt-a-game.md)

## In one sentence
Approach game development as a professional job: set your personal taste aside, find the fun in whatever genre or goal you're given, and never lose sight of entertaining players.

## Key ideas
- Most developers would say they make games "Because I like games," and that's how it should be. His own answer is "Because that's what I'm best at." Liking games, or liking to make them, ranks lower for him.
- "The job of a game developer is to create games, but the work itself is not a game." Hobby projects are one thing. Once money, sales and numbers are involved, it's a job like any other. Indie developers become professionals too once they earn money, and "being a pro means being light on your feet and ready to respond to demand."
- Many developers work on games that aren't to their taste. His example is an artist who loves cute things texturing lifelike zombies and roaches day after day. The job still has to be done well, and it helps to work out what makes the work interesting and rewarding.
- Even directors and game designers who create ideas from scratch have no guarantee of making what they personally want.
- He sets his preferences aside. He's an experienced gamer but made Kirby's Dream Land for newcomers, and he made Meteos though he isn't good at block puzzles. He treats every project as "an assignment in which I create a game to fit a certain genre or goal," and still finds the fun in it. He enjoys making his games and often plays them after release.
- "Once you understand what makes games fun, you'll be able to create all kinds of experiences regardless of your own preferences."
- Every developer shares one goal: entertain the players. When you feel lost, come back to it. The desire to entertain supplies the motivation to keep going.

## Do this
- At the start of each project, write down the genre, goal and audience it has to fit, and design to that brief rather than to your own taste.
- On tasks you don't enjoy, name one thing that makes the work interesting or rewarding, and do the task well anyway.
- When you're stuck or unmotivated, ask "How does this entertain the player?" and let the answer decide.
- Study what makes games fun across genres, including ones you're weak at, so your range doesn't depend on what you like.

## Avoid
- Treating paid development like a hobby where you only make what you want.
- Designing games for yourself instead of for the assignment and its players.

## Examples from the episode
- **Kirby's Dream Land:** made for newcomers by an experienced gamer.
- **Meteos:** made by a director who isn't good at block puzzles.
- **An artist who loves cute things:** texturing zombies and roaches every day, and still has to do it well.
