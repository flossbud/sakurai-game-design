# 211 · Advancing Text

**Category:** UI · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CkXSIUX5l_s) · [Transcript](../transcripts/211-advancing-text.md)

## In one sentence
Let players get through text and dialogue at their own, usually fast, pace, with line-by-line skipping in cutscenes or voiced lines they can hear while playing, because modern players want to enjoy as much as possible in as little time as possible.

## Key ideas
- People generally read on-screen text and subtitles quickly, and zipping through game text feels good. Sakurai demonstrates his own reading speed: "a bit fast, but I can understand what's going on."
- Cutscenes with detailed performances can't move at the pace you want. He compares it to the difference between turning the pages of a book and watching a film.
- As a player he wants these scenes to allow as much fast-forwarding as possible. Some games let you skip dialogue one line at a time, jumping straight to the start of the next line. "It's a real breeze."
- He recommends making text and dialogue easy to speed through, or letting players listen to dialogue while they keep playing.
- Subscription services and YouTube offer endless video, and some let viewers raise the playback speed, which many people surely use. You want people to slow down and appreciate the performances you staged, "And yet, everyone wants to enjoy as much as possible in as little time as they can." Supporting both lets each player choose to take it slow or go faster.
- He has noticed that most games made outside Japan ship with subtitles off by default, so the idea may be seen differently abroad. He raises it to build awareness.
- His closing question: are slowly advancing scenes really suitable today?

## Do this
- Let one button press finish the current text box and move straight to the next, so fast readers can zip through.
- In voiced cutscenes, add a skip that jumps to the start of the next line, in addition to a skip for the whole scene.
- Where you can, deliver dialogue as voiced lines during gameplay so players can listen without stopping.
- Playtest text pacing with fast readers, and treat any scene that can't be sped up as something that needs a reason.

## Avoid
- Forcing every player through text or cutscenes at one fixed, slow pace.

## Examples from the episode
- **Line-by-line dialogue skip (games not named):** fast-forwards to the start of the next line, which Sakurai calls "a real breeze."
- **Video platforms' playback-speed setting:** evidence that audiences now expect to choose their own speed.

## Quote
> Hence, by making dialogue skippable as much as possible, you may just be making a great number of players happy. (02:13)
