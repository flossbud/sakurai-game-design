# 017 · Just Let Them Play!

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PlbwQ_rntCM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/017-just-let-them-play.md)

## In one sentence
Make players understand why your game is fun within the first three minutes by getting to real gameplay fast, in both your trailers and your opening, and saving long story and CG for later.

## Key ideas
- From the arcade world: "players should understand why a game is fun in the first three minutes." People decide quickly whether a game is fun, and if you make them wait too long they may put it down.
- Trailers: many open with story sequences and take a long time to reach gameplay footage, so he has gotten in the habit of skipping ahead. "Save the high-end CG movies and openings for later." People won't buy a game if they can't tell what kind of game it is.
- In the game itself, long prologues only delay players from playing. "A game that starts slow is often a game that's starting on the wrong foot." Answer "What's the actual game like?" by jumping into gameplay early. Interesting visuals can still make an impression on their own.
- RPGs need some introduction, but gameplay should still come first. Drop the player into "a slice of the action that takes place later chronologically," or let them fight a battle as characters other than the protagonists.
- It depends on the game. Games with well-known mechanics, blockbusters, and games with prominent visual or story elements need their own careful approach to what they show.
- Other visual media can show video too, so give players a taste of "the fun that only games can offer."
- Tutorials should be fun too. Weave them naturally into the main game rather than making them obvious practice.

## Do this
- Time your opening. A first-time player should be playing, and should understand the core fun, within three minutes of starting.
- Cut or postpone long prologues. If you need story context, open with a playable slice from later in the timeline, or a battle with non-protagonist characters.
- Put real gameplay near the start of trailers, and move CG movies and story later.
- Build tutorials into actual play instead of separate, obvious practice sections.

## Avoid
- Trailers that open with long story sequences before any gameplay.
- Slow starts and long prologues.
- Leaving players asking "What's the actual game like?"

## Examples from the episode
- **Arcade games — first three minutes:** the source of the idea that the fun must be clear immediately.
- **Game trailers on online storefronts:** often open with story, so he skips ahead.
- **RPG openings (suggested structure):** start with a later slice of the story, or with a battle as other characters.

## Quote
> A game that starts slow is often a game that's starting on the wrong foot. (00:46)
