# 090 · Supervising Art Through Retouches

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s08NT-TVCvU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/090-supervising-art-through-retouches.md)

## In one sentence
When supervising art, show rather than tell: a 5–10 minute retouch of the artist's own piece gets the direction across better than words, and then the artist does the real work of finishing it.

## Key ideas
- There's no better way to explain what you want from graphics than to show it. He shares background art he supervised, each shown as the original next to his retouch.
- The cave background for sending Spirits exploring had a color palette that was too understated, so he made a retouched example. He also retouched the background for the boss fight against Marx. For a World of Light pathway he wanted more contrast and a better sense of heat, but kept the magma from getting too bright so it wouldn't be hard on the eyes.
- Changes like these are hard to explain in words. A director can't retouch every piece of art, and no amount of time would be enough. But each of these took only about five minutes, ten at most, which is more efficient than explaining verbally.
- His cave retouch in Photoshop: duplicate the layer and boost the contrast, tweak the hue, erase the hue-shifted layer wherever light would hit (the purple layer takes on a reflective glow), then adjust the colors to work together. Adding a complex color mesh to a monochrome background improved the whole piece. He uses only a mouse, no drawing tablet.
- Retouches are just examples. The artists still have to carry out the idea themselves, and it's best for everyone when the art is approvable on the first try.
- Supervision is really charting the creative course. Getting a piece to release quality takes serious work from the artist.

## Do this
- When art feedback is hard to put into words (palette, contrast, mood, lighting), spend 5–10 minutes on a rough retouch or paintover of the artist's piece and send it side by side with the original.
- To enrich a flat or monochrome background, try his sequence: duplicate the layer, boost contrast, shift the hue, erase the shifted layer where light would hit, then harmonize the colors.
- When adding heat or glow (magma, lava, fire), raise contrast but cap the brightness of the glowing areas so they aren't hard on the eyes.
- Label retouches as direction, not final art, and leave the release-quality execution to the artist.
- Save retouches for pieces where words fail. You can't retouch everything.

## Avoid
- Long verbal descriptions of visual changes that a quick retouch could show.
- The director trying to retouch every asset. There will never be enough time.
- Bright glowing elements that strain players' eyes.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Spirits exploration cave:** an understated palette, retouched with contrast, a hue shift and a reflective glow.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Marx boss-fight background:** original vs. retouch.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — World of Light pathway:** more contrast and heat, with the magma kept from being too bright.

## Quote
> When supervising a game's graphics, there's no better way to explain what you want than by just showing it. (00:03)
