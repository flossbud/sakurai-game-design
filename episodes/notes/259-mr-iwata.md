# 259 · Mr. Iwata

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o2wYKkqhkG0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/259-mr-iwata.md)

## In one sentence
Lead the way Sakurai remembers Satoru Iwata leading: trust creators with their content, step in personally when problems arise, meet trouble with calm logic instead of temper, and invest in direct, one-on-one communication.

## Key ideas
- This is the final Grab Bag episode and a short record of Iwata's life from Sakurai's point of view. They first met when Iwata, then HAL Laboratory's chief of development and about 10 years Sakurai's senior, interviewed him. Two things stood out: how fast and forcefully he typed his notes, and his carefree, genuine smile. He was really enjoying an interview with a complete stranger.
- At HAL, Sakurai barely worked with Iwata directly. Iwata was an extraordinary programmer in a management and leadership role, but whenever problems arose he jumped in to help the teams. He never told Sakurai how to approach the content of his games; Sakurai thinks he trusted him to decide.
- Around the end of development on the first Kirby game, HAL entered settlement proceedings because it couldn't pay its debts. Iwata became president and rebuilt the company from the ground up. Salaries were never stopped, and there were even small bonuses. For Kirby's Adventure, the second game by a new employee, the team pulled in people from entirely different teams to finish it, a risky decision that produced something great.
- The only time Iwata wrote code for a Sakurai game was the prototype that became Smash Bros. for the Nintendo 64, Dragon King: The Fighting Game (plus a separate adventure game). He used days off to help and reportedly enjoyed it. He also picked the "Brothers" in Super Smash Bros., in a naming meeting with Shigesato Itoi: the characters aren't brothers, but the word adds the nuance that they're friends settling a little disagreement, not simply fighting.
- In Melee's final stretch, bugs in the code stalled progress. Even with correct parameters, code mistakes mean almost no amount of adjustment gets the result you want, and that was eating Sakurai's time as the one making adjustments. Iwata, by then head of Nintendo's corporate planning division, worked directly with the programmers to find the problems, despite how hard it is to read someone else's code. Without him, Sakurai doubts Melee would have released before the end of 2001.
- Iwata was the smartest person Sakurai knew: he used logic rather than instinct to find the best way forward, was a constant learner, thought about why people think the way they do, and stayed calm and offered analysis or suggestions where most people would lose their temper. As Nintendo CEO, a surprising pick in a company traditionally run by the Yamauchi family, he created Iwata Asks and made Nintendo Directs possible. He valued communication: one-on-ones with employees from his HAL presidency on, visits to several companies whenever he was in Tokyo, and invitation emails written by himself, not his assistant.
- After surgery for a bile duct tumor in 2014, his health declined. In January 2015, right after Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U, he visited Sakurai before a trip to Seattle, and Sakurai drove him the hour and a half to Narita Airport; Iwata was happy he could eat meat again. That was their last conversation; he passed away that July. Sakurai calls him the person who understood him best, and says that without meeting him he'd probably be doing something completely different. Iwata also loved doing the comedy-style skits in Nintendo Directs.

## Do this
- If you lead creators, let them own the content decisions of their games, but step in personally to help whenever a team hits problems.
- When tuning stops producing the results you expect, suspect the code: get strong programmers to hunt for bugs directly, because no parameter adjustment can fix code mistakes.
- Hold regular one-on-one conversations with team members.
- When things go wrong, respond with calm analysis and suggestions instead of anger.
- When naming a game, pick words for the nuance they carry about its tone, the way "Brothers" turned a fighting game into friends settling a disagreement.
- Send important invitations and messages yourself rather than through an assistant.

## Avoid
- Trying to tune parameters on top of buggy code.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. (Nintendo 64) — the title:** Iwata chose "Brothers" so the fighting reads as friends settling a little disagreement.
- **Dragon King: The Fighting Game — prototype:** the only Sakurai game Iwata wrote code for, on his days off.
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee — final stretch:** Iwata debugged the code alongside the programmers so it could ship before the end of 2001.
- **Kirby's Adventure — HAL's rebuilding:** staff pulled in from other teams to finish the game.
- **Iwata Asks and Nintendo Directs:** new ways of making development visible to the public.

## Quote
> In situations where most people would lose their temper or get offended, he would remain calm and offer analysis or suggestions instead. (05:49)
