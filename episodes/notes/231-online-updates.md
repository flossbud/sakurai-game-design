# 231 · Online Updates

**Category:** Design Specifics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e89D_CG9P0g) · [Transcript](../transcripts/231-online-updates.md)

## In one sentence
Treat post-launch patches as a bonus, not a plan: modern games will ship with bugs that only the market finds, but no patch helps once players have given up, so ship in the best shape you can.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai is grateful for updates and the companies that make them. Ideally a game is in perfect shape at release, but that needs you to know the finished game and how to build it in advance, which is all but impossible for modern titles.
- 1) Modern games are too complex. AAA games are 100, 1,000 or even 10,000 times more complex than in the past, and that much data creates instability you wouldn't expect. Just getting it to work is a miracle.
- 2) You can't play the finished game during development. Every fix restarts testing from scratch, so with bug fixes and balancing, developers get almost no time with the final product.
- 3) The market is the best bug tester. A bug that one in a thousand people will see is unlikely to show up in testing, but after launch that person can upload a video. Debug conditions keep changing, so re-checks can miss even bugs that are easy to reproduce once known. The same goes for game balance.
- 4) Updates are a free bonus. Fatal, crashing bugs should be fixed, but balance tweaks and quality-of-life features are extras. They keep the director and key staff tied up, and for a one-off physical release the developers kept their promise at launch, so starting the next game is often more effective. Still, nobody should criticize developers for improving a released game.
- Games-as-a-service titles may be different.
- From the player's side, no patch matters if players have already given up on the game, so aim for the best condition from day one.

## Do this
- Set release quality as if no patch will come, because players who quit at launch won't return for an update.
- Lock content early enough that the team can play a near-final build, since every late fix restarts testing from scratch.
- Triage post-launch work: fix crashing and fatal bugs as a must; treat balance and quality-of-life changes as optional, weighed against freeing the director and key staff for the next project.
- After launch, watch player videos and reports as your biggest test pass, especially for rare (one-in-a-thousand) bugs and balance problems.

## Avoid
- Shipping in rough shape on the assumption that patches will fix it later.

## Examples from the episode
- **A one-in-a-thousand bug:** unlikely to appear in testing, but after launch the one player who hits it can upload a video for everyone to see.

## Quote
> no matter how much you improve your game through patches, it won't mean anything if your players have already given up on the game. (03:31)
