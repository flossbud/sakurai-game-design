# 242 · Being Kind to Beginners

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R7rd4-ua2AI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/242-being-kind-to-beginners.md)

## In one sentence
Lower the barrier to entry for new players from the first moment until they're hooked, without over-explaining, because a beginner who gets crushed or lectured will simply quit.

## Key ideas
- In Japanese arcades, fighting-game cabinets face each other, so you can't see your opponent. Sakurai once won 50 matches in a row, so he wasn't bad at fighting games (though he says he is now).
- In The King of Fighters '95, a challenger joined. Playing Takuma Sakazaki, he landed a flashy string: jump, strong punch, uppercut, then three special moves in a row (Ko-Ou Ken, Hien Shippuu Kyaku, Zanretsuken). An on-screen note says it's not technically a combo.
- The opponent wasn't fighting back. Peeking over, he found an ordinary couple, with the girlfriend playing. The boyfriend took over for the last round and Sakurai went easy, but it was surely obvious. He imagines they'd dropped by just to try a new game for fun, and he wiped the floor with them. Since then he has mostly stopped playing strangers in fighting games.
- Going easy on someone isn't impolite unless they want you to go all out. There's no point in playing if you're not having fun.
- So be kind when introducing new players to a game. In competitive and single-player games alike, lower the barrier to entry from the start until players are fully drawn in.
- The hard part: hold their hand too much, so they feel pulled along and everyone keeps telling them how to play, and they won't enjoy it. Give them enough freedom to get into the game themselves.
- It's hard enough to get people to play a game at all. If someone joins you, be kind if you don't want them to give up on it.

## Do this
- Walk through the path from first boot until a player is fully drawn in, and remove barriers to entry along it, in both single-player and competitive modes.
- Design the first matches or encounters so a newcomer gets to act and have fun instead of being flattened before they understand the game.
- Limit tutorials and prompts so players aren't constantly told how to play; leave room for them to find their own way in.
- When playing with or playtesting alongside a newcomer, ease off unless they clearly want you to go all out.

## Avoid
- Over-hand-holding that makes new players feel dragged along.
- Crushing someone who has just come to try the game for fun.

## Examples from the episode
- **The King of Fighters '95 — arcade match:** Sakurai's flashy Takuma Sakazaki string against a couple who were only trying the game; afterward he mostly stopped playing strangers.

## Quote
> Ultimately, there's no point in playing games if you're not having fun. As such, it's important to be kind when introducing new players to a title. (01:50)
