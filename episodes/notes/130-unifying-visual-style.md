# 130 · Unifying Visual Style

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BLzEMMcwUKk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/130-unifying-visual-style.md)

## In one sentence
Unify your game's look by starting from a few image boards by your best artist and having everyone, whatever their personal specialty, build to that single visual concept.

## Key ideas
- Professional developers must handle a variety of tasks regardless of their personal strengths. Artists on one team must produce the same visual style, so someone great at realistic, musclebound characters may have to make more fanciful art.
- At a company running several projects you're more likely to land in a genre you're good at, but sometimes there's no choice. Most artists are very good at matching a set style, and with fierce competition in art and games, that skill is needed.
- Teams often unify style with "image boards": art that shows the game's world, scenery, characters, items and more. Projects usually have only a few, made by an exceptional artist on the team. Using them as a base, the team builds models and nails down lighting, ironing out differences in skill and style and pointing everyone in the same direction.
- In Sakurai's games, Mario's overalls have often had a denim texture, because Smash Bros. mixes realistic and cartoony characters and needed clever ways to unify the visual style. Otherwise it might look like a fight between live action and animation.
- The more advanced a console's visual capabilities, the more it costs to unify style, but it's a necessary step, so you have to find a way.
- Game artbooks of concept art are great reference material; they also show how many people it takes to build an elaborate world.

## Do this
- Before mass production, have your strongest artist make a small set of image boards (world, scenery, characters, items), and use them as the reference for every model and the lighting.
- When combining characters drawn in different styles (realistic vs. cartoony), add shared material detail to bridge them, for example real-world fabric textures like denim on a cartoony character's clothes.
- Expect every artist to work outside their specialty to match the set style, and review their assets against the image boards.
- Budget explicitly for style unification; it costs more the higher your visual fidelity.
- Study game artbooks and concept art as reference.

## Avoid
- Letting each artist work in their own preferred style, so characters look like they come from different media ("live action and animation").

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — Mario's overalls:** given a denim texture so cartoony Mario sits better beside realistic fighters.
- **Game artbooks:** collections of concept art that are great reference and show how many people build a world.

## Quote
> Start with a singular visual concept, then execute on that concept with skilled art. That's the way that countless games come together. (02:24)
