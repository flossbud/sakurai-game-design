# 059 · Kirby Air Ride

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bRDAYgRns4E) · [Transcript](../transcripts/059-kirby-air-ride.md)

## In one sentence
Build a game on one analyzed piece of fun, widen the range of play styles instead of rewarding a single perfect line, and when delegated work stalls, rebuild it on clear specs with variety driven by parameters.

## Key ideas
- **Context:** the Kirby anime began airing in Japan in October 2001, just before Smash Bros. Melee came out. Sakurai was chief director of the Kirby series, which included overseeing the anime, while Melee was at the height of development. A stronger game lineup was needed to coincide with the anime, and he judged a racing game quicker to make than a traditional action game. The other titles were the Nightmare in Dream Land port, Amazing Mirror (made by Flagship) and a GameCube Kirby that was cancelled.
- **Essence:** drifting is fun because of risk and reward: it's thrilling to lose control with walls in front of you, and it feels great to get through. Just making drifting faster wouldn't be enough. Charging up after braking into a drift gives a sense of "squeeze and release."
- **Not about perfection:** shaving tenths of a second off a time has game essence, but he wanted a broader design where players don't converge on one method. City Trial shows this best: a huge sandbox where different power-ups and stadiums make every session unique. The goal was to expand the possible play types.
- **Checklist:** Air Ride introduced his Checklist feature, before PS3 trophies and Xbox achievements. Completing an objective shows the clear conditions for the adjacent squares, which gives a sense of accomplishment even in casual play.
- Kirby Air Ride is unrelated to the cancelled N64 Air Ride, whose essence was closer to a snowboarding game, though that project may have sparked ideas about what would or wouldn't work.
- **Development:** as chief director he tried to leave work to the people responsible, but on Air Ride he was involved in almost everything, giving very specific feedback on graphics and sound to nail the Kirby "feel." After a year of delegated development, not one Warp Star worked properly. At the lead programmer's request he returned as director, and they rebuilt the game from scratch on new specs, finishing in three and a half months. Courses and machines were mostly done already, and the exhausted team stuck with him to the end.
- **Parameters:** every machine feels totally different, but all share the same underlying behavior. Star and bike types differ; everything else is parameters. The reworked code let them change a machine's character by tweaking a few numbers, and Sakurai wrote all the parameters and the design specs before them. With Air Ride completed and the anime over, he left HAL Laboratory and went independent.

## Do this
- Take the one action you think is fun, name its risk and reward, and add a squeeze-and-release layer (hold to charge during the risky part, release for the payoff) rather than just making it faster.
- Add at least one mode built for variety rather than perfection, such as a sandbox with random power-ups and events, so players don't converge on a single optimal method.
- Build a checklist grid where completing an objective reveals the conditions of the adjacent squares, giving casual players a steady sense of accomplishment.
- Make all your vehicles or characters run on one shared behavior and express their differences as parameter sets, so new feel comes from tweaking numbers rather than new code.
- If delegated core gameplay is still not working after a long stretch, step in, write fresh specs and rebuild the game logic on the existing assets.

## Avoid
- Designing only around perfection and time-shaving, so every player converges on the same method.
- Making drifting merely faster instead of giving it a charge-and-release payoff.
- Leaving the core gameplay delegated while it stalls (a year in, no Warp Star worked).

## Examples from the episode
- **Kirby Air Ride — charge drifting:** brake into a drift to charge, then release for "squeeze and release."
- **Kirby Air Ride — City Trial:** a large sandbox with varying power-ups and stadiums, so every session is different.
- **Kirby Air Ride — Checklist:** an achievement grid from before console trophies; clearing a square reveals the conditions of its neighbors.
- **Kirby Air Ride — machines:** they feel completely different but differ only by parameters (apart from star vs. bike types).
- **Air Ride (cancelled N64 version):** a different essence, closer to snowboarding.
