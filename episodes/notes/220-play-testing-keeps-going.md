# 220 · Play Testing Keeps Going

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TayNJ4603Hc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/220-play-testing-keeps-going.md)

## In one sentence
Rely on dedicated play testers, and on online match data where you have it, to tune balance, while the director steps in mainly to protect each character's individuality.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai plays his own games far less than players do. Some people have put hundreds, thousands or even tens of thousands of hours into his competitive titles. ("Be sure to play other games, too!")
- Nobody is better at a game in development than its play testers, whose job is to keep playing. Their opinions guide the balance between fighters.
- Up through Smash Bros. Melee, and in fact through Kirby Air Ride, Sakurai decided all parameter adjustments himself. Parameters meant not just strength but mobility and everything else. He played against teammates, judged who felt too strong, too weak or "not unique enough," and adjusted them. With short schedules and his director duties on top, "it took all I had just to finish."
- Smash Bros. Brawl added play testers, but only four, "exactly the number we needed for a Brawl mode match." They weren't tournament players or the most skilled. Online updates weren't possible until after Brawl, so its balance was fixed at release and no match data could be collected.
- After Kid Icarus: Uprising and Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U, Smash Bros. Ultimate got a much larger and more talented play testing team. Online matches and tournaments had also become popular, so their results could be used as reference. He thinks it would be hard to get much more balanced than that.
- His tuning approach is to make each fighter highly unique. Divided opinions among players are ideal and part of the fun. Looking at any one part alone might make the game seem unfair, but he accepts that for the sake of individuality.
- He's glad top tournament players use a wide range of fighters, but that isn't the only thing the team looks at, since some fighters are naturally better suited to multiplayer matches. During development he spends half an hour at lunch play testing multiplayer himself.
- He often wonders whether something is really balanced, but the testers are far more attuned to it and battle every day, so he trusts their analysis. When he does speak up, "it's most often because a fighter's individuality is being threatened."

## Do this
- Staff dedicated play testers whose job is to keep playing the in-development build, and feed their opinions into balance changes.
- When you can, use online match and tournament results as reference data for tuning.
- Tune every parameter (mobility and the rest, not just power), judging each character as too strong, too weak or not unique enough.
- As director, play the game yourself regularly (Sakurai plays about 30 minutes of multiplayer at lunch), but leave detailed balance judgments to the testers.
- Check balance in multiplayer matches as well as by top tournament usage.
- Step in on balance changes mainly when they threaten a character's individuality.

## Avoid
- Having the director set every parameter alone on top of directing. Sakurai says it took everything he had just to finish those games.
- Judging fairness from one part of the game in isolation, or from top tournament usage alone.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee / Kirby Air Ride:** Sakurai set all the parameters himself by playing against teammates.
- **Super Smash Bros. Brawl:** four play testers, no online updates and no match data, so balance was fixed at release.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate:** a much larger, more skilled testing team plus online and tournament results as reference.

## Quote
> When I do end up saying something, it's most often because a fighter's individuality is being threatened. (03:14)
