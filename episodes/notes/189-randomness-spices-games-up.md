# 189 · Randomness Spices Games Up

**Category:** Design Specifics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e9v_FwUGOwY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/189-randomness-spices-games-up.md)

## In one sentence
Use randomness to keep repeated play fresh and make players adapt, but never let luck alone decide a loss, keep it out of strict skill contests, and seed it so players can't manipulate it.

## Key ideas
- **Upside:** games repeat the same content over and over, and randomness makes them harder to get bored of. Games with static rules struggle to offer variety, but a bit of randomness makes each play go differently, which keeps it fresh and boosts the fun. Varying probabilities instead of fixing results adds flavor to strategy. Random elements can look like they undercut strategy, but it's the opposite: they challenge players to handle a range of situations.
- **Downside:** it isn't really fair. When luck decides the outcome, hard work can come to nothing, and a player who lost only because they were unlucky may struggle to accept it. Players need to feel, to some extent, that they're responsible for their own failure.
- RPGs sometimes make "luck" a stat you can raise, a tradition that goes back at least to Wizardry in 1981. That makes randomness a little easier to accept, but players often forget about it because it isn't a top-priority stat, or they don't want to spend precious resources on something so vague.
- Randomness fits poorly with sports and strict competitions. If you want a contest decided by skill alone, keep random elements out.
- Smash Bros. has been removing a lot of randomness from its fighters. Peach's Smash Attack used to give a random result, and now pressing up or down changes it. The Hero's command list is randomized every time it's opened, which they decided was okay because you still need skill to use each command well.
- Items in Smash are still markedly random. That shakes up the playing field and gives anyone a chance to come back, whatever their skill. Advanced players still use items better than beginners, but that's preferable to matches decided purely by skill, since the series is more about having fun and moving on to the next match than about winning.
- A caution: computers can only do things in a predetermined way, so they can't produce true randomness. Sometimes players can reset the game or perform certain actions to always get the result they want. To prevent that, factor an indefinite value such as the system's internal clock into random number generation.

## Do this
- Add randomness wherever repeated play is getting stale, and vary probabilities rather than fixing results, so players must adapt their strategy to new situations.
- Check that no loss is decided by luck alone. Give players ways to respond to random events so they feel their failures are their own.
- Where a random outcome feels unfair, put it under player control, the way pressing up or down now changes Peach's Smash Attack.
- Keep randomness that still rewards skill, such as a random set of commands that each take skill to use well.
- In casual or party play, use random items so players of any skill can come back. For strict competitive modes, remove randomness.
- Seed random number generation with an unpredictable value, such as the system clock, so players can't reset or repeat actions to force the result they want.

## Avoid
- Matches or fights where luck alone decides the outcome.
- Relying on a "luck" stat to make randomness acceptable, since players forget it or won't invest in it.
- Random elements in sports games or strict skill-based competitions.
- Predictable random number generation that players can manipulate by resetting.

## Examples from the episode
- **Wizardry (1981):** an early use of "luck" as a stat players can raise.
- **Smash Bros. — Peach's Smash Attack:** once random, now controllable by pressing up or down.
- **Smash Bros. — the Hero's command list:** randomized each time it opens, kept because using each command still takes skill.
- **Smash Bros. — items:** still markedly random, giving anyone a chance at a comeback.
