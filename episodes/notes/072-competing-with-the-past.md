# 072 · Competing with the Past

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EhcitspNMuI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/072-competing-with-the-past.md)

## In one sentence
Every new work competes with all past works for people's time, so offer something fresh that can't be found anywhere else, and keep that broad view of the competition.

## Key ideas
- Film may have it harder than games. Subscription services give viewers tens of thousands of movies and shows, quickly and cheaply, so every new title has to hold its own against countless older ones.
- For the viewer, time spent on something new is worth the same as time spent on something old. Meanwhile new creators, and so new titles, keep arriving, which makes the competition fierce.
- Games have it better for now. Even with ports of classics, old games rarely compete directly with new ones, because technology has advanced and many players aren't satisfied with old-school graphics and controls (though people who grew up with them may treasure them).
- Game scale has changed too. 1980s games were small pick-up-and-play experiences, so you can't compare their time with a two-hour film.
- In free-to-play, smartphone, and "competing for screen time" markets, the battle may be especially fierce.
- These markets thrive because so many creators want to work in them. To stand out you need something fresh that can't be found elsewhere, which is easier said than done.

## Do this
- When pitching a game, list what else competes for the same player's time: older games, ports and remasters, and other media. Write down what your game offers that none of them do.
- Widen your reference set beyond this year's releases, so you know what you're really up against.

## Avoid
- Assuming your only competition is other new releases.

## Examples from the episode
- **Subscription video services:** new films and shows compete directly with decades of back catalog.
- **Ports of classic games:** usually don't compete head-on with new games, because of advances in graphics and controls.
- **1980s games vs. a two-hour film:** short pick-up-and-play games can't be measured in time the same way.
- **His YouTube channel:** he expects the same past-vs-present competition applies there too.

## Quote
> If you really want to stand out from the crowd, you need to offer something fresh that can't be found elsewhere. (02:18)
