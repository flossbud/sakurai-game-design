# 219 · Mandatory Strategies and Strategic Balance

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U1b2OXdNcUY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/219-mandatory-strategies-and-strategic-balance.md)

## In one sentence
Choose deliberately where your encounters fall between authored, mandatory strategies and free-form approaches, based on your own game's systems rather than copying another game's answer.

## Key ideas
- Nintendo games in particular tend to define clear strategies for beating bosses, such as striking a foe's eye at the right moment and slashing it while it is stunned. The developers carefully craft these strategies.
- As a player, Sakurai would rather be free to choose how he fights. A strategy that feels mandatory is "almost like a tutorial." It shows that someone put thought into the battle, which is one reason those games are popular, but he doesn't want games telling him exactly how to play. It is still a valid way to make a game.
- In series like Castlevania you can fight with any weapon in any way, so "there are as many strategies as there are players." Compared with crafted strategies this can feel somewhat flavorless and doesn't tend to create push and pull. That is also a valid approach.
- Some games have an ideal strategy that players work out by trial and error. Others let players pick from many weapons based on their skill or the current enemy.
- Neither answer is more correct, but you need to settle on the experience you want. Don't base your design on another game. Learn what it can teach, then find the balance that fits your own game's systems.
- In The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, things feel less mandatory, possibly because the developers rethought traditional Zelda conventions.

## Do this
- For each boss or major encounter, decide in advance whether it will have one authored solution, an ideal strategy players find by trial and error, or many viable approaches (such as weapon choice by skill or matchup).
- If you author a mandatory strategy, treat it as a tutorial in disguise and design the fight around teaching it.
- If you allow free-form strategies, add push and pull to the fight on purpose so it doesn't feel flavorless.
- When you study a reference game's boss design, write down what you learned, then rebuild the balance from your own game's systems instead of copying its structure.

## Avoid
- Copying another game's strategy design wholesale instead of deriving one from your own systems.

## Examples from the episode
- **Nintendo boss fights (titles not named):** hit the eye at the right time, then slash the stunned foe, a carefully crafted, tutorial-like strategy.
- **Castlevania series:** any weapon, any way. It gives freedom but can feel flavorless and creates less push and pull.
- **The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild:** fights feel less mandatory, perhaps from rethinking Zelda conventions.

## Quote
> Don't simply reference another game and craft your design based on that— learn what there is to learn, then find the balance that's right for your game's systems. (01:18)
