# 209 · 1986: The Year of Legend

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l2yPIetSPDw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/209-1986-the-year-of-legend.md)

## In one sentence
A history episode: 1986 produced an unusual number of lasting franchises because new storage capacity, fierce competition and fast growth drew people to games, and today's developers build on the paths those predecessors carved.

## Key ideas
- In Sakurai's personal opinion, 1986 was the most incredible year for the game industry. The video shows the franchises that began that year. The list appears on screen only and is not in the subtitles. Almost all of those series appear in Smash Bros. in some form.
- The Famicom Disk System launched that year and allowed far more capacity than was common then. "Megabit ROMs" also arrived, another big capacity boost that widened what games could do.
- Console, arcade and PC games were all on the rise and competed ruthlessly. Hardware and software were both improving remarkably fast, and when something gains appeal that quickly, people are naturally drawn to it.
- The NES (the overseas Famicom) came out in Europe in 1986, after its 1985 North American release, so games likely changed dramatically there too.
- In Japan, hidden characters and secret codes were very popular. They heightened "the surprise and joy of discovering the unknown," though it must have been tough for players who weren't in the know.
- Games were getting larger and harder, and Sakurai experienced firsthand how harsh that felt.
- Luxo Jr. was also released in 1986, "the dawn of CG as a form of entertainment." There are now many ways to play games from that era, and he is thankful for that.
- The path today's creators walk was carved by their predecessors, and now they have to create a new path of their own.

## Do this
- Play games from formative eras like this one (many are playable today) to see where current conventions came from.
- Use hidden characters and secrets for the joy of discovery, but don't make the game's main appeal depend on insider knowledge, since players who weren't in the know had a tough time.
- When you build on an older game's ideas, aim to carve a new path instead of retracing the old one.

## Examples from the episode
- **Famicom Disk System and megabit ROMs (1986):** jumps in capacity that let games grow larger.
- **Hidden characters and secret codes (Japan, 1986 era):** a source of surprise and discovery, but hard on players who weren't in the know.
- **Luxo Jr. (1986):** marked the dawn of CG as entertainment.

## Quote
> The path we walk on was first carved out by our predecessors all those years ago. (04:36)
