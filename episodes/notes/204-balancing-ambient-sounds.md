# 204 · Balancing Ambient Sounds

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jAgi6Uk1CU0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/204-balancing-ambient-sounds.md)

## In one sentence
Mix ambient sounds at the lowest priority so they never mask important effects, but don't cut them, because quiet, fairly realistic ambience is what makes the game world feel real.

## Key ideas
- Even Smash Bros. has ambient sounds, though they are hard to hear. With the volume turned up there are surprisingly many of them.
- Ambient sounds are the least important sounds and take the lowest priority. If they stand out too much, players can't hear the sound effects that really matter.
- That doesn't make them inaudible. Sound carries differently in different environments, and players with good speakers will notice whether the ambience is well made. If the player stops for a second and hears dead silence, the scene has no atmosphere.
- Games should make players feel they have entered another world. That immersion matters most with first-person and over-the-shoulder views.
- Most sound effects need to be exaggerated, but ambient sounds work well when they are fairly realistic. Even cartoony games can usually layer in realistic sounds without trouble.
- Experience from other visual media is likely to help here.

## Do this
- Give ambient sounds the lowest priority in your mix, and confirm they never cover a gameplay-critical sound effect.
- Stop playing in each area and just listen. If you hear dead silence, add ambient layers.
- Check the mix on good speakers too, where the quality of the ambience is easier to hear.
- Use fairly realistic recordings for ambience, even in a cartoony game, and keep exaggeration for the gameplay sound effects.
- Put extra effort into ambience in first-person or over-the-shoulder games, where immersion matters most.

## Avoid
- Ambience so loud or busy that it buries the sound effects that matter.
- Dead silence whenever the action stops.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Bros. — stage ambience:** played back with the volume raised to show how many quiet background sounds the stages contain.
