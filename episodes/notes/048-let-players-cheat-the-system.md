# 048 · Let Players Cheat the System

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVsVTgJKuzU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/048-let-players-cheat-the-system.md)

## In one sentence
Leave players a little room to "cheat the system" within your game's rules, because getting gains without losses is fun and memorable, and just following the path you laid out isn't.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai defines strategy as how players minimize losses and maximize gains, sometimes by taking a bigger loss for an even bigger gain. The definition sounds odd, but it applies across genres (action, FPS, fighting, RPG, RTS, puzzle, gambling). Any game with push and pull involves some strategy.
- Games are built in complicated ways, so it sometimes becomes possible to "cheat the system": to get gains without taking any losses.
- Balance-breaking situations like these are usually removed during debugging, but letting players cheat the system can be fun and memorable.
- Dragon Quest's battles are carefully balanced, yet metal slime farming lets players skip many of them for a huge advantage. It's still fun, and players remember it.
- Players will do whatever they can to cheat the system, including resetting until they get the result they want. Even that process can be part of the fun.
- The limit: this doesn't extend to letting players rewrite game parameters. A little cheating within the bounds of the game is fine.

## Do this
- When testers find a balance-breaking trick, ask whether it stays inside the game's rules (luring enemies off ledges, farming a rare high-value enemy, resetting for a result) and whether finding it is fun. Keep the fun ones instead of patching them out by reflex.
- Consider adding a deliberate within-the-rules shortcut, like Dragon Quest's metal slimes, that lets dedicated players skip part of the balanced grind.
- Map the strategy in each system: list how players can cut losses, raise gains, and trade a large loss for a larger gain.
- Keep exploits inside the game's own mechanics, and keep blocking anything that amounts to rewriting game parameters.

## Avoid
- Removing every balance-breaking trick during debugging without asking whether it's fun.
- Letting players rewrite game parameters. That's outside the bounds Sakurai means.

## Examples from the episode
- **Dark Souls — enemy luring:** standing in certain spots tricks enemies into falling to their deaths, so you rack up souls with no work.
- **Dragon Quest — metal slime farming:** gives a huge advantage and bypasses many carefully balanced battles, but it's fun and players remember it.
- **Resetting:** resetting until you win guarantees an eventual win, and the process itself can be fun.

## Quote
> After all, just following the path laid out before you isn't very fun, is it? (02:26)
