# 132 · The Team Behind Super Smash Bros. Brawl

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MammcQ3XkpY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/132-the-team-behind-super-smash-bros-brawl.md)

## In one sentence
When a project truly needs you, commit fully (clear your other work) and use the time before the team arrives to write the design docs and win over stakeholders, because a series' spirit depends on whoever holds it together.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai had left HAL Laboratory after Melee to go freelance. In May 2005, the day before E3, Nintendo announced a new Smash Bros. at a press conference. Sakurai was in the audience, and nobody had told him. Iwata then called him to his top-floor hotel suite in Los Angeles and asked him to be involved "in a role as close to director as possible."
- Nintendo announced the game before any development had started because Smash was the most-wanted title for the upcoming Wi-Fi Connection service in both Japan and the US, and it showed off the Wii's network capabilities.
- As a freelancer he had already accepted other projects and couldn't decide before seeing the new hardware. When he asked what would happen if he declined, Iwata said they'd find someone else, but it would be Melee's 26 fighters untouched with network support added. Sakurai took that to mean Iwata, who had a keen eye for people, couldn't picture anyone else making a truly new Smash.
- Zelda producer Eiji Aonuma wrote (quoted from Sakurai's column at the time) that without Sakurai the series would likely end: he packs in elements from many games yet keeps it coherent and fun, through ability, taste and thought process nobody could replace. Facing a task only he could do, with people (including creators of the featured titles) counting on him, Sakurai accepted.
- Making Smash would take all his time, so he turned down his other projects except Sodatete! Kouchuu Ouja Mushiking, which was already in progress; he still feels sorry about that. Iwata decided Nintendo would assemble a new team in Tokyo with Sakurai at its center, instead of HAL. The core came from Game Arts, introduced by Mr. Miyamoto; they had played so much Melee that their analog stick ridges were worn down like an old eraser, and both executives and developers were excited.
- A new office opened in Takadanobaba, and development began at the start of October. Between E3 and then, Sakurai wrote the design docs and pitched each character to its original creator. Team members brought in more members, reaching over 100 people.
- Building a new team around a freelance director for an ongoing series is extremely rare. Looking ahead past Ultimate, one option is separating the series from its creator, but speaking objectively he can't yet imagine a Smash without him, since no one can simply take the reins. He'd like to keep working with Nintendo however he can.

## Do this
- When you take on a project that needs all of you, clear or decline competing commitments up front rather than splitting your focus.
- Use the gap before full development starts to write the design documents and get buy-in from key stakeholders (Sakurai pitched each character to its original creator).
- When forming a core team, favor people who already know and love the game or genre and are excited to work on it.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Brawl — announcement:** revealed at the 2005 pre-E3 press conference before development began, to showcase Wi-Fi Connection.
- **Super Smash Bros. Melee:** the fallback plan without Sakurai was Melee's 26 fighters plus network support; Game Arts staff had worn their sticks down playing it.
- **Sodatete! Kouchuu Ouja Mushiking:** the one in-progress project he kept.
