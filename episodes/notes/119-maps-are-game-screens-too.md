# 119 · Maps Are Game Screens, Too

**Category:** Design Specifics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Fhj07C1ou0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/119-maps-are-game-screens-too.md)

## In one sentence
Design your map as a real game screen and put navigation information into the main view, so players look at your world instead of staring at the mini-map.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai often feels he is "just playing on the mini-map": the huge, beautifully rendered 3D screen goes unseen while his eyes stay on the map in the corner, which is a real shame.
- 3D space is much harder to depict than flat terrain, where an overhead view is enough. Subnautica's map has to show vertical space, which can be hard to understand.
- Opening and closing a full-screen map again and again is frustrating. It may be the most common approach, but that doesn't mean you shouldn't look for something better.
- The Mystery Dungeon series lays a translucent map over the whole game screen, so you can read it while playing, and one button hides the gameplay to show only the map. It won't work in every genre, but it was groundbreaking at the time.
- You can also put more information on the game screen itself. Forza's visual guidance tells you how tight a corner is without a look at the map. Failing that, overlay supplementary info directly on the screen. Terrain can also be placed and shaped to guide players around corners and into interesting areas.
- Think about where players will look while playing, and even more, anticipate what they will be thinking.

## Do this
- In playtests, note where players' eyes go. If they mostly watch the mini-map, move the information they need into the main view.
- Try a translucent full-screen map overlay players can keep up while moving, plus one button that switches to a map-only view.
- Add on-screen guidance (for example, racing-line style markers that show how sharp the next corner is) so players can steer without checking the map.
- Shape and place terrain so it naturally leads players around corners and toward interesting areas.
- For vertical or 3D spaces, design how the map shows height instead of assuming a flat top-down view is enough.

## Avoid
- A mini-map so essential that it pulls players' eyes away from the rendered world.
- Making players repeatedly open and close a full-screen map.

## Examples from the episode
- **Subnautica — map:** has to show the verticality of 3D space, which can be hard to wrap your head around.
- **Mystery Dungeon series — map overlay:** a translucent map over the whole screen, with a button to hide gameplay and view only the map.
- **Forza — visual guidance:** shows how tight a corner is without looking at the map.

## Quote
> It's important to consider where players will look while they're playing your game. And it's even more important to anticipate what they'll be thinking. (02:02)
