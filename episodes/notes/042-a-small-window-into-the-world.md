# 042 · A Small Window Into the World

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DGIJk0Uh8jU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/042-a-small-window-into-the-world.md)

## In one sentence
The screen is a small window (about 30 degrees of the player's vision) into your game's world, so decide deliberately how much of it your gameplay and targets occupy.

## Key ideas
- Even with near-live-footage graphics, racing games never feel like actually driving, and a key reason is screen size: a screen fills only around 30 degrees of the player's vision, nothing like real driving.
- Games squeeze a wider field of view into that narrow window, which brings its own conflicts; screens are confined, so be constantly aware of how much real estate you're using.
- A cockpit view would probably feel most realistic in racing, but most developers choose something else, because it severely reduces the screen space left for gameplay.
- In VR, lower resolution makes distant objects too blurry, a problem when the player relies on it to see more, and you can't glance sideways without turning your whole head. Still, he recommends trying VR racing at least once; for games that separate your viewpoint from your direction of travel, VR is the perfect fit (motion sickness is always possible).
- In first-person shooters the screen is big but the target is tiny. Players need to see their surroundings, so it isn't a problem as such, but it lacks some impact as entertainment. In Kid Icarus: Uprising he made enemies several times bigger than the player to compensate, though it depends on what you're going for.
- When you're wrapped up in games, it's easy to forget the screen is a small window; think about how you use each part of it.

## Do this
- Treat the screen as roughly a 30-degree slice of the player's vision, and decide what must fit inside it before choosing the camera and field of view.
- For each camera option (e.g., cockpit vs. other views), check how much of the screen is left for actual gameplay, and prefer the one that leaves more.
- If targets look tiny on screen, scale them up, for example by making enemies several times bigger than the player character, to restore impact.
- When considering VR, account for blurry distant objects and having to turn the whole head, and favor it for games where viewpoint and direction of travel are separate.
- Periodically review what each region of your screen is being used for.

## Avoid
- Choosing a camera for realism (like cockpit view) at the cost of playable screen space.
- Tiny targets on a large screen that lack impact.
- Forgetting the screen is a small window into the world.

## Examples from the episode
- **Racing games — cockpit view:** feels most realistic, but shrinks the screen space for play, so most developers choose another view.
- **VR racing:** blurry distance and whole-head turns, but a perfect fit when viewpoint and direction of travel are separate.
- **First-person shooters — small targets:** a big screen with a tiny target lacks some impact.
- **Kid Icarus: Uprising — enemy size:** enemies made several times bigger than the player to compensate.
