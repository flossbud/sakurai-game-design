# 143 · Official Websites

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ABow3YMNg1Q) · [Transcript](../transcripts/143-official-websites.md)

## In one sentence
Keep an official website as the home base for all your game's information, shaped around your game rather than a standard template, and if people can't see your game's depth, build your own channel (like a players' guide) to explain it.

## Key ideas
- Websites used to be essential for keeping up with a game, but people now dig into them less. Short bursts of information on social media may fit today's world better. Still, major titles will keep official sites, because "it's still important to have a place to deliver all your information."
- **Smash Ultimate's site:** Sakurai is involved in its production and layout and checks every update. It goes in depth to match the game's variety: fighters, modes and techniques, online play, amiibo, over 100 standard stages (more with DLC), Pokémon and Assist Trophies, a built-in music player, fighter reveals and "Mr. Sakurai Presents" videos, a blog run alongside the official Twitter account, and tournament, event and photo-contest info. It holds "far more information than the average game site."
- "Marketing is vital," and so is a home base. Instead of making a site "in a standard fashion," find the form that best fits your game.
- **The original Smash Bros. setback:** Right before release, with the game basically finished, Sakurai couldn't get other parties to understand its gameplay, fun and game essence past their preconceptions ("You can't make Nintendo characters fight each other!", "The game seems shallow compared to other fighting games"). He believed it had deep push and pull, but a game unlike anything before is hard to explain. He wrote documentation on how to play and on its push and pull and sent it to game magazines, and almost none would give him the time of day.
- **His workaround:** He turned that documentation into a players' guide website, Smash Bros. DOJO!!, writing the HTML himself despite little experience and tight data limits. "Proactively searching for new solutions will ultimately prove fruitful in the future": the effort behind Smash Ultimate's fighter reveals likely traces back to that setback.
- With social media so popular, the role of official websites may need to shift slightly. Sakurai leaves open what the most effective way to deliver information will be in the future.

## Do this
- Set up one official site as the canonical home for all information about your game, and keep it updated together with your blog and social accounts.
- Organize the site around your game's actual content (characters, modes, techniques, online features, stages, music, events) instead of a generic template.
- Have someone who knows the design intimately (Sakurai does it as director) review the layout and every update.
- Write a document that explains how your game is played and where its push and pull lies. If press or partners won't run it, publish it yourself as a players' guide.

## Avoid
- A one-size-fits-all site built "in a standard fashion" that doesn't fit your game.
- Waiting for others to explain a game unlike anything before. Preconceptions are hard to break, so explain the game yourself.

## Examples from the episode
- **Smash Bros. Ultimate official site:** fighters, modes, techniques, 100+ stages, Pokémon/Assist Trophies, a music player, fighter reveals, "Mr. Sakurai Presents" videos, a blog, tournaments and photo contests.
- **Super Smash Bros. (original):** magazines ignored his push-and-pull documentation, so he built the Smash Bros. DOJO!! players' guide site with hand-written HTML.
- **Smash Bros. Ultimate fighter reveals:** in his view, an outgrowth of that early setback.

## Quote
> Instead of just making a site in a standard fashion, you want to find a form that best fits your game. (02:31)
