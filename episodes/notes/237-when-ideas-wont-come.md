# 237 · When Ideas Won't Come

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kh0DhZGfaIY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/237-when-ideas-wont-come.md)

## In one sentence
When you're stuck for an idea, define the theme of the problem first, then generate as many candidate solutions as you can, following each thread before you prune.

## Key ideas
- Some people "sleep on it." Sakurai keeps thinking until he squeezes something out. It doesn't solve everything, but it's his approach.
- Step one: define the theme of the problem, and keep it simple. His example theme is "how to put out a fire in some ruins."
- Step two: run mental trial and error on solutions. Throw water on it: there's none nearby. Cut the air flow: there's no way to block it. Smother it with sand: sand is hard to find, but dirt might work. Use explosives: too much damage, and where would you get them? Cover it with a bucket: the fire is too big, but a bigger bucket might work.
- Think up broad concepts, then follow their threads to an answer. Don't pass over minor variants like dirt or a bigger bucket; dig into every possibility.
- Keep producing solutions. Even unrealistic ones are better than none. Quantity over quality; prune later. This gets you past the feeling of having nothing and opens the way to improvements.
- Silly ideas are valid, because nobody can see inside your head. Cast a wide net and you may land on a good idea eventually.
- He has used this process many times for game ideas and design decisions. If you try to think of something from nothing, you have no starting point, and ideas naturally won't come.

## Do this
- Write the problem as a one-line theme before brainstorming (e.g., "how to put out a fire in some ruins").
- List many broad solution types, and note next to each why it fails in this context.
- For each rejected idea, look for a variant that fixes its flaw (no sand, so try dirt; the bucket is too small, so try a bigger one).
- Aim for quantity first, silly or unrealistic options included, and prune only once the list is long.
- Keep thinking actively instead of waiting for inspiration.

## Avoid
- Trying to come up with something from nothing, with no defined problem.
- Throwing out minor or silly ideas too early.

## Examples from the episode
- **Thought experiment — putting out a fire in ruins:** water, cutting airflow, sand (then dirt), explosives, a bucket (then a bigger bucket).

## Quote
> If you try to think of something from nothing, you won't have a clear starting point. And then it'll only be natural if the ideas won't come. (02:08)
