# 065 · Falling Sells the Feeling of Flight

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j45EIuChfqs) · [Transcript](../transcripts/065-falling-sells-the-feeling-of-flight.md)

## In one sentence
To make flight (or speed) feel good, add an opposing force such as falling, crashing or a requirement to fly, because the thrill comes from the risk and from barely scraping by.

## Key ideas
- To create the sensation of flying freely, start by adding a falling mechanic or some other penalty, or replace the penalty with a requirement the player has to meet in order to fly.
- No motion feels complete until there's an opposing element. In flying games that's falling; in high-speed games it's conditions that slow players down.
- In the first Ace Combat, you fly over the ocean and come to a very long bridge that makes you want to fly under it. Pulling it off feels great because of the inherent risk and the sense of barely scraping by: hit the bridge or the water and you're finished. The real fun is threading the needle.
- A game with nothing but sky and no ground to crash into would have no tension. That's why flying games in outer space are harder to get right than ones inside the atmosphere, and why high-altitude dogfights don't come together as quickly as stages with ground and obstacles.
- To make superhuman flight feel good, the player's interaction with the surrounding environment has to be spot-on. In Kirby Air Ride, machines fly when launched off cliffs and ramps, and searching for those spots is fun in itself. Having some machines that can fly and some that can't makes flight more enjoyable either way.
- When you add a mechanic, think about how to oppose it too, the way player attacks and enemy attacks come as a set. Risk and reward are two sides of the same coin.

## Do this
- For every movement power you add (flight, a speed boost), design its opposing force at the same time: falling, a crash penalty, slowdown conditions or a requirement to activate it.
- Put ground, water and obstacles near flight paths, such as a bridge players can try to fly under, so there's always a needle to thread.
- Tie flight to features in the environment like cliffs and ramps, and let players hunt for those launch spots.
- Include some vehicles or characters that can't fly alongside ones that can, so flight feels special.
- If your setting is open sky or outer space with nothing to crash into, add terrain or obstacles to create tension.

## Avoid
- Flight with nothing to crash into, which has no tension.
- High-altitude dogfights with no ground or obstacles to contend with.
- Adding a mechanic without designing what opposes it.

## Examples from the episode
- **Ace Combat (the first game) — the long bridge:** flying under it is thrilling because hitting the bridge or the water ends you.
- **Kirby Air Ride — flight spots:** machines fly off cliffs and ramps; some machines can fly and others can't.
- **Space flight games and high-altitude dogfights:** harder to make tense, because there's no ground to crash into.

## Quote
> Imagine a game with nothing but sky, with no ground at all to crash into. It wouldn't have any tension. (01:06)
