# 256 · Give Yourself a Handicap When Balancing Your Game

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2T86JqRAKjc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/256-give-yourself-a-handicap-when-balancing-your-game.md)

## In one sentence
Developers know their game too well to be the difficulty standard, so tune difficulty while handicapping yourself (one hand, less health, stricter conditions) and then make it easier than you think it should be.

## Key ideas
- Hard action games where you're meant to die repeatedly aren't actually too tough to balance. The dev team knows the game, so if they can just barely squeak by, you have proof it's beatable, but only by people who know it inside and out. That guarantees a real challenge.
- Skill varies across the team, and the more you tune for the skilled members, the tougher the game gets. Balance it for the best player on staff and nobody who buys it will reach the end, which is no fun at all.
- Even balancing for the median, average-skilled team member leaves the game hard, since even people who know the game struggled with it. Players won't spend all their time with just your game.
- The best solution is someone unfamiliar with the game playing from the start. But they often don't have time to finish, and they also grow familiar, so you can't call the same person back a second or third time.
- So lower the difficulty a good deal from what feels right to the team. Sakurai does this by giving himself a handicap with the controls, such as playing with one hand: it's easy to mess up even when he knows the game well. Tangible limits like lower health or stricter conditions work too.
- Skill levels vary drastically, so there's no one right answer, and there's real joy in persevering through a difficult challenge. Still, developers who know the game shouldn't be the standard.

## Do this
- Play through your difficulty tuning with a handicap: one hand on the controller, reduced health, or stricter self-imposed conditions. Tune so you can still clear it that way.
- Don't use a clear by the best player on staff, or even the average one, as the target. Set difficulty clearly below what feels right to the team.
- Bring in people who have never played the game, start them from the very beginning, and treat each person as a fresh tester only once.
- After tuning, lower the difficulty one more notch, since it will still feel easier to you than to players.

## Avoid
- Balancing around the most skilled member of the dev team.
- Calling the same outside tester back repeatedly as if they were still unfamiliar with the game.

## Examples from the episode
- **Sakurai's own method — one-handed play:** he handicaps himself with the controls so he makes mistakes even though he knows the game well.

## Quote
> Developers who already know a game shouldn't be considered as the standard— instead, make the game even easier than you think it should be, and it'll be just right. (02:12)
