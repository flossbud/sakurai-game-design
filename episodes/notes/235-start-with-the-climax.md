# 235 · Start with the Climax

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eAd0pt-uv8g) · [Transcript](../transcripts/235-start-with-the-climax.md)

## In one sentence
Open your game with a climactic scene the player controls and save the backstory for later, because players want to take control and discover the game right away.

## Key ideas
- A game's intro is extremely important. Starting with a climax doesn't mean putting the last boss first (though you can).
- Final Fantasy VII's opening wouldn't feel out of place as the run-up to the final dungeon. Told chronologically, the story might start with why Cloud joined Avalanche; instead it starts in style.
- Many games start slowly, spending a long time on the hero's backstory, the world's customs and the hero's friendships. Films also do better to open with a climactic event and show how things got there later.
- Games especially should not amble along, because players want to take control and find out what the game is. Let them play first thing.
- If possible, show early that the enemy means business. In Kid Icarus: Uprising Chapter 1, the door opens and you're thrust into an Air Battle, and you meet the first antagonist, Medusa, in under a minute. You don't fight her directly, and it's clearly only the first chapter.
- Uprising skims over Pit's link to Palutena, the sky and surface worlds, Air versus Land Battles, Powers and the Underworld Army in favor of a fast start.
- It's fine to let events unfold slowly through daily life, but then hand control to the player as soon as possible. If a narrative peak at the start doesn't suit the structure, at least keep the opening short. Even Phoenix Wright puts you in a trial at the very start.

## Do this
- Make the first minutes playable and high-stakes: give the player control in a dramatic scene before any exposition.
- Introduce the main threat or antagonist within about the first minute and show they mean business (Uprising does it in under a minute).
- Postpone backstory, world rules, relationships and secondary systems until the player is hooked.
- If the game has to open on everyday life, hand over control as early as possible and keep the opening short.
- Consider opening in the middle of the action, then showing how the characters got there later.

## Avoid
- Slow openings that spend a long time on backstory, customs and relationships before the player gets to play.
- Telling the story strictly in order when that costs you a strong opening.

## Examples from the episode
- **Final Fantasy VII — opening:** could pass for the run-up to a final dungeon; it skips why Cloud joined Avalanche.
- **Kid Icarus: Uprising — Chapter 1:** straight into an Air Battle, with Medusa introduced in under a minute and the lore skimmed over.
- **Phoenix Wright — first case:** you're in a trial right at the start.

## Quote
> With games in particular, players really want to take control and find out about your game— so it's no time for ambling along. Let them play, first thing! (01:00)
