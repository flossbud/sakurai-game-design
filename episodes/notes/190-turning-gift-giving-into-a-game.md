# 190 · Turning Gift-Giving Into a Game

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CB85OhWD9o8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/190-turning-gift-giving-into-a-game.md)

## In one sentence
When you have things to give your team, turn the giveaway into a small game: it works as an icebreaker and builds the kind of director–team relationship game development should have.

## Key ideas
- When Sakurai commuted to an office, especially before the COVID-19 pandemic, he often brought gifts for the dev team: a second copy of a game a publisher sent him after he'd already bought it, things he bought twice by accident, and things he didn't really use, including expensive items like graphics cards and gaming monitors. Sometimes he brought merchandise from Smash Bros. events too. His team would make better use of them than he would.
- He never handed things out at random. He made it a kind of lottery: he listed the day's items on the Daily Report page and interested people added their names.
- He then picked winners with different rules each time: a rock-paper-scissors tournament, a battle in the Smash Bros. they were making, a round of Samurai Kirby, whoever pulled off a Kamikazee first, or whoever posted closest to 0 seconds (with that rule kept secret).
- Making these into games and events served as an icebreaker for the team. If you're giving something away, "the real winner is anyone who has fun."
- In recent years his team members have given him all kinds of birthday presents, which he sees as proof of a good relationship between director and team, and "that's the way game dev should be."

## Do this
- Pass spare items, such as duplicate games, unused hardware and event merchandise, to your team instead of letting them sit.
- List the available items in a shared daily channel (his was the Daily Report page) and let anyone interested sign up.
- Pick winners with a quick game: a rock-paper-scissors tournament, a match in the game you're building, first to pull off a specific tricky move, or a secret-rule contest such as whoever posted closest to 0 seconds.
- Use these small events on purpose as icebreakers to build friendly relationships between the director and the team.

## Avoid
- Handing things out at random with no fun attached.

## Examples from the episode
- **Daily Report page:** where the items on offer were listed and people signed up.
- **Smash Bros. (in development):** used as a battle to decide who won a gift.
- **Samurai Kirby:** another way to settle who won.
- **Pulling off a Kamikazee first, or posting closest to 0 seconds:** other ways of picking winners, with the 0-seconds rule kept secret.
- **Birthday presents from the team:** a sign of a healthy director–team relationship.

## Quote
> After all, if you're giving something away, the real winner is anyone who has fun. (01:39)
