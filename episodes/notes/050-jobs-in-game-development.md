# 050 · Jobs in Game Development

**Category:** Team Management · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hk2npGxEQRo) · [Transcript](../transcripts/050-jobs-in-game-development.md)

## In one sentence
Know what each role on a game team does and who covers it, and share the project's objectives with all of them, because the roles only produce a game when they work in tandem.

## Key ideas
- **Director:** generally one per team. Comes up with and consolidates game ideas, makes almost all final decisions, and supervises every aspect, adjusting as needed. If they conceived the vision, they may also be called the game designer. The director sits on almost every subteam and oversees adjustment after adjustment until the game reaches release quality.
- **Planners** turn the director's ideas into design documents (spec sheets) detailed enough to program from. They also manage teams, balance gameplay, supervise testing, and sometimes write scripts that run outside the game code. **Programmers** turn those specs into instructions the computer understands and pull every other team's assets together. Computers are uncooperative, so the job takes patience and flexibility.
- **Art roles:** modelers build polygon objects (fighters, stages, enemies, items), so teams need many of them doing very different work. Artists make 2D illustrations, and the line between artist and modeler blurs. Animators need exaggeration, variety, smart posing and movement that serves the game's goals, not just "realistic" motion; animation teams are often large, and riggers build the skeletons. Effect designers make dash clouds, hit effects, blade trails, flames and lightning with impact and believability, which takes a different animation sensibility.
- **Sound** splits into music composers, sound effect designers (impacts, footsteps, environmental noise) and voice-over supervisors (who run recording sessions, then edit and post-process the clips). **UI designers** make menus; the UI team mixes planners, artists and programmers, but with different goals from their counterparts on other teams.
- **Support:** technical operations runs servers and dev environments and troubleshoots; their work is rarely seen by players but is indispensable. Managers handle schedules, holdups, staffing, company rules, office upkeep and hardware. Play testers report issues and tuning needs to the planning team; competitive games need testers with the right skill level and the perseverance to keep playing. Bug testers write reports explaining how to reproduce each bug, and a manager assigns each report to the right team. Footage specialists capture material for fighter reveals.
- Teams are often split into subteams (e.g. characters, stages), each a mix of professions; the Smash "fighter team" includes planners, programmers, modelers, artists, animators, effect designers and sound designers. Nothing gets done without mutual understanding, so objectives and goals should be shared with everyone.

## Do this
- List these roles against your own team (one person may cover several) and name an owner for each: direction, specs, programming, 3D, 2D, animation/rigging, effects, music/SFX/voice, UI, tech ops, management, play testing, bug testing and footage.
- Have someone in a planner role turn the director's ideas into spec sheets detailed enough to program from, and distribute them to the team.
- Require every bug report to include steps to reproduce it, and have one person route each report to the team that owns the fix.
- For a competitive game, choose play testers with the skill and perseverance to keep playing, and send their feedback to the planners for review.
- Organize work into subteams that mix professions, put the director in every subteam, and share the project's goals with all of them.

## Avoid
- Treating animation as just "realistic" movement. It needs exaggeration, variety and posing that serve the game.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — reveal footage:** captured in-engine by the team's own footage specialists, which Sakurai credits for the high quality.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — fighter team:** a single subteam that combines nearly every discipline.
