# 250 · Be Mindful of the Time Players are Giving You

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-49L25Q0YAU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/250-be-mindful-of-the-time-players-are-giving-you.md)

## In one sentence
Treat play time as a cost the player pays, not a selling point: build the fun first, let long play time follow from it, and keep the player in control for as much of that time as possible.

## Key ideas
- Being told "This game has 300 hours of content!" before you start is likely to put you off. When people decide whether to buy, they may pass on a game that looks fun if it seems like it will take too long, because they weigh that time against everything else they could do.
- Some games do get played for hundreds or thousands of hours, Smash Bros. included. But that time should be a result of how fun the game is. Play time, especially as it's perceived before starting, is better thought of as a cost.
- Today games are in a battle for people's time. Even people with lots of free time always have other things to do, so a game that takes hundreds of hours gets weighed against all of it.
- Many people spend free time on phones and tablets, on social media or browsing, because it offers diverse content with a low barrier to entry. Games have a higher barrier and take more effort to start, so your game needs enough appeal to overcome that.
- Focus first on fun experiences and on things that reinforce that fun. Play time will fall into place later.
- Separately, Sakurai always watches how much of the time the player spends in control, because the longer they're in control, the better.

## Do this
- Don't sell or plan your game around an hour count. Lead with what's fun, and let length be a by-product.
- In planning, write down the core fun experiences and the systems that reinforce them, and settle those before deciding how much content to make.
- Measure how much of a typical session the player actually has control, versus cutscenes, loading, menus and transitions, and cut the non-control time.
- Assume players are comparing your game with low-effort phone content, and make sure its appeal is strong enough to be worth the extra effort of starting it.

## Avoid
- Advertising a huge play time ("300 hours of content!") as the main attraction.
- Treating play time as a goal in itself rather than a result of fun.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — long play time:** plenty of people have spent hundreds or even thousands of hours on it, an example of time that comes from the game being fun.
- **Phones and tablets — social media and browsing:** they win people's free time through diverse content and a low barrier to entry.

## Quote
> The time players spend in a game is a cost, so it's best to be mindful of the time they're choosing to give you. (02:31)
