# 038 · Writing Game Proposals

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Iiu1k16Ukc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/038-writing-game-proposals.md)

## In one sentence
Build proposals as fast-moving slides, one image and two lines of text per page, grouped into categories, and talk around them instead of reading them aloud.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai's recent proposal format is PowerPoint, and almost every page has one screenshot or diagram and just two lines of text. Each page is its own topic within those limits.
- His Smash Bros. Ultimate proposal had over 200 pages, but they move quick and snappy like comic panels, so it doesn't take long to get through. Some of it goes deep into mechanics that only the dev team needs for implementation.
- You'll often hear proposals should be lean, but that isn't always true: be clear, engaging and to the point. If you move through a dense presentation quickly, page count isn't really a problem.
- He breaks the deck into categories and subcategories so people can follow along and see how far in they are, because nobody likes an endless presentation.
- He started this format around when he went freelance. He says a little about each slide but never reads straight off the page; a wall of text read aloud is annoying, and people just read ahead.
- The most important thing is still what the proposal says.

## Do this
- Build proposal slides with one screenshot or diagram and at most two lines of text each, one topic per slide.
- Move through slides quickly, like comic panels; a long deck (his Smash Ultimate proposal had 200+ pages) is fine if each page is quick.
- Group slides into categories and subcategories, and show which section you're in so the audience knows how far along they are.
- Say a little about each slide out loud instead of reading its text.
- Put implementation detail the dev team needs in the same snappy format rather than cutting it.
- Make sure the content is strong before polishing the format.

## Avoid
- Wall-of-text slides read aloud word for word.
- Endless presentations with no visible structure.
- Assuming a proposal must always be short.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — proposal:** a PowerPoint of over 200 pages, nearly all one image and two lines of text, presented quickly like comic panels.
