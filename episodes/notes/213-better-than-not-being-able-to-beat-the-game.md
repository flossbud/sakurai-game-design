# 213 · Better Than Not Being Able to Beat the Game

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G3eacnEqdV0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/213-better-than-not-being-able-to-beat-the-game.md)

## In one sentence
Always give stuck players a way forward, such as changing difficulty at any time or skipping after repeated failure, because once a player gives up, your challenge is worth nothing.

## Key ideas
- Games give the most catharsis when you are straddling the line between victory and defeat, and they are designed to deliver it constantly. Putting dozens of hours into a game only to hit an impossible difficulty spike at the very end is something to "avoid at all costs."
- In old-school RPGs you could always win eventually by leveling up. That simplicity is part of why RPGs succeeded. Action games are the problem, because getting stronger alone won't win, and some simulation games share the issue. Even when you expect players to improve, "everyone has their breaking point."
- Some games suggest lowering the difficulty after repeated failure. "It's humiliating, yes, but it's likely better than not being able to beat the game." Others let you skip a hard stage and treat it as won. Even though that diminishes the gameplay, it is still better than being unable to go on.
- Games that make you start over because difficulty can't be changed mid-game have "a fundamental design flaw." However fun tense challenges are, the moment players give up the challenge becomes worthless.
- The way older games pushed players to their limits may not suit the modern era, though hardcore games can still become smash hits.
- Sakurai tailored Kirby's Dream Land to beginners from the very start and wants to be kind to players, but as a player he sometimes wants a real challenge too. His advice is to offer a helping hand without humiliating players when you can. Simply letting them change difficulty whenever they want "should make a big difference."
- Dropping kindness for intense battles is still a valid goal, since some pleasures only come from that. Just assume not every player wants it in every game.

## Do this
- Let players change difficulty at any point in a playthrough without restarting.
- After repeated failures in the same spot, offer an easier setting or a skip that counts the section as cleared, and word the offer so it helps without humiliating the player.
- In action or simulation games where leveling up can't get players past a skill wall, add an explicit way around it, such as a difficulty change or a skip.
- Playtest the endgame for difficulty spikes that could stop players who have put in dozens of hours.
- If you choose to make a hardcore game, do it on purpose, knowing that not every player will want it.

## Avoid
- Locking difficulty at the start so that a stuck player's only option is to start over.
- An impossible difficulty spike at the very end of a long game.

## Examples from the episode
- **Old-school RPGs:** if you keep leveling up you can eventually win, which is part of why they succeeded.
- **Kirby's Dream Land:** designed for beginners from its inception.
- **Games that suggest an easier mode or let you skip stages (titles not named):** a little humiliating, but better than never beating the game.

## Quote
> No matter how fun tense challenges may be, the moment players give up, it's all over. (01:20)
