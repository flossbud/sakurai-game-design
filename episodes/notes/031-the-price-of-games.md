# 031 · The Price of Games

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i6jKMAU3aJ8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/031-the-price-of-games.md)

## In one sentence
Games are remarkably cheap for the hours of fun they give and the size of the teams behind them, so if there's a game you want to play or support, buy it or play it through a subscription.

## Key ideas
- People often ask whether games are too expensive. Sakurai's personal opinion is that they're really cheap: from an adult's perspective, few things offer so many hours of fun for such a reasonable price.
- Most other fun costs a lot (eating or drinking out, theater or movies, which are especially expensive in Japan, gambling, vacations, hotels, local delicacies). Games give dozens of hours for around 70 dollars at most, even AAA titles; smaller games can cost as little as 10 dollars, and free-to-play games cost nothing. Prices vary by country.
- Games are tens or even hundreds of times bigger in scope and content than they used to be, yet prices have largely stayed the same. They need large teams that must be supported, and marketing and release cost plenty more.
- Subscription services now offer even cheaper entertainment, for games as well as film and TV. Great for consumers; for creators, production costs get covered, but they must compete with older titles. It goes both ways.
- A game's price doesn't mean much if nobody plays it, so he asks players to buy games they want to support or play them through a subscription.

## Do this
- Buy the games you want to play or support, or play them through a subscription, so their creators get supported.
- If you're considering releasing on a subscription service, weigh both sides: your production costs get covered, but your game competes with older titles in the same library.

## Examples from the episode
- **Movie tickets in Japan:** especially expensive, compared with dozens of hours of play from a game costing around 70 dollars at most.

## Quote
> At the end of the day, the price of a game doesn't mean much if nobody plays it. (02:24)
