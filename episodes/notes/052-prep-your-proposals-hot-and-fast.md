# 052 · Prep Your Proposals Hot and Fast

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1cFrEoiziDA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/052-prep-your-proposals-hot-and-fast.md)

## In one sentence
Write your game proposal fast while your motivation is hot, keep it brief and image-backed with only what's needed, and treat showing the game's appeal as your responsibility.

## Key ideas
- Above all, don't drag proposals out; nothing good comes from droning on. Once the outline is done and the main points are itemized, put them straight into the presentation.
- Speed depends on preparation: sort ideas into main topics and subtopics, keep sentences brief, and use images or photos throughout to reinforce the text.
- Don't linger on your grand vision (you wouldn't explain it to players either), and cut graphs, research and the plan B. Keeping it interesting is key. Fewer pages isn't automatically better; each page should hold the right amount of information.
- Strike while your motivation is hot; the longer a proposal sits, the less fresh it gets, and momentum counts for a lot.
- A proposal's job is to show how the game is fun, yet it often lacks elements that later become essential. Monster Hunter at the proposal stage ("hunt monsters, make gear from their materials") doesn't sound novel, and probably only a few of its best points were in the proposal, so polishing is also part of making a game fun.
- There's no game without a proposal; it has to make the people who provide funding and talent think "We should definitely do this." If it gets rejected, don't decide the other side lacks taste.

## Do this
- Once the outline and main points are itemized, write the whole proposal in one push instead of letting the draft sit.
- Structure it as main topics and subtopics, keep every sentence short, and put an image or photo on the pages to back up the text.
- Cut graphs, market research, plan B and long passages about your grand vision.
- Review each page for the right amount of information rather than trying to cut the page count.
- If the proposal is rejected, rework how it shows the game's appeal instead of blaming the reviewers.

## Avoid
- Droning on, or lingering on your grand vision.
- Padding the proposal with graphs, research or a plan B.
- Assuming fewer pages is always better.
- Telling yourself a rejection means the other party has no taste.

## Examples from the episode
- **Monster Hunter — proposal level:** "hunt monsters and make gear from their materials" isn't novel on paper; most of what makes it great likely came from polish.
