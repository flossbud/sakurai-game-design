# 075 · Consider Rewards First

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SpwQkNzNsXk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/075-consider-rewards-first.md)

## In one sentence
As soon as the bones of your game exist, design its rewards, and make sure every saved-up reward has something appealing to spend it on, because rewards are where player investment comes from.

## Key ideas
- Games about defeating enemies need rewards for defeating foes and finishing missions. Old games gave a score, but that's not enough today, so games now give power-ups, money, experience, skill points and more.
- Without rewards it's still a game, and progress is a reward in itself. But that alone doesn't motivate players in modern games. Rewards are the main source of a player's investment.
- It depends on the genre, but once the bones of the game are in place, think about rewards next.
- Numerical rewards that build up over time (money, points) must come with a way to spend them. If the things you can buy are unappealing or don't suit the game, it won't turn out well.
- Materials are another way to quantify rewards: collect them, then use them to make something new. The player has to feel they're profiting and that the result is a must-have. He calls this a particularly effective technique.
- Collectibles such as playable characters or equipment also work. Whether players trade saved currency for them depends on your game's goals.
- As a player, he finds reward-collecting fun but sometimes a chore. A game where collecting is the *only* thing you do would get unpleasant.

## Do this
- Right after the core prototype works, write a reward plan: what each enemy, mission and activity gives, and where each reward gets spent.
- Design the spending side of every currency or points system at the same time as the earning side, and check that the things to buy are appealing and suit the game.
- For crafting, make sure the result feels clearly worth more than its materials and is something the player actually wants.
- Unlock collectible features (characters, equipment, collections) through play instead of giving them all at the start.
- Make sure collecting sits alongside other activities and is never the only thing the player is doing.

## Avoid
- Currencies whose rewards are unappealing or don't fit the game.
- Loops where collecting materials is all the player does.
- Handing out everything at the start, which removes the joy of earning it.

## Examples from the episode
- **Old arcade-style games — score:** the original reward, no longer enough on its own.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Spirits:** if Spirits had all been unlocked from the start instead of granted as rewards, there would be no joy in the feature.

## Quote
> Rewards are key to keeping players engaged! So like I said earlier, you should make them the first thing you consider. (02:27)
