# 253 · Odds and Ends of Supervising: Sora Edition

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qqjXBzWX3_A) · [Transcript](../transcripts/253-odds-and-ends-of-supervising-sora-edition.md)

## In one sentence
Quality comes from relentless, specific polish passes, so check poses, follow-through, effect readability, contrast and both versions of every move, and flag even tiny flaws with visual paintovers as soon as you see them.

## Key ideas
- This is a sample of the retouch images Sakurai made for the team while adding Sora to Smash Ultimate. Everything shown was addressed in feedback and fixed before release.
- Animation and posing fixes: the back jump's silhouette needed more energy; on landing Sora's position slipped (his foot moved too far); an attack follow-through was moved to a position that reads better in a game viewed from the side; the forward aerial's pose was lacking, so he used a posed figure to show how upper-body twists "bring more information to the poses"; the landing after a forward aerial needed a full follow-through; the losing clap needed energetic bend in the wrists, because it feels limp if the wrists follow a straight line from the arms; Sonic Blade's windup was a bit slow, so Sora should pull directly into the right pose, and his axis of rotation during the lead-in was too far back on his body; the last hit of Aerial Sweep should look more like punching through.
- Moves have to be checked in every version. Sonic Blade's reach shrank when attacking upward (a type of bug), and its aerial and ground poses were drastically different. For special moves, check both the ground and aerial versions.
- Effect readability: layered effects in the down throw hid the fighters; Blizzaga's effect had only bright elements, which are hard to see on bright stages, so dark elements were mixed in; the Final Smash background's atmosphere was cut back so it wouldn't clash with the visual effects, and extra smoke made the explosion read clearly; hit-effect stars were too bunched and got lost behind Donkey Kong's body, so they now fly out much farther.
- Faithfulness to the source: a costume's A and B parts had different brightnesses in the original game, but that difference was hard to see on the model; the Timeless River costume's eyes were a bit too tall and its damage expression too weak; a tiny extraneous mark on the face had to go ("easy to overlook, so you should point them out as soon as you find them"); the reveal CG model was compared with the in-game model; on Kirby's copy ability, the hair was poking into the eye highlight.
- Stage art: the Rising Falls water looked like ice, so its color was pushed toward green; character lighting on the stage was too dark (an issue that comes up a lot); the distant clouds had higher information density on the right than the left and needed balance, not symmetry; the pipes looked flat from a distance and needed gradation; the same material should have strong contrast in the foreground and weaker contrast in the background, and it was backwards (also common); fighters blended into the stained glass; drop-through platforms were hard to see and changed hue depending on their position because of light reflections; the floor stood out so much that objects like Greninja's Water Shuriken were hard to see.
- His method: retouched images, a posed figure, and sketches he always scribbles with his mouse. He also supervised the design of the door from its conception and suggested an ending image for Sora's single-player mode. Making a game calls for fine-tuning over and over, and polishing without fail is what raises quality. He asked a lot of the team, but it was a relationship built on mutual trust.

## Do this
- For every special move, play the ground and aerial versions side by side. Check that the poses match and that range and distance don't change in some directions (for example, when aimed upward).
- Test each effect on your brightest stages. Mix dark elements into bright-only effects, and make sure no effect or background atmosphere hides the fighters or competes with the attack's own effects.
- On each stage, check that a material has strong contrast in the foreground and weaker contrast in the background, that character lighting isn't too dark, and that gameplay objects (platforms, projectiles) stand out from the floor and backdrop without the floor overpowering them.
- In side-view games, check attack follow-throughs from the side, add upper-body twist to flat poses, give landings a full follow-through, and make sure feet don't slide on landing. Bend the wrists in hand gestures instead of letting them follow the arm in a straight line.
- Give art feedback visually: a retouched screenshot, a quick mouse sketch, or a posed figure. Flag even tiny flaws, such as a stray mark on a face, the moment you find them.
- When adapting a character from another game, compare your model against the original (relative brightness of costume parts, eye shape, expressions) and against any reveal CG.

## Avoid
- Effects made only of bright elements, and layered effects that bury the characters.
- Background atmosphere or a high-contrast floor that competes with effects and projectiles.
- Contrast that's stronger in the background than in the foreground.
- Letting small flaws slide because they seem minor.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Sora's forward aerial:** posed with a figure to show how upper-body twists add information.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Sora's losing clap:** limp until the wrists bent instead of following the arm line.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Blizzaga:** bright-only effect got dark elements so it reads on bright stages.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Sonic Blade:** aerial and ground poses unified, and the shortened upward distance fixed as a bug.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — hit-effect stars:** made to fly farther so they don't vanish behind Donkey Kong.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Hollow Bastion stage:** water recolored toward green, contrast fixed for stained glass and drop-through platforms, and the floor toned down so Greninja's Water Shuriken stays visible.
