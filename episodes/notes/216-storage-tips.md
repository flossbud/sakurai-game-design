# 216 · Storage Tips

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3tpttuaYiPI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/216-storage-tips.md)

## In one sentence
Keep your reference library of games, consoles, controllers and cables organized and quick to reach, because for a developer old games remain working tools.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai owns a lot of games and never sells them, so storage is "a life-or-death matter." To save space he had to give up the original packaging.
- Famicom and Mega Drive cartridges are the size of cassette tapes, so he keeps them in cassette cases, with each Famicom game labeled with its title and release date. The cases were hard to find because cassettes were already obsolete. Super Famicom cartridges are bigger, and the specialized cases for them were in short supply.
- He owns an overwhelming number of CD-size discs. He keeps them in non-woven fabric cases, several dozen of them, separated by system, and only moves a game into one after it has been out for at least a year. He has heard such cases can damage Blu-ray discs but hasn't had trouble. GameCube discs fit mini-CD-single cases, and he uses Sora stickers on some of them to track lending. PSP UMDs go in an accessory case. Anything that doesn't fit gets organized with dividers.
- Controllers he doesn't use regularly are shelved, and he prunes them when he packs away a console. The plastic on old controllers can "bleed" and get sticky.
- Systems a few generations old stay easy to reach. Older hardware is boxed up, since few TVs support RF or composite anymore, and goes to his building's storage or to paid storage facilities, some of which mail back individual boxes on request.
- His "pièce de résistance" is a custom-made game cabinet. It has six doors holding different consoles (Xbox Series X and PS5, Switch, PS4, PS3, Wii U, then swappable miniature consoles and drawers for controllers), and a selector switches between them. Frequently used controllers hang on side hooks.
- Ventilation: the door of the console in use stays open, doors slide back into the cabinet, the rear panels are open, and the top panel is open for the hot Xbox Series X and PS5. HDMI, power and LAN cables run down the rear center. A remote-controlled selector sends video to either of two TVs in the room, and each outlet can be switched off on its own.
- He has two cabinets, one at the office and one in the living room, with one of each console per cabinet. The Wii U is the exception: its account can't be shared across consoles, so he moves his single unit between them. The cabinets were expensive, but he can't imagine working without them.
- Other details: his Wii Virtual Console games are on labeled SD cards, a case keeps cables tidy, and a Tepra label printer marks what is plugged into the selector and which adapter goes with what. For Smash Bros. Ultimate he bought models of the fighters for the artists to use as reference. "Cats store themselves."
- Organizing is a pain, but not doing it is worse. Old games still come in handy for his work, for example footage for this channel. He has since switched fully to digital purchases. He has no particular attachment to physical things, but he cherishes his memories of these games.

## Do this
- Keep the games and hardware you use for reference connected and switchable in one place, for example through a video selector, so you can check a mechanic or capture footage in minutes.
- Label every cable, adapter and selector input with a label printer so you can always tell what goes where.
- Give each console its own switchable power outlet, and plan ventilation (open doors or panels) for the hottest machines.
- Buy physical models or figures of the characters your artists are building, as reference material.
- Store media compactly in standard-size cases sorted by system, and box up hardware your current displays can't connect to.

## Avoid
- Letting your collection go unorganized. Sakurai says it gets even worse if you don't stay on top of it.

## Examples from the episode
- **Custom game cabinet:** six doors, a selector, two TVs, per-outlet power switches and open panels for heat. He has two of them, one at the office and one in the living room.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate development:** fighter models bought as reference for the artists, kept on display, including figures from "Mr. Sakurai Presents Pyra/Mythra."
- **GameCube discs:** kept in mini-CD-single cases, with Sora stickers to track lending.

## Quote
> In some ways, it's a pain getting organized, but it'll be even worse if you don't stay on top of it. (08:41)
