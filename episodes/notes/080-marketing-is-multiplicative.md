# 080 · Marketing is Multiplicative

**Category:** Marketing · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iwOgFiJNCoU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/080-marketing-is-multiplicative.md)

## In one sentence
Development and marketing multiply each other, and an unknown game may as well not exist, so even as a developer, plan from the start how your work will help make the game known.

## Key ideas
- This episode opens the Marketing category, about getting the word out rather than about games themselves. His "Mr. Sakurai Presents" videos are themselves marketing.
- A game must be good and compelling, but it's equally important that it be well known. An unknown game may as well not exist, so to get the most from your hard work, people first have to learn the game exists.
- Marketing falls under sales. Development and marketing spin on the same axle, and if one wheel doesn't turn properly, you can't move forward.
- Success is multiplicative: the success of one is proportional to the success of the other.
- He works actively to make his games known, for example with fighter reveal movies and the old Smash Bros. Dojo site. When he makes in-game assets like fighter artwork, he always considers that they may be used outside the game too.
- He has publishers share marketing and operations details so he can make suggestions. He doesn't write the official Twitter or website updates, but he checks them all and gives feedback. He's a director, not a producer, yet he takes on many producer duties apart from money.
- In 2021 alone an estimated 10,000+ games were listed on Steam, and more on console and mobile. Getting onto people's radar takes work. Nintendo's promotion department does most of the heavy lifting for him, and he avoids posting too many announcements on his personal Twitter because that can rub people the wrong way.
- Drawing a lot of attention isn't always fun, but sometimes overdoing it a bit is exactly what's needed.

## Do this
- When you make in-game assets (character art, renders, cinematics), produce them at a quality and format that marketing can reuse outside the game.
- Ask your publisher or marketing team for the marketing and operations plan so you can suggest ideas while there's still time to act on them.
- Review official social posts and website updates about your game before they go out, and give feedback.
- Budget development time for dev-made promotion such as reveal trailers or an official info site, treating it as part of shipping the game.

## Avoid
- Assuming a good game will be found on its own. With thousands of releases a year, it won't be.
- Flooding your personal social accounts with announcements. It can rub people the wrong way.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — fighter reveal movies:** he calls them solid, helpful marketing tools.
- **Super Smash Bros. — the Smash Bros. Dojo site:** an earlier example of the director driving promotion.
- **Mr. Sakurai Presents:** his presentation videos are a form of marketing.
- **Steam, 2021:** an estimated 10,000+ games listed for sale in one year.

## Quote
> Development and marketing are multiplicative. The success of one is proportional to the success of the other. (01:56)
