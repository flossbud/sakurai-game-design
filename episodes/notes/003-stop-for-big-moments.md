# 003 · Stop for Big Moments!

**Category:** Design Specifics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OdVkEOzdCPw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/003-stop-for-big-moments.md)

## In one sentence
Briefly stop the action at your game's big moments (landing a hit, beating a boss, getting hit or dying) so players clearly feel the impact of their success or defeat.

## Key ideas
- "Hit stop" is a brief stop in the action, for example when you hit an opponent. Whether it's there or not "will completely change how heavy a hit feels."
- "Boss stop" is his own term for the stop when you defeat a boss: the screen stops and flashes for a moment. He used it as far back as Kirby's Adventure on the NES, and calls it "vital for letting the player really feel that impact." Smash Ultimate's Finish Zoom extends the same idea and is closer to boss stop.
- Stops work for defeat as well as success. The action also stops when Kirby loses a life.
- He thinks stops "should be used all over the place, regardless of genre." In many scrolling shoot 'em ups you can die without realizing it. If the player's reaction is "Wait, I got hit?", the game has only told them something went wrong, and that's "a real missed opportunity."
- Fantasy Zone (1986) stops the screen for a second the moment you're hit. It's "just a pause," but you clearly see the point of impact and get that "oh no!" feeling. Defender (1980), which Fantasy Zone took cues from, has a stop built into its hit effect.
- A stop is one tool among several. He shows shooting-game footage with no effects, then with screen stop, screen shake and slow motion added. Stops are "relatively easy" to implement, so there's little reason to skip them.

## Do this
- Add hit stop: briefly freeze the action when an attack connects. Compare builds with and without it to judge how heavy each hit feels.
- When a boss is defeated, freeze the screen and flash it for a moment ("boss stop") before the defeat plays out.
- Pause the game for an instant when the player takes a hit or loses a life, so the exact point of impact is visible.
- For the biggest moments, layer screen stop, screen shake and slow motion together.
- During playtests, listen for "Wait, I got hit?" Any death that confuses players needs a stop.

## Avoid
- Letting players die without realizing what happened, which is common in scrolling shoot 'em ups.
- Skimping on stop effects because they seem like minor polish.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Finish Zoom:** an extension of stop effects, close to boss stop.
- **Kirby's Adventure (NES) — boss defeat and losing a life:** the screen stops and flashes when a boss falls, and the action stops when Kirby loses a life.
- **Fantasy Zone (1986) — getting hit:** the screen stops for a second, so the point of impact is clear.
- **Defender (1980) — getting hit:** the stop is part of the hit's visual effect.
- **Shooting games (comparison footage):** no effects, then stop, shake and slow motion added.

## Quote
> In your game's big moments, stop! Let your players feel those moments of success—or defeat. (03:35)
