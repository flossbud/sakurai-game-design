# 061 · Don't Anchor Your Center Point

**Category:** Design Specifics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kLmd2F8LaLk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/061-dont-anchor-your-center-point.md)

## In one sentence
Don't lock the camera's center point to the character: let the character move off center, especially vertically, so players can feel their jumps, dodges and drifts.

## Key ideas
- Every 3D game camera focuses on a "center point," which is usually invisible. Cameras can rotate tightly around the character because the center point is often set to the character's position, anchored to it. Sakurai's advice: don't anchor it.
- In Super Mario 64, Mario stays at screen center while walking, but when he jumps his position on screen moves up. It's obvious when you look for it, but not in normal play.
- Smash Bros. Ultimate with the camera set to track the fighter shows what anchoring does: it's very hard to tell how high Mario is jumping. So the center point shouldn't track the Y axis, or should at least track it less, so players feel the height of their jumps.
- An anchored center point can make it hard to see how the character is moving. It may still be right for some titles, such as third-person racing games with realistic precision, but good framing improves presentation.
- In Armored Core, moving in any direction moves your AC away from screen center, which makes it easier to see the bullets and missiles you dodged, so dodging has more impact.
- In Kirby Air Ride, the machine moves slightly away from the center point during a drift, so players really feel the input and the sense of careening past walls.
- Side-scrollers that want a wider field of view move the camera to show more of the screen ahead. Don't assume the camera should always follow the character; make it fit the gameplay and presentation.

## Do this
- Stop tracking the camera's center point on the Y axis during jumps, or track it at a reduced rate, so the character visibly rises on screen.
- Let the character drift off center in the direction of quick moves and dodges so players can see what they just avoided.
- During signature actions like drifts, offset the character or vehicle slightly from the center point to make the input felt.
- In side-scrollers, shift the camera to show more space ahead in the direction of travel.
- Keep a fully anchored center point only where it clearly fits, such as precise, realistic third-person racing.

## Avoid
- Anchoring the camera's center point to the character position by default.
- Full vertical tracking that hides how high the character jumps.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Mario 64 — jumping:** Mario stays centered while walking but rises off center when he jumps.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — tracking camera:** with the center point anchored to the fighter, jump height becomes hard to read.
- **Armored Core — movement:** the AC moves off center, making dodged bullets and missiles visible and impactful.
- **Kirby Air Ride — drifting:** the machine shifts slightly from center to emphasize the input and the feeling of careening past walls.

## Quote
> Don't assume the camera should always be following your character, but instead make sure it fits your gameplay and presentation. (02:30)
