# 045 · That Was Then, This Is Now

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CVQ4PuihnTk) · [Transcript](../transcripts/045-that-was-then-this-is-now.md)

## In one sentence
Don't hold modern games to nostalgic standards: fun is relative to its time and circumstances, so keep trying new things and enjoy the present.

## Key ideas
- Many people think games were better in the old days: more memorable music, fresh titles instead of sequels, a competition of ideas full of surprises, rapid hardware and software progress, mystery and fantasy, and adventures built on your own trial and error rather than YouTube videos or wikis.
- Some people saw no appeal in modern games and quit the hobby, and some only talk about old games and use them as the standard for all others.
- But that was then and this is now: the past is gone, and the present will also be out of reach one day, so enjoy it to the fullest.
- The fun of a game isn't absolute but relative. The year you play, your personal situation, current trends and technology all greatly affect how much fun you have.
- Old games have a certain charm and inventiveness, but in total fun and breadth of play Sakurai doesn't think they measure up to today's more refined, larger titles; try an old game next to a recent one to see. Older games arguably had more room to experiment because design theory wasn't yet settled.
- Works we meet when young leave a deep imprint, so it's a shame to bounce off modern games because they can't match those memories. There are now plenty of ways to play old games, and it's better to keep trying new things.

## Do this
- Play recent games regularly, including ones outside your usual favorites, instead of measuring everything against games from your youth.
- Before claiming an old game is better, replay it today next to a recent one rather than relying on memory.
- When judging how fun a game is, account for context: the year, your personal situation, current trends and the technology of the time.

## Avoid
- Treating old games as the standard all other games are held to.
- Dropping modern games because they can't match special memories.

## Examples from the episode
- **Old vs. modern discovery:** past adventures relied on your own trial and error; today YouTube videos and wikis spell everything out.

## Quote
> The fun of a game isn't based in absolutes. It's more of a relative thing. (01:34)
