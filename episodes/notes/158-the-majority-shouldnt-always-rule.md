# 158 · The Majority Shouldn't Always Rule

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dP94K69NSVc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/158-the-majority-shouldnt-always-rule.md)

## In one sentence
Don't settle creative decisions by majority vote. Give them to the person who holds the big picture, and make sure that person has all the information they need to decide well.

## Key ideas
- When opinions split over design details or specs, it's tempting to vote, but Sakurai says there's "essentially no point to it". The ideal is for the person with the big-picture idea of the project to decide its direction.
- More people wanting something doesn't make it right in creative work. Ideas everyone opposed during planning often turn out fun once they're actually built. Metal Gear Solid 3's wound-patching system reportedly had only four people on the team behind the proposal at first.
- Majority decisions converge on the safest ideas, which means the most mediocre. That can sometimes be the right call for team management, but for a game, having a key figure like the director call the shots usually turns out best.
- The person with that power has to keep a balanced view. Barking irrational orders with paper-thin reasoning turns direction into dictatorship. Good decisions need good information, so the team should share everything on their minds, problems and possible solutions alike. Anyone can make the wrong call without transparency.
- Sakurai still polls his team sometimes, out of curiosity. For the Mii Fighter's maid outfit he leaned toward A or B, asked the team, and found the spread of opinions interesting. They went with A, but he made a director's call to color the socks white, because brighter extremities make a character's movement easier to see.

## Do this
- When the team is split on a design question, have the director (whoever holds the whole-game vision) make the call and explain why, instead of counting votes.
- Before a big decision, ask everyone to put problems and candidate solutions on the table, so the decision-maker isn't working blind.
- Don't kill an unpopular idea in planning by headcount. If the person with the vision believes in it, build it and judge it in the game.
- Use a team poll as input when you're curious about reactions, but keep the final decision and any adjustments with the director.
- Make a character's extremities (hands, feet, socks) brighter so their movement reads at a glance.

## Avoid
- Settling creative direction by majority vote. It converges on the safest, most mediocre option.
- Leading by decree with paper-thin reasoning. That's dictatorship, not direction.
- Keeping problems or ideas from the person who has to decide.

## Examples from the episode
- **Metal Gear Solid 3 — wound treatment system:** only four people on the team initially agreed with the proposal, yet fixing up injured areas turned out to be fun in the finished game.
- **Super Smash Bros. — Mii Fighter maid outfit:** Sakurai polled the team on rough designs out of curiosity (he leaned toward A or B). The option they went with was A, and as director he then made the socks white for movement readability.

## Quote
> Ideas decided by majority end up being the safest ideas. In other words, the most mediocre. (01:01)
