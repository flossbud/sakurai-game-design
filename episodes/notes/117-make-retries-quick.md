# 117 · Make Retries Quick

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oSi0wEJHLuA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/117-make-retries-quick.md)

## In one sentence
Cut the time between the player's "Ugh, I blew it!" and getting control back to a minimum, with a punchy failure effect of about a second, because fast retries make players tolerate much more difficulty.

## Key ideas
- Depending on the game's goals and essence, restarts should generally be very quick, so players try again instead of giving up. Limit the time when players aren't feeling anything, tighten the presentation and keep load times short.
- Penalties and how much of the game a player must replay also affect whether they stick with it, but speed matters most. Quick retries raise the player's tolerance for difficulty and hardship.
- The critical window runs from the moment the player thinks "Ugh, I blew it!" to the moment they actually have control again. Interest cools fast during that window, and the longer the wait, the more they lose interest. If you hand back control before they give up, they get right back to playing, and once they get a bit further they find the motivation to keep going.
- The game moving on its own isn't enough to keep players engaged; they have to be in control. Even pressing buttons to read through messages helps, but nothing beats getting back into the game.
- There's a middle ground: infinite respawns right where the player died can make the game feel mindless.
- The loss should really be felt. Make the effect flashy and impactful but short, about a second depending on genre, then return control as soon as possible.
- Don't let enemies bombard players the moment they respawn. If players keep losing without even touching the controller, they won't want to try anymore.

## Do this
- Time your build from the moment of death to the moment the player can act again, then cut it: trim the death sequence, shorten loads and remove non-interactive screens.
- Make the failure effect flashy and impactful but keep it to about one second, then hand back control.
- If some non-gameplay step must remain, make it player-driven (button presses advance messages) rather than auto-playing.
- Place respawn points where enemies can't hit the player right away.
- Keep some real cost to failure (checkpoint distance or a penalty) so respawning never feels mindless.

## Avoid
- Long death animations, slow fades and loading between failure and retry.
- Infinite respawns exactly where the player died, which makes the game feel mindless.
- Respawning players into enemy attacks so they lose again before touching the controller.
