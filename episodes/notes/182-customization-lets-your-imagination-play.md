# 182 · Customization Lets Your Imagination Play

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rwE-EEn3hYU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/182-customization-lets-your-imagination-play.md)

## In one sentence
When customization is part of the fun, make the work of customizing fun as well: show each option's effect, make the trade-offs clear, make choosing fast, let players test right away and make every part look cool.

## Key ideas
- Some games make customization part of the fun, and it's especially common in robot games. The same points apply to weapon and skill selection. Because the work players put into customizing is a big part of the appeal, the work itself has to be fun.
- It's like making anything: Sakurai imagines how people will react as he adds things, because making something good means thinking about how others will respond.
- With deep customization, care about the process and the UI players go through, not just the end result. It's still work and can feel like a chore, so make it clear what the work will get them.
- He lists five points:
  1. **Illustrate the effect.** For example, show the attack range. Many games show attack power with numbers alone, but there are more intuitive ways to make players feel the difference.
  2. **Make pros and cons clear.** One weapon might have a wide spread but short range, and another less spread but longer reach. Markedly distinct options with meaningful differences set a game's customization apart.
  3. **Make it easy to pick what to customize.** Lots of items is good for collecting, but players pile up a lot of stuff. Sort or categorize items, make sure weapons, parts and icons are easy to tell apart by sight, use the UI itself to speed up choosing, and include a way to automatically equip the strongest gear, which he calls essential.
  4. **Let players try things quickly.** Ideally they can test a customization immediately, and it's even more effective if they can test changes on the fly.
  5. **Make the designs cool.** Nobody wants to use something that doesn't look good. Even with no special abilities involved, changing your look can be fun, and some customization systems are built entirely around that. "Cute" can stand in for "cool" here.
- Customization offers a different kind of fun from pure game essence. Think carefully about both what is customized and how.

## Do this
- Show each part's effect visually on the customization screen, for example by drawing its attack range, instead of showing only a stat number.
- Give every option a clear trade-off (such as wide spread with short range versus narrow spread with long range) and show the pros and cons side by side.
- Add sorting and categories, make each weapon or part's icon clearly different from the others, and include a one-press "equip strongest" option.
- Let players test a build immediately, ideally by swapping parts on the fly without leaving play.
- Make sure every part and outfit looks cool or cute, and consider cosmetic-only options as well.

## Avoid
- Showing the differences between parts only as numbers.
- Options without meaningful differences, which make the choice pointless.
- Icons and parts so similar that players can't tell them apart.
- A customization process that feels like a chore because players can't see what the work will get them.
