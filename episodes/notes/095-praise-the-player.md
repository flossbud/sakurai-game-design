# 095 · Praise the Player!

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fryDyXROp8A) · [Transcript](../transcripts/095-praise-the-player.md)

## In one sentence
Go out of your way to praise players when they do something good—with crowds, allies, bonuses or celebratory effects—because items and progress are expected rewards that don't, on their own, tell players "well done."

## Key ideas
- Many of Sakurai's games include cheering. In Smash Bros. the crowd reacts when you send an opponent flying, when you KO someone and on the victory screen, and gives a subtle "oh!" when you barely grab a ledge. In Kirby Air Ride, people clap along to the dance music at the end of a stage.
- Games are meant to be fun, so praising players for doing something good matters.
- Items and progress count as praise in a sense, but they "don't quite cut it." Players expect them in any game, and they aren't there specifically to say the player did well.
- Other games do this too. Dynasty Warriors' allied commanders praise you based on things like how many enemies you've defeated. In Forza almost everything earns a bonus, whether you drive well or wreck your car. These work, so don't hold back.
- Smash Bros. Ultimate added confetti to the KO explosion. That can look odd for the player who made the mistake, but it's a colorful way to praise the player who scored the KO.
- Don't express praise in the most obvious way. Match how the player is feeling or heighten that feeling, directly or indirectly.

## Do this
- Add audience or spectator reactions tied to player skill: cheers for big launches and KOs, and a quieter gasp for near-misses and close saves (like barely catching a ledge).
- Have allies or NPCs comment approvingly on what the player achieves, triggered by measurable feats such as how many enemies they've defeated.
- Attach small labeled bonuses to many actions, including clumsy ones, so the player keeps getting acknowledged.
- Put a celebratory flourish (confetti, color bursts) on the moment one player scores against another, aimed at the scorer.
- For each praise moment, ask what the player is feeling right then and design the reaction to heighten it, not just announce "Good!"

## Avoid
- Counting item drops and progression as your only praise. Players expect them, so they don't say "well done."

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — crowd:** cheers on big launches, KOs and the victory screen, plus a subtle "oh!" when someone barely grabs a ledge.
- **Kirby Air Ride — stage end:** spectators clap along to the dance music.
- **Dynasty Warriors — allied commanders:** praise the player based on things like enemies defeated.
- **Forza — bonuses:** almost every action earns some kind of bonus, even wrecking your car.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — KO confetti:** confetti in the KO explosion praises the player who landed the KO.
