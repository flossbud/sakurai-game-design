# 023 · Keeping Rewards in Sight

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KePsEzN-IAM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/023-keeping-rewards-in-sight.md)

## In one sentence
Keep the next reward visible and within reach by showing how far players have come and how far they have left, so they always have a clear objective pulling them forward.

## Key ideas
- Rewards and prizes are an obvious motivation to keep playing. "I do think it's a shame when a game doesn't keep you thinking about the next reward."
- Ys (Nihon Falcom, 1987, on home computers; shown here in the TurboGrafx-16 version) has a small main game screen, probably to reduce processor load since drawing was slow back then. That leaves room for information, including a display of your current EXP and how much you need to level up.
- That number rises with every enemy you defeat, which keeps you pushing toward the next level. "It shows how far you've come, and how far you have left to go."
- Keeping rewards in sight and giving a clear objective both motivate players.
- Faxanadu, a game related to Nihon Falcom, has only a few shop items, all expensive, but each gives "a pretty massive boost" once you can afford it. Players scrounge for money, thinking about how much more they need for the next big piece of gear, "a real plus for the game."
- Today's games are generous with money and usually have lots of equipment, so this kind of fun "isn't as easy as it used to be." Still, placing objectives within reach and letting players feel the joy of completing them is worth learning from. "Looking back now, I wish I'd done more of it in my games, actually!"

## Do this
- Show progress toward the next reward as current versus required (for example, EXP now and EXP needed for the next level) on the main screen.
- Make every enemy defeated or coin collected visibly move that counter.
- Make key purchases few, expensive and powerful, so saving up for the next one becomes a goal in itself.
- At several points in a playthrough, check what the player is working toward right now. If the answer is unclear or out of reach, add a nearer objective.

## Avoid
- Hiding how close the player is to the next reward.
- Handing out so much money and so much minor equipment that no single purchase is worth saving for.

## Examples from the episode
- **Ys (Nihon Falcom, 1987; TurboGrafx-16 version) — EXP display:** shows current EXP and the amount needed to level up.
- **Faxanadu — shops:** a few expensive items that each give a massive boost, so players scrounge toward them.

## Quote
> It shows how far you've come, and how far you have left to go. Keeping rewards in sight. (00:56)
