# 167 · Strong Attack, Light Reverb

**Category:** Audio · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nWjT49-cds) · [Transcript](../transcripts/167-strong-attack-light-reverb.md)

## In one sentence
Shape impact sounds for busy action: reach a strong level almost instantly and keep the reverb tail short, so every hit registers right away and doesn't bury the next one.

## Key ideas
- A Smash Bros. impact sound's waveform is loud at the start, followed by a tail of reverberation. The pre-check audio Sakurai gets from the sound team generally looks different, so he explains what he wants and it's revised into the final sound.
- Smash can have four or even eight players, so sound effects play constantly and audio easily gets drowned out. To keep impact "no matter the situation", he focuses on two adjustments.
- First, a strong attack: make the start of the sound stronger, which means a shorter attack and a peak at the beginning. An impact sound must never feel late, because players need a reaction to their attack right away. It can't simply start at peak volume and still sound right, but it has to reach a strong sound almost immediately.
- Second, light reverb: echo makes a sound feel stronger, but if new sounds play while it's still ringing, they get lost in the mix. Battles move fast, and with too many overlapping sounds you can't tell where each one starts; everything gets "buried in a sea of noise". Light, rapid hits like jabs should be pared down to just long enough to still feel like consecutive hits.
- Strong attacks get hitstop (the screen freezes right after the hit). The sound keeps playing during the freeze, but ideally its main part is over before the action resumes.
- The policy is "strong attack, light reverb", though it depends on the game. The goal isn't just "good" sounds but the right type of sounds for your game.

## Do this
- Edit hit sounds so they reach a strong level within the first moments (a short attack, not a literal start at peak), so the reaction lands with the hit.
- Trim the reverb tails on impact sounds in multiplayer or busy scenes so each hit finishes before the next one starts.
- Cut rapid multi-hit sounds (jabs, flurries) down to the shortest length that still reads as separate, consecutive hits.
- For heavy hits with hitstop, time the sound so its main body plays out during the freeze and is done when action resumes.
- Review impact sounds in a crowded mix (several players attacking at once), not only one at a time.

## Avoid
- Impact sounds that feel like they arrive late.
- Long, echoing tails that sound powerful on their own but turn the mix into noise.
- Picking sounds because they're "good" in isolation rather than right for your game.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — impact sound waveforms:** a loud start followed by a tail of reverberation. The sound team's pre-check versions are revised toward "strong attack, light reverb".
- **Super Smash Bros. — rapid jabs vs. strong attacks:** jab sounds are shortened to stay distinct, and strong-hit sounds are timed to finish during hitstop.
