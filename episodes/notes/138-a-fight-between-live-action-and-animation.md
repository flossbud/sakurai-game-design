# 138 · A Fight Between Live Action and Animation

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=luB-5GqW55Q) · [Transcript](../transcripts/138-a-fight-between-live-action-and-animation.md)

## In one sentence
When characters from different art styles share one screen, pull every one of them toward a common middle (color, texture detail, proportions, design detail and real-world believability) so no one looks like they came from a different medium.

## Key ideas
- Copying each character's official design exactly would produce "an incohesive visual style." Mario in Brawl, for example, got a wide range of material textures such as denim, with Nintendo's permission. Sakurai lists five adjustments.
- **1. Color:** Bright red, blue and yellow are rendered slightly paler and made more responsive to light sources. The reason: in games, a bright red looks roughly the same under any lighting, which clashes with characters who use more subdued palettes.
- **2. Texture density:** Cartoony characters usually have single-color fills and realistic ones have rich textures. To bridge the gap, cartoony characters get more detail and realistic ones have their textures eased back.
- **3. Proportions:** Body styles vary so much that some matchups would look ridiculous, so proportions are tweaked slightly for consistency. Bayonetta looks quite different from her original when compared side by side, but "it doesn't feel strange when incorporated as part of the game."
- **4. Refined design detail:** Olimar's suit is very different from the original. R.O.B.'s added detail and information make it feel more modern. The Ice Climbers' boots have spikes on the soles and what looks like snow. Because every fighter gets this treatment, "Everyone is treated the same, so it doesn't feel strange."
- **5. Believability:** In their own games, Donkey Kong's and Yoshi's pupils shut comic-book style. For Smash, Sakurai got permission to have their eyelids close instead, and Donkey Kong's teeth fit together a little differently. The team asks how a character would look and behave if they really existed, and fills in details from that perspective.
- Sakurai often tells his artists, "Don't make it look like a fight between live action and animation." The same unification applies to animation, effects and audio.

## Do this
- Slightly desaturate pure primary colors on stylized characters and let them respond more strongly to scene lighting, so they don't clash with characters who use subdued palettes.
- Meet in the middle on surface detail: add material texture (e.g., fabric weave) to flat-color cartoon characters and tone down the texture intensity on realistic ones.
- Line up all characters side by side in-game and make small proportion tweaks where a matchup looks ridiculous. Judge the result in gameplay, not against the original artwork.
- Give every character the same refinement pass (extra detail and information on simple designs) so no one stands out as treated differently.
- For each stylized feature (eyes, teeth, mouths), ask how it would work if the character really existed. For example, close the eyelids rather than shrinking the pupils. Get the license holder's permission for changes like these.
- Run the same consistency check on animation, effects and audio, not just models.

## Avoid
- Importing each character's official design as-is, which yields an incohesive style.
- Leaving bright primary colors flat and unaffected by light next to realistically shaded characters.

## Examples from the episode
- **Mario (Smash Bros. Brawl):** given a wide array of textures, such as denim.
- **Bayonetta:** proportions changed noticeably from her original, yet it doesn't feel strange in-game.
- **Olimar:** suit very different from his original look.
- **R.O.B.:** added detail and information make him feel more modern.
- **Ice Climbers:** spikes on their boot soles and snow.
- **Donkey Kong and Yoshi:** eyelids close instead of comic-style pupils, and Donkey Kong's teeth fit together differently.
