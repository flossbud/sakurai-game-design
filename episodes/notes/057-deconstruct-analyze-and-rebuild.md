# 057 · Deconstruct, Analyze, and Rebuild

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VD0K1Sr5u0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/057-deconstruct-analyze-and-rebuild.md)

## In one sentence
To make something original, don't copy what's fun: deconstruct which part is fun, analyze why (usually risk and reward), and rebuild that element into your own mechanic.

## Key ideas
- Copying something fun won't make your game feel original. Sakurai deconstructs why a game is fun, then rebuilds those elements into a unique work.
- Example: a racing game. Pursuing realism leaves little room for creativity, so assume something more fanciful. **Deconstruct:** start from the assumption that drifting is fun. In a real car drifting is slower than grip driving, but making drifting itself fun is a great way to make the game fun.
- **Analyze:** drifting is fun because it has risk and reward. You lose control with nothing but walls ahead, which is dangerous, but if you brake well and clear the curve, you come out with an advantage.
- He tests reward options. A score bonus has no tangible benefit. Permanent power-ups let skilled drifters run away with the race. A speed boost works, since some speed is needed so the risk is rewarded.
- **Rebuild:** give the speed after the curve is cleared, not during it. Then combine drifting with charging up power: braking starts the charge, the charge continues through the drift, and releasing at the end gives a burst of speed. That's the thinking behind Kirby Air Ride, a racing game you can play with one button. He didn't make it one-button to stand out; the process led there.
- His hobby is driving, but he doesn't take cues from it; he designs what he thinks will be fun as a game. Succeeding requires both examining the fun in a game and coming up with ideas to raise it, and practicing that process deepens your understanding.

## Do this
- Pick a mechanic you find fun and write one sentence on why it's fun in terms of risk and reward.
- List possible rewards for it (score, permanent power-up, temporary speed) and reject any that have no tangible benefit or let skilled players snowball out of reach.
- Pay out the reward once the risky action is completed (at the exit of the curve), not while it's happening.
- Try fusing the mechanic with another concept, such as charging up power, to find a new verb: brake to start charging, hold through the drift, release for a burst.
- When realism and fun conflict, choose fun; make the risky action rewarding even if it wouldn't be in real life.

## Avoid
- Copying a fun game wholesale and expecting it to feel original.
- Rewards with no tangible benefit (a score bonus for drifting) or permanent power-ups that let skilled players run away with the race.
- Boosting speed throughout the drift instead of after the curve.

## Examples from the episode
- **Kirby Air Ride — charge drifting:** brake to start charging, drift, and release at the end of the curve for a burst of speed; the result is a racer you can play with one button.
- **Real-world drifting vs. grip driving:** drifting is slower in reality, but in a game it can be made the fun part.
