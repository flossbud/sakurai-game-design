# 027 · Super Smash Bros.

**Category:** Game Concepts · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3IOWaVDbx0) · [Transcript](../transcripts/027-super-smash-bros.md)

## In one sentence
Build a concept by naming a real problem in an existing genre and designing core rules that solve it, then prove the idea with a playable prototype.

## Key ideas
- In 1996, after Kirby Super Star, Sakurai taught himself 3D on a Silicon Graphics machine running Softimage, then proposed two Nintendo 64 games: a four-player free-for-all fighter and an RC robot adventure where you hacked security cameras. He built prototypes of both (he did design, graphics, modeling and animation; Mr. Iwata programmed; one other person did audio), and Nintendo praised both.
- The fighter prototype, "Dragon King: The Fighting Game," had no special moves, dodges or items, but already used Smash's rules: smash attacks, midair jumps, shields, dashes, five-direction aerial attacks and a Battlefield-style layout.
- Schedule chose the project. The rest of the team was busy (Mother 3 for the 64DD, a snowboarding-like Air Ride, unannounced titles); when those fell through they needed a finished game fast, and the RC game would have taken two years, so they made the fighter.
- Calling Smash an "antithesis" to fighting games isn't a rejection: fighting games have great push and pull. But he felt combos had none: after the first hit the outcome depended on stringing attacks, the opponent's skill became irrelevant, and newcomers just watched themselves get beaten up. Wanting interplay and improvisation led to accumulated damage: opponents react differently to every hit, and getting knocked off-screen loses no matter how much damage you have.
- Command inputs had become extremely complicated by 1996, so he chose simple controls (a stick direction plus a button) and the smash input, a quick flick of the analog stick for a stronger attack or a jump. He focused on the time it took to complete an analog input rather than on its analog range.
- Nintendo's characters weren't in the original proposal. They were negotiated later to fix a console fighting-game problem: 8 or even 12 unknown main characters arriving at once (Zelda, by contrast, starts with one main character, and arcades let people grow attached by watching others play). Sales staff and wholesalers rejected the idea of Nintendo characters fighting before they played it, and traditional fighting-game fans' preconceptions were hard to break, so he made tools like Smash Bros. DOJO!! to bridge the gap.
- It was his first game with voice actors and fixed Kirby's official voice. Its flexible rules (chaotic four-player battles with items on, or tense 1-on-1 with items off) started the series' philosophy of "playing what you want, how you want."

## Do this
- Write down the specific problem your concept solves in its genre (e.g., "after the first hit, the opponent can't act") and design a core rule that removes it (e.g., accumulated damage with varying knockback, ring-outs to win).
- Build a playable prototype of just the core rules (no specials, dodges or items needed) before full production, and use it to sell the idea.
- When choosing between concepts, estimate development time for each, and pick the one you can finish if the team needs a game soon.
- Map moves to a stick direction plus one button, and use input timing (a quick flick) for stronger variants instead of long command strings.
- Plan first impressions for a large cast: use characters players already know, or create ways for players to grow attached to new ones.
- Make rules configurable (items on/off, player count, stage) so different groups can play the way they want.

## Avoid
- Systems where the outcome is decided after the first hit (long combos), leaving the opponent nothing to do.
- Complicated command inputs that new players can't pick up intuitively.
- Launching with a roster of 8–12 unfamiliar main characters and no reason for players to care about them.

## Examples from the episode
- **Dragon King: The Fighting Game (prototype):** Smash's rules already worked without special moves, dodges or items.
- **RC robot adventure (prototype):** promising, but would take two years, so it lost to the faster-to-finish fighter.
- **Super Smash Bros. — accumulated damage:** opponents react differently to each hit, and a ring-out decides the stock regardless of damage.
- **Super Smash Bros. — smash input:** a quick flick of the N64 analog stick triggers a stronger attack or a jump.
- **The Legend of Zelda:** one main character, Link, to build publicity around, versus a fighter's 8–12 at launch.
- **Smash Bros. DOJO!!:** one of his solutions to bridge the gap with players who had traditional fighting games in mind.
