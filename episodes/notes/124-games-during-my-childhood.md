# 124 · Games During My Childhood

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDVaOyzO5pY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/124-games-during-my-childhood.md)

## In one sentence
What we love as children shapes our tastes as adults, so remember your game may be shaping someone's childhood, and that direct control of a bright, flashy screen is what leaves a lasting impression.

## Key ideas
- His first game memory is Pong-style games that used a potentiometer dial: one twist moved objects directly on screen, and the games were bright and flashy. That made a strong impression on young Sakurai.
- Space Invaders mania hit Japan around 1978, when he was 7 or 8. As the fad faded, arcade games were everywhere.
- On most Sundays his family shopped at the local Chujitsuya supermarket. His parents gave him 200 yen, enough for four 50-yen games, so he was very particular about which games got a coin. Then he played the consoles on display at the hobby shop until his parents finished shopping.
- In nearby Tachikawa, department stores held surprise game tournaments and RC car races with no sign-up. He joined them and sometimes came home with both arms full of prizes; he was pretty good at games. Stores also let him try PCs and PC games he couldn't afford; he didn't own a PC until high school.
- Nihon Falcom's development office was built into a PC store in Tachikawa, so you could see into it from the shop floor. That may have been his first in-person look at game development.
- The Famicom (1983) had a massive impact, but lots of hardware already existed, and games were being made for arcades, consoles and PCs. Every year brought big technical advances, which made the era electrifying.
- The things we love as kids, and the joy they bring, strongly shape our adult tastes; as a creator he may have had that effect on someone else's childhood. In a 2019 survey by CoroCoro Comic, Japan's most popular children's magazine, video games were the top hobby and Super Smash Bros. Ultimate was the top game. He wonders how kids who start with today's advanced games feel.

## Do this
- Make the player's first moments of control direct and immediate on a bright, eye-catching screen, the combination that made such a lasting impression on young Sakurai.
- Remember that children may be among your players and that what they love now can shape their tastes for life; design their experience with that in mind.

## Examples from the episode
- **Pong (and other potentiometer games):** twisting a dial moved objects directly on a bright, flashy screen.
- **Space Invaders:** the craze of around 1978 that filled Japan with arcade games.
- **Nihon Falcom:** its development office was visible inside a Tachikawa PC store.
- **Famicom (1983):** a huge impact, arriving amid many other hardware releases.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate:** the most popular game in CoroCoro Comic's 2019 survey.

## Quote
> I'd say that the things we love as kids and the joy those things bring us go on to have a huge impact on our tastes in adulthood. (04:02)
