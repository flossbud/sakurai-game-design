# 196 · Crafting “Similar” Games

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BO_q72ug1eA) · [Transcript](../transcripts/196-crafting-similar-games.md)

## In one sentence
It's fine to start by imitating a game you admire, but by the end of development your game needs many kinds of appeal the original lacks, or players will just play the original.

## Key ideas
- Making something similar to a game you admire is common, especially among indies. Sakurai thinks that's good: the industry grew and was refined through exactly that process. Above all, making any game beats making none, and finishing and releasing a game deserves praise in itself.
- If you're making an homage, remember that people might be better off playing the original. You're using it as a reference because it's compelling, and everyone else can tell it's fun too, so it's normal for players to decide to stick with it.
- The original may be so fun it leaves people wanting more, but you shouldn't just ride its coattails.
- An homage needs, at minimum, appeal the original doesn't have. Not just one thing, but lots of them.
- Designing the systems is the hardest part of making games. If you skip it by borrowing systems, you have to make up for that elsewhere.
- If you work hard to pack in plenty of unique appeal, your game develops an originality of its own.
- It isn't about whether you surpass the original. Appeal has many facets and can't be measured on a single scale. Imitating is fine when you start out, but by the end you should aim to make something players can't get anywhere else.

## Do this
- Before committing to a game inspired by another, write a list of reasons a player would pick yours over the original, and aim for many reasons, not just one.
- Because you're borrowing the original's systems, put the effort you saved into extra appeal that the original lacks.
- Use imitation only as a starting point, and check again near the end of development that the game offers things players can't get anywhere else.
- Compare your game with the original across several dimensions of appeal instead of trying to "beat" it on one.

## Avoid
- Riding on the original's coattails with a near-copy.
- Relying on a single point of difference to justify an homage.

## Examples from the episode
- **Indie homages (in general):** games made out of admiration for an existing title, which Sakurai encourages as long as they add their own appeal.

## Quote
> But by the end of development, you should strive to make something that players can't get anywhere else. (01:42)
