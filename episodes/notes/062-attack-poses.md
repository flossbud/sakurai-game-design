# 062 · Attack Poses

**Category:** Animation · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99gdMDF7V2E) · [Transcript](../transcripts/062-attack-poses.md)

## In one sentence
Make the pose that freezes during hit stop exaggerated, asymmetric, twisted and clear in silhouette from the game's viewing angle, because it's the most memorable moment of an attack.

## Key ideas
- In games about trading blows, attack animations matter most to how fun the game feels. This episode focuses on the "hit" pose at the moment an attack lands.
- Smash Bros. adds hit stop the instant an attack connects, so the pose at that moment tends to be the most memorable. That's why Sakurai almost always includes that moment in his animation reference photos.
- **General rules:** exaggerate, so the motion reads even from far away. Keep attacks centered, because if they stray toward or away from the screen, hits may not register correctly. Don't make poses completely symmetrical unless it's for comic effect or otherwise intentional. Avoid simply bending the torso sideways; a little twist makes the pose much more interesting.
- For sword swings, start hit stop once the sword has swung out a bit where possible; stopping before the swing is complete loses some of the "hit" feeling. Check poses from several perspectives, since a pose looks different on the left of the screen than on the right.
- **Extra important:** each pose needs a distinct silhouette, with all four limbs clearly visible. Smash is viewed from the side, so profile silhouettes come before everything else.
- One of Pyra's poses as submitted, compared with Sakurai's correction, shows that a minor tweak to the silhouette makes a huge difference. Much of this is case by case, and playing around with physical objects helps you get a feel for it.

## Do this
- Treat each attack's hit-stop frame as a key pose and review it on its own, including it in your animation reference.
- Exaggerate hit poses until they read from the farthest camera distance you use.
- Keep the attack in the gameplay plane rather than angled toward or away from the camera, so the hit registers.
- Break symmetry and twist the torso instead of a plain sideways bend.
- For sword attacks, trigger hit stop after the blade has swung out a bit.
- Check each pose's silhouette from the game's main viewpoint (side profile in a side-view game), mirrored for both screen sides, and make sure all four limbs are readable.

## Avoid
- Perfectly symmetrical poses, unless they're deliberately comic.
- Plain sideways torso bends with no twist.
- Starting hit stop before a sword has swung out, which loses the "hit" feeling.
- Attacks that drift toward or away from the screen and may not register.
