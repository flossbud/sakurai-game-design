# 232 · Manga-like Visuals

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9lQpn67Wos) · [Transcript](../transcripts/232-manga-like-visuals.md)

## In one sentence
Borrow manga and anime's non-realistic conventions, like putting the mouth on the side of the face in profile, whenever they express a character better than realism would.

## Key ideas
- This isn't about cel-shading. Manga and anime have many expressive conventions games can use, such as showing a character's eyes through the frame of their glasses or drawing the mouth on the side of a face in profile.
- In Mega Man Legends cutscenes, a character's mouth moves to the side of the face in profile shots. It isn't natural, but it fits the anime style. It also lets the mouth move by swapping cels without animating the jaw, which is easier to produce and may even make mouth movement easier to read.
- Sonic also has his mouth moved to the side. It's strange if you think about it too much, but it's a great way of expressing the character.
- Manga-like visuals can mean more work in CG. Tom and Jerry squeezing and stretching through narrow spaces is hard to do well in CG; shape animation might be one way.
- Lack of realism is no reason to avoid these techniques. Sakurai recommends them and says we may be better off welcoming the fun expression they bring.
- In Smash Bros. Ultimate, Kirby and Jigglypuff were first modeled with an older style of mouth. He asked "time and time again" to remove the whiskers, but it didn't happen. He requested a new mouth design, and the final models are close to it. A true profile would look different, but his version is more expressive.
- You can't fix these things if you don't notice them, so don't take your givens as givens.

## Do this
- For characters shown in profile, try placing the mouth on the side of the face (anime-style) instead of an anatomically correct profile, and judge which reads better.
- For cheap lip-sync in profile shots, swap mouth textures or cels instead of animating a jaw.
- When a cartoon effect like squash-and-stretch through a gap is hard with a skeletal rig, try shape (blend-shape) animation.
- Review character models at the angles players actually see, and question any default anatomy that looks less expressive.

## Avoid
- Rejecting a technique only because it isn't realistic.
- Accepting inherited modeling conventions without questioning them.

## Examples from the episode
- **Mega Man Legends — profile shots in cutscenes:** the mouth sits on the side of the face and moves by cel swaps.
- **Sonic — mouth placement:** moved to the side of the face for expression.
- **Tom and Jerry — squeezing through gaps:** hard to reproduce in CG; shape animation might work.
- **Smash Bros. Ultimate — Kirby and Jigglypuff mouths:** remodeled to Sakurai's request so the profile is more expressive than a realistic one.

## Quote
> don't take your givens as givens, and instead look for a suitable—or even exaggerated—way of expressing your visuals. (02:44)
