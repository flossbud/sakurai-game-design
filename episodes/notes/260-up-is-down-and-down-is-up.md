# 260 · Up is Down, and Down is Up

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8ZZlnvNvBY) · [Transcript](../transcripts/260-up-is-down-and-down-is-up.md)

## In one sentence
A leader isn't above the team: a director's instructions flow downstream, but they are really requests made from the bottom up to the people doing the work, so treat everyone with respect and as an ally.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai wrote about this in his column before, and repeats it because it's an important rule.
- Being a director, producer or other leader doesn't make it your job to bark orders. You're in a position to ask for things as you guide the title to the finish, but you're no higher than anyone else.
- The people with ideas and proposals are powerless without others helping them. The director stands farthest upstream, and instructions travel down from there, but in reality the director is making requests from the bottom up to the team members who'll do the actual work.
- Think of customers and publishers. A publisher can't dictate that customers buy its game; the customer decides. The customer is downstream in distribution but upstream in choosing and valuing the game, and the "king" in that relationship is the customer.
- A dev team hired by a company all share a duty to do what the company asks, and in that nobody is higher or lower. Neither the director nor team members should act superior or subservient.
- Even if you are above someone, nobody likes a demanding customer; you'll just be resented. Whether you're the boss or a core member, show respect to those around you. Every team member is an ally working toward a better product, and an occasional disagreement doesn't make you enemies.
- Sakurai addresses team members politely, in both name and speaking style, even brand-new hires much younger than him, and is a little taken aback when others address new coworkers casually. With external companies, even higher-ups at Nintendo, he doesn't write very formally in emails.

## Do this
- Phrase your direction to the team as requests to the people who'll do the work, not as orders.
- Address every team member politely in name and tone, including new and much younger hires.
- Treat disagreements as normal between allies, and settle them without treating the other person as an enemy.
- If you're a team member rather than a lead, don't act subservient either; you and the director share the same duty to the project.

## Avoid
- Barking orders or acting like a demanding customer toward your team.
- Acting superior or subservient to anyone on the team.
- Addressing new coworkers casually just because they're junior.

## Examples from the episode
- **Customer and publisher:** the customer is downstream in distribution but upstream in deciding whether to buy, which is how a director should see the team.

## Quote
> Up is down, and down is up. Be sure to remember this, especially if you're in a leadership or directorial position. (02:53)
