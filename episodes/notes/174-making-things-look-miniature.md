# 174 · Making Things Look Miniature

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=omeFOX21-wc) · [Transcript](../transcripts/174-making-things-look-miniature.md)

## In one sentence
Shallow depth of field and high saturation make things look small, so to make large objects feel big, keep blur out of the frame, lower the saturation of big and distant objects, and judge scale on the whole final image.

## Key ideas
- In games the sense of scale often feels off, and large objects end up looking too small.
- The "miniature" photo trick shows why. Take an overhead landscape photo and blur the top and bottom, which gives it a shallow depth of field so it looks shot from up close. Then raise the saturation and, if possible, reduce the blue hues to remove the sense of aerial perspective. Those few steps make big things look small.
- Shallow depth of field seems like an easy way to add atmosphere, but outside of very specific spots it's usually better to avoid it. People focus on different parts of a composition, and trying to focus on something blurry can be physically uncomfortable.
- Saturation is how vivid colors are. It should be lower on large objects because they have aerial perspective; in other words, the closer an object is to you, the higher its saturation should be. You have to strike a balance.
- Sakurai gives feedback on visual scale often. Drawing each material's texture on its own won't create cohesive visuals, so step back and look at the final image as a whole.

## Do this
- To make a big object or vista feel huge, lower its saturation and let some bluish atmospheric haze build up with distance, and keep the strongest saturation on objects close to the camera.
- To make something deliberately look like a toy or model, do the reverse: blur the top and bottom of the frame, raise saturation and cut blue hues.
- Remove depth-of-field blur and focus pulls from gameplay and trailer shots unless one specific spot truly needs it.
- Review scale on a fully composed frame or screenshot, not asset by asset or texture by texture.

## Avoid
- Adding shallow depth of field everywhere as a cheap atmosphere boost, since it can make viewers physically uncomfortable.
- Giving large, distant objects full saturation, which makes them read as small.
- Polishing each material's texture in isolation without checking how the whole image hangs together.

## Examples from the episode
- **Miniature photo demo:** an overhead landscape photo turned into a miniature with top and bottom blur, higher saturation and less blue.
- **Smash Bros. — Sora's fighter reveal:** one shot originally shifted focus from the background to the foreground, and Sakurai had that removed.

## Quote
> To put it another way, the closer an object is to you, the higher its saturation should be. (01:39)
