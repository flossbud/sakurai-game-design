# 113 · Game Writing is Unique

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssvIEm_2mYM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/113-game-writing-is-unique.md)

## In one sentence
Write game stories around play—start with gameplay, brief players as they go, keep the protagonist's perspective, and don't let story undo the player's wins or strip their party—breaking these rules only on purpose.

## Key ideas
- Writing for games differs from conventional media in "four or so" ways, and those differences can be used to make something fresh:
  1. **Story can get in the way.** It's fine for players who want to see what happens next, but some games keep you stuck in cutscenes instead of playing, which frustrates people, especially in action games. Skipping makes people worry about missing key story beats, which leaves you feeling the story has to be "skippable."
  2. **The story usually unfolds from the main character's perspective.** Long backstory about comrades or villains is almost a given in media like comics, but it often doesn't fit games.
  3. **The hero almost always needs to win.** Winning a battle and then losing in the next cutscene is frustrating. Negating the player's achievements through the story causes real stress.
  4. **Comrades can't die or leave.** That works in other media, but in games it can throw off party balance or cost the player valuable items.
- You can always ignore these and do what feels right. You may upset some players, but those feelings might make the game and its story more memorable.
- The key principle for modern game writing: "Start with gameplay, not with story." Players today won't always latch onto a story, so skip long background explanations in the prologue, get right to the game and save the detailed story for later.
- In Kid Icarus: Uprising, Pit runs through a door at the start of each stage and gameplay begins. Instead of the usual mission briefing, you get briefed while you play. "Longer games are better" is a thing of the past (the rest of this line is garbled in the transcript).
- Sakurai wrote Uprising's story himself and had it all set before development really kicked off. He thinks it uses the kind of writing and staging that games are good at.

## Do this
- Get players into control within moments of starting the game and each stage. Cut background exposition from the prologue and save detailed lore for later.
- Replace up-front mission briefings with briefings delivered during play, through dialogue while the player is already moving and fighting.
- Tell the story through the protagonist's eyes, and trim long backstory segments about allies and villains.
- Never have a cutscene undo a fight the player just won. If the plot needs a loss, don't stage it right after a victory the player earned.
- If a party member must leave, protect party balance and the items the player invested in them, or rethink the departure.
- Lock the story before development really kicks off, as Sakurai did for Uprising.

## Avoid
- Long cutscenes that keep action-game players from actually playing.
- Story beats that cancel out the player's victories.
- Removing party members and their valuable gear without accounting for balance.
- Long-winded background explanations in the prologue.
