# 159 · The Shoryuken Command

**Category:** Game Essence · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zswiCPS5CSM) · [Transcript](../transcripts/159-the-shoryuken-command.md)

## In one sentence
Build risk into the inputs themselves, so the most powerful option requires passing through the most vulnerable positions, then scale the reward to that risk, and decide on purpose how much execution difficulty your audience can handle.

## Key ideas
- The shoryuken first appeared in the original Street Fighter (arcade, 1987), where it was a true killer move: it could KO an opponent in two or three hits, but it was surprisingly hard to perform. Street Fighter II made the command mainstream. It was much easier to throw out, and the move was also made much weaker.
- The command (forward, down, down-forward, then attack) walks the stick through steadily more dangerous positions. Forward means approaching the opponent without guarding, since guarding is "back". Down is a crouch, which leaves you open to aerial attacks. Down-forward mixes the two, drops all your defenses and can't instantly crouch-guard: it's "the most dangerous stick position possible". Pressing attack then turns that aerial vulnerability into an anti-air attack.
- SFII adds push and pull. The shoryuken is stronger near its base and invincible at the start, repelling all kinds of attacks, so it gets more effective the closer you let the opponent come. Being close also leaves you open to counterattack: "the closer they get, the greater the potential to turn things around."
- The move works because several factors are balanced together: appropriate input difficulty, the time it takes to finish the command, the opponent's jump and attack speed, and the danger you're in if it misses.
- The light, medium and heavy versions differ in jump height and strength. Sakurai feels the heavy one could have been stronger still: "A move with high risk should deliver a big reward."
- In Smash Bros., Ryu (and Ken, Terry and Kazuya) can use the command. Ryu's shoryuken works with one button or the full input, and the full input gives a stronger attack and more invincibility frames. From a game essence standpoint the command version should have been even stronger, but making the command essential would have split players into those who can and can't do it, so they kept the current balance to stay casual. Yoko Shimomura, composer of SFII, was thrilled to land one (even if it was the one-button version). The same input can serve different purposes in different games.

## Do this
- Map your strongest reversal or anti-air move to an input sequence that passes through vulnerable states (stepping forward, dropping guard), so starting the input is itself a gamble.
- Give the reversal startup invincibility and its highest power when the opponent is closest, so letting the threat get close is exactly what makes it pay off.
- When tuning a special move, review four things together: input difficulty, time to finish the input, the opponent's jump and attack speed, and the punishment for a miss.
- If you offer a simplified input (one button) alongside the full command, give the full command a clear bonus (more damage, more invincibility frames), but not so much that players who can't execute it are shut out.
- Separate strength variants (light, medium, heavy) by height and power, and make the riskiest variant's payoff clearly the largest.

## Avoid
- Underpaying the riskiest option. Sakurai thinks SFII's heavy shoryuken could have been stronger.
- Making a hard command input mandatory in a casual game. It splits players into those who can and can't play properly.

## Examples from the episode
- **Street Fighter (1987) — original shoryuken:** KO in two or three hits, but very hard to input.
- **Street Fighter II — the command and its tuning:** easier to perform and weaker, invincible at the start, strongest close in, with light, medium and heavy versions.
- **Super Smash Bros. — Ryu's shoryuken:** one-button or full command, with the full command rewarded by more power and more invincibility frames. Ken, Terry and Kazuya can also use the command.
