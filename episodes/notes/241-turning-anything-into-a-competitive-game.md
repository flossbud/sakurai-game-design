# 241 · Turning Anything Into a Competitive Game

**Category:** Grab Bag · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2z5xpvtcRw) · [Transcript](../transcripts/241-turning-anything-into-a-competitive-game.md)

## In one sentence
You can turn any single-player game into a head-to-head contest: two players start the same game at the same moment and race to a shared goal or to see who fails first.

## Key ideas
- Before the COVID-19 pandemic, Sakurai often had visitors at home, and with lots of games around they never ran out of things to play.
- They sometimes played "simultaneous competitive gameplay": both players start the same game at the exact same time, and whoever fails first loses, or the higher score wins. He used the same format in his crossover with Retro Game Master, where he and Arino Kacho started each game together.
- It suits arcade-style games where one mistake can end your run. Depending on the game, you can judge by progress or by some other set goal.
- Even Dragon Quest can be competitive, e.g., "the first to beat three slimes is the winner!" It sounds simple, but it gets heated when you can see how far your opponent has gotten.
- Once someone makes a mistake or wins, they jump straight to the next game, even if one player wants to keep going. You can get through dozens of games, which feels like working out your gaming muscles.
- You need the same game on two screens. On Switch this works with friends subscribed to Nintendo Switch Online, and Nintendo World Championships: NES Edition has everything you need for this kind of multiplayer in one package.

## Do this
- Run the same game on two screens, start both at the same moment, and agree on a win condition: first to fail loses, higher score wins, or first to reach a set goal.
- For slow-paced games, invent a short, concrete goal (e.g., "first to beat three slimes" in Dragon Quest).
- Keep both players' progress visible to each other; seeing how far your opponent has gotten is what makes it heated.
- When designing a race or versus mode, give players short, clear goals and a quick move to the next round once someone fails or wins.

## Examples from the episode
- **Retro Game Master crossover:** Sakurai and Arino Kacho started each game at the same time and competed.
- **Dragon Quest:** "the first to beat three slimes is the winner!"
- **Arcade games:** a great fit, because one mistake can end your run.
- **Nintendo World Championships: NES Edition:** everything for this kind of multiplayer in one package.
