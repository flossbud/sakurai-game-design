# 120 · The Fun in Picking Sides

**Category:** Planning & Game Design · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_A4tgLIXO4) · [Transcript](../transcripts/120-the-fun-in-picking-sides.md)

## In one sentence
Give spectators a reason to back one side, even a tiny wager, because a contest is far more exciting to watch when you care who wins.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai rarely watches sports because he isn't invested in any team. The Olympics are different because he has a country to root for, though he can still respect a strong opponent. The secret to enjoying a contest is being invested in one side.
- When only one side can win, having someone to root for going in makes you more fired up.
- Smash Bros.' online spectator mode lets you wager a few points on which player will win a replayed match. You get a few bits of information, but the players are strangers and it's nearly impossible to predict. The points aren't the real draw, since you won't earn many either way; what matters is that people become invested in a side, however lightly.
- The effect is stronger when spectators recognize the players, which is why the rise of popular esports athletes helps. The same players always getting the same results could become a problem, but any motivation to cheer is a plus.
- Countries regulate depictions of gambling differently, and the Smash spectator mode needed careful, repeated adjustments to comply. It's tougher than you might think.
- The same idea could apply to gacha in free-to-play games: offering two or three possible draws to choose from, with some reason the choice carries weight, has more meaningful impact than purely random draws.

## Do this
- In spectator or replay modes, have viewers pick a winner before the match starts. A small point wager is enough; keep payouts small, since the goal is investment, not earnings.
- Show identifying info about the competitors (names, known players) so viewers have a reason to root for someone.
- Check gambling-depiction rules in every region you ship to before finalizing any wager feature, and budget time for repeated adjustments.
- Where rewards are random, let players choose among two or three draws and give each option a reason that makes the choice matter.

## Avoid
- Purely random reward draws where a meaningful choice could create investment.

## Examples from the episode
- **Super Smash Bros. — online spectator mode:** viewers wager a few points on who will win a stranger's replay; the points are small, but they create investment, and the feature needed region-by-region gambling-law adjustments.
- **The Olympics:** having a country to root for makes watching exciting.
- **Free-to-play gacha (general):** offering two or three draws with weighted choices beats purely random draws.

## Quote
> In a contest between two sides where only one can win, you'll find yourself more fired up if you have someone to root for going in. (00:37)
