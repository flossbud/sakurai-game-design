# 180 · Let Them Skip, Let Them Pause

**Category:** UI · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4-eS6Ezui8) · [Transcript](../transcripts/180-let-them-skip-let-them-pause.md)

## In one sentence
Whenever the player isn't in control, let them skip what you're showing (all at once if possible) and let them pause cutscenes, even if you'd love them to watch every second.

## Key ideas
- Games have lots of moments when you aren't actually playing: company logos, opening movies, intermissions and cutscenes. Sakurai asks that players be able to skip these whenever possible, and all at once if you can.
- Creators naturally want players to see every sequence they poured their heart into, and may worry players won't follow the story. But a player with no save data isn't necessarily seeing the game for the first time.
- With video content like movies, not being able to fast-forward or skip is flat-out inconvenient. Games aren't measured by quite the same standards, but they should still be skippable whenever possible.
- Logos sometimes can't be skipped because of company guidelines or regulations, but that isn't popular nowadays. Sakurai thinks whoever writes those guidelines should treat skippability as a given.
- In-engine sequences can usually be skipped one line of dialogue at a time, and it's even better if cutscenes allow that too.
- Cutscenes must also be pausable. Some can still be skipped but not paused, which isn't "modern" design. Players get phone calls, visitors arrive, dinner is ready. Online games that keep advancing may be the exception.

## Do this
- Make every non-interactive sequence (company logos, opening movie, intermissions, cutscenes) skippable, ideally with a single press that skips the whole thing.
- Allow skipping on a brand-new save too. Don't lock the skip behind having watched the scene once.
- Let players advance in-engine scenes one line of dialogue at a time, and give cutscenes the same line-by-line control if you can.
- Let players pause in the middle of any cutscene, not just skip it, unless it's an online game that keeps advancing.
- If you set company logo guidelines, make skippable logos the default.

## Avoid
- Forcing players to watch sequences because you want everyone to see your work.
- Assuming that no save data means the player is seeing the game for the first time.
- Cutscenes that can be skipped but not paused.

## Examples from the episode
- **Movies and other video content:** not being able to fast-forward or skip is flat-out inconvenient, and the same frustration applies to games.

> When the player's not in control, make sure they can skip what you're showing them—I can't emphasize that enough. (01:55)
