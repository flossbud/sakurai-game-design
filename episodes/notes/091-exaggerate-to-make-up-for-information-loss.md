# 091 · Exaggerate to Make Up for Information Loss

**Category:** Animation · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ivwt37x-2EU) · [Transcript](../transcripts/091-exaggerate-to-make-up-for-information-loss.md)

## In one sentence
Game characters lose much of the information real people convey, so exaggerate performances and animations ("too much" is "just right"), especially when characters are small on screen.

## Key ideas
- Clock Tower 3 (2002) was the final work of famous Japanese film director Kinji Fukasaku, and its cutscene performances are completely over the top, almost funny.
- Fukasaku reportedly explained that polygon characters lose much of the information real humans give off, so you have to make up for it with big performances. It was his first video game, and Sakurai was stunned by how clearly he got to the heart of it.
- Game characters have plenty of range, but no part of them truly exists, animations included. So "too much" is "just right."
- Motion capture data generally can't be dropped straight into a game and work. It depends on the genre, but going really big tends to look best.
- On-screen size matters. Motion that works up close can feel far too understated when the character is small or far away.
- Every action game differs in push and pull and control feel, but it's generally better to go big and all-out. If a sword swing is just a sword swing, it will come up short where it matters most.
- Clock Tower 3 isn't better than today's games, but it's a valuable reference.

## Do this
- Exaggerate motion-capture and keyframe animation beyond what's realistic (bigger poses, larger arcs, stronger accents) instead of importing raw capture.
- Review every animation at the real gameplay camera distance and character size. If it reads as understated there, push it further.
- For key actions like attacks, don't settle for a literal motion. Make a sword swing more than "just a sword swing."
- Tune exaggeration together with screen presence, character size and controls, so big animations still fit the game's control feel.

## Avoid
- Dropping motion capture into the game unmodified.
- Judging animations only in close-up, where understated motion still looks fine.

## Examples from the episode
- **Clock Tower 3 — cutscenes:** Kinji Fukasaku's over-the-top performances, based on his view that polygon characters need big acting to make up for lost information.

## Quote
> When you're lacking information, create big animations to make up for it. (01:56)
