# 098 · How I Stuck With My Column

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ff7HadWHTJo) · [Transcript](../transcripts/098-how-i-stuck-with-my-column.md)

## In one sentence
To keep routine creative output going for years, keep it light: play to strengths you already use, cap the time it takes, and drop the sense of obligation so you enjoy the work instead of feeling beholden to it.

## Key ideas
- Sakurai's Weekly Famitsu column ran 18 years and 9 months, 640 articles in all, which he thinks is probably a record for a game creator in a game magazine. (Hikaru Ijuin has been writing even longer.)
- It began when he was chief director of the Kirby series and had just returned as director of Kirby Air Ride. He left HAL Laboratory soon after, and his editor refused to run a piece about leaving the company ("We can't publish this!"). Dev work left little time to write, sometimes meant all-nighters and left no time to play other games, and unless his current project had been announced he had nothing to write about. His pool of ideas could have run dry.
- He gives three reasons he kept it going:
  1. **Output:** as a director, putting ideas across isn't a chore for him. It's his job, and the YouTube channel proves it.
  2. **Minimal time investment:** he usually finished a column in about an hour.
  3. **Low pressure:** he didn't write because he had to or because many people were reading. He shrugged off the pressure and sense of duty and just put out what he thought was good, since overthinking gets you nowhere.
- He didn't do it out of obligation or "for the sake of the industry." Thinking that way can take a major toll, so with any job, and routine work especially, stay a little looser. Enjoy it rather than feel beholden to it.
- The column habit led to this channel, so the column was its predecessor. He got the column through a chance meeting: Famitsu's column editor had partied with him in Final Fantasy XI without ever meeting him in person and was convinced his column would be interesting. You never know what will set things in motion.

## Do this
- Time-box recurring output (devlogs, columns, videos) to a small fixed slot. Sakurai finished each column in about an hour.
- Pick recurring work that uses a skill you already exercise every day. For a director, that's explaining ideas.
- Publish what you think is good without writing for an imagined audience or out of duty, and stop overthinking each installment.
- Keep up productive habits even when they seem minor. The column habit is what gave him the idea for his next big project, the channel.

## Avoid
- Taking on routine work out of obligation or "for the sake of the industry." It takes a major toll over time.
- Overthinking each piece of a recurring series.

## Examples from the episode
- **Weekly Famitsu column:** 640 articles over 18 years and 9 months, usually written in about an hour each, even during crunch.
- **Final Fantasy XI:** where he met the editor who offered him the column.
- **Kirby Air Ride:** the project he was directing when the column started.

## Quote
> You have to enjoy it, not feel beholden to it. (02:29)
