# 152 · Do Graphics Impact Fun?

**Category:** Graphics · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VM-kH-j9_OQ) · [Transcript](../transcripts/152-do-graphics-impact-fun.md)

## In one sentence
Graphics and game essence feed each other: gameplay gives environments their "presence," and visuals decide whether players try the game at all. So develop the two together and raise both as far as you can.

## Key ideas
- People say graphics have nothing to do with how fun a game is. You can treat game essence and graphics as independent, but game essence clearly brings graphics to life.
- Game environments are "nothing more than polygons." Without collision detection you can't touch them, and without gravity they're "mirages you can pass straight through." Yet their presence in-game is a different story.
- **Presence comes from gameplay:** In Dark Souls or Sekiro, you die often, so you grow wary of your surroundings, scrutinize every movement for warning signs, and look into every nook and cranny. That gives each environmental object far more presence. The same graphics in a game with Sonic running at high speed would leave a very different impression. Whether the world means more than a mirage depends partly on the game's content.
- Environment and character art are usually made alongside the rest of the game, so mismatches are rare, but be aware that a game's fun and the appeal of its graphics affect each other.
- **Graphics also decide whether anyone plays:** On storefronts like Steam with an almost endless lineup, people often use a game's banner to decide whether to look at it. "If your visuals don't draw players in, people won't play your game." In that sense, "graphics multiply the fun of a game."
- Graphics are in the eye of the beholder. It's valid to say graphics don't determine fun, but it's best to strike a balance and elevate both.

## Do this
- Work out how closely your gameplay makes players look at the environment (cautious, high-death play vs. high-speed play) and put environmental detail where players will actually scrutinize it.
- Build environment art alongside the mechanics, and check that visual density suits the pace of play rather than finishing art in isolation.
- Treat your storefront banner and key art as critical: on crowded stores, they decide whether players look at your game at all.

## Avoid
- Writing off graphics as irrelevant to fun. If the visuals don't draw players in, they won't play.

## Examples from the episode
- **Dark Souls / Sekiro:** frequent deaths make players scrutinize every corner, giving environmental objects strong presence.
- **Sonic:** the same graphics at high speed would leave a very different impression.
- **Steam:** with an endless lineup, players judge games by their banners.

## Quote
> In that sense, you could say that graphics multiply the fun of a game. (02:05)
