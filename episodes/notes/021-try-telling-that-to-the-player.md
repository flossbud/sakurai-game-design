# 021 · Try Telling That to the Player

**Category:** Work Ethic · [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ik3deWOVCHI) · [Transcript](../transcripts/021-try-telling-that-to-the-player.md)

## In one sentence
Design for the player's experience, not the developers' convenience, because players won't cut you slack for a hard development, and when a compromise would hurt players, look for alternatives with your programmers.

## Key ideas
- Development means working with computers that don't always cooperate and managing large human-resource costs, so what you want to do and what you can do don't always line up.
- When he asks a game designer for a design document, what comes back often "will prioritize the developers' jobs, rather than the player experience." Sometimes that's a sensible compromise for efficiency. Other times it's "just plain bad for players," or puts too much weight on ease of development.
- His response: players don't care why developers do what they do, only whether the game is good, and "they aren't going to cut you slack just because development was hard. Nor should they."
- Developers are players too, so they should know how customers will feel. On the dev side, though, it's easy to forget that perspective, especially as problems pile up.
- Problems will come up, tastes vary, and sometimes a technical limit leaves no other choice. "But when push comes to shove, you're doing this for the player, and nobody else." If you think players won't like something, look for other solutions.
- This isn't only a design issue. Talk to your programmers too, and strike a balance between the ideal and what's technically feasible.

## Do this
- When reviewing a design document, flag every decision made for development convenience and ask whether players would accept it. Keep it only if it's a sensible efficiency trade players won't notice.
- Before accepting a compromise you think players won't like, look for alternative solutions first.
- Bring programmers into those discussions to find the balance between the ideal and what's technically feasible.
- Play your build as a customer would, especially when problems are piling up and the player's perspective is easy to lose.

## Avoid
- Specs that put ease of development ahead of the player experience.
- Expecting players to forgive problems because development was difficult.
