## Game essence

Chapter: [02-game-essence.md](02-game-essence.md)

- [ ] **At the moment the player can score, how exposed are they?** *(ESS-L01)*
  - Good: The scoring position is also a dangerous position; rewards carry new risk.
  - Red flags: Safe spots where the player can still win; screen-clearing options with no cost.
- [ ] **What does each verb cost the player when they use it?** *(ESS-L02)*
  - Good: Every action has a matching danger: recovery, ammo, stamina, exposure, falling.
  - Red flags: An action whose benefit has no downside; flight or speed with nothing to oppose it.
- [ ] **Is the riskiest option also the most rewarding one?** *(ESS-L03)*
  - Good: Payoff climbs with startup, exposure, wager or accumulated danger.
  - Red flags: The safe play scores best; the heavy variant is underpaid.
- [ ] **Where are the stress, relief, growth and progress in this section?** *(ESS-L04)*
  - Good: A clear squeeze, a satisfying release, a reward that strengthens the player, and a next goal.
  - Red flags: No obstacle at all, or a release that leads nowhere.
- [ ] **Can you state in one sentence why this mechanic is fun?** *(ESS-L05)*
  - Good: A risk-and-reward explanation that would survive changing the surface action.
  - Red flags: 'Because game X does it'; an idea that only renames the genre's default action.
- [ ] **What decision does each rule create for the players?** *(ESS-L06)*
  - Good: Every mechanic adds a read, a counter or a trade-off; reads can beat raw stats.
  - Red flags: Inherited rules with no decision attached; outcomes settled by the first hit or the bigger number.
- [ ] **What does the player get, what can they do with it, and when?** *(ESS-L07)*
  - Good: Rewards planned early, earned through play, spent on things that help with the next challenge.
  - Red flags: Currency with nothing worth buying; everything unlocked at the start; rewards that snowball.
- [ ] **After a loss, does the player want one more try?** *(ESS-L08)*
  - Good: Short restarts, an entertaining defeat, 'I'll get you next time!'.
  - Red flags: Long waits after death; defeat that only humiliates.
- [ ] **What is this game's main source of fun, and is the polish budget going there?** *(ESS-L09)*
  - Good: A named source (essence, control feel, story, fandom, simulation, creation, rhythm) with matching investment, and risk layered in where it fits.
  - Red flags: Assuming story alone carries the game; essence pushed so hard the game loses flair or becomes a chore.

## Balance and difficulty

Chapter: [03-balance-and-difficulty.md](03-balance-and-difficulty.md)

- [ ] **Who is the target player, and what does a "minor" risk feel like to them?** *(BAL-L01)*
  - Good: A written target by experience level and risk dials set to match it.
  - Red flags: Difficulty tuned to the team; one-hit deaths or heavy inertia in a game for newcomers.
- [ ] **Can a player using only the simplest core finish the game or hold their own?** *(BAL-L02)*
  - Good: A core-only playthrough that works; depth that is optional; hard inputs rewarded but not required.
  - Red flags: Progress or competitiveness gated on the depth system or on hard inputs.
- [ ] **What happens to a player who fails the same spot ten times?** *(BAL-L03)*
  - Good: Difficulty changeable anytime, an offer to ease off or skip without shame, variety on the way back.
  - Red flags: A forced restart, an impossible spike, the same scenery every attempt.
- [ ] **How many seconds pass between 'Ugh, I blew it!' and the next input?** *(BAL-L04)*
  - Good: About a second of punchy failure feedback, then control, at a safe respawn.
  - Red flags: Long fades, loads, unskippable text, respawning into enemy fire.
- [ ] **Who set this difficulty, and how well do they know the game?** *(BAL-L05)*
  - Good: Tuning done handicapped, then lowered again; fresh testers used once each.
  - Red flags: 'The best player on the team can just about clear it.'
- [ ] **After this balance change, does every option still have a peak and a valley?** *(BAL-L06)*
  - Good: Distinct strengths and weaknesses; fixes made to secondary parameters; win rates in a narrow band; options that still feel different.
  - Red flags: Options converging; signature strengths shaved; weaknesses patched in the same change.
- [ ] **Could a player lose purely because of luck?** *(BAL-L07)*
  - Good: Random events players can respond to; randomness that rewards skill or enables comebacks in casual modes.
  - Red flags: Single rolls deciding outcomes in skill contests; manipulable random numbers.
- [ ] **Does each penalty match how bad the mistake was, and is there a way back?** *(BAL-L08)*
  - Good: Small slips cost little, big blunders cost more, every irreversible choice has a recovery path.
  - Red flags: Instant death for a missed jump; all power-ups wiped; permanent penalties with no redemption.
- [ ] **How much does this game help the player who is behind, and was that a decision?** *(BAL-L09)*
  - Good: A stated choice between casual catch-up and skill-reflecting chaos, plus handicap options for mismatched groups.
  - Red flags: The same player winning every casual match, or stat buffs for losers in a skill game.
- [ ] **Where are the peaks and flats on your difficulty curve?** *(BAL-L10)*
  - Good: Bosses as peaks with rewards, breathers and farming spots as flats, no late impossible spike.
  - Red flags: A steady ramp in lockstep with the player; level scaling that removes the reason to get ahead.

More questions:

- [ ] Is this fix touching the one thing players love about the option? *(BAL-07)*
- [ ] Does this consequence match how good or bad the action really was, and is there a way back? *(BAL-08)*
- [ ] Where are the peaks and the flat stretches on your difficulty curve? *(BAL-09)*
- [ ] What does a player see on their tenth attempt at this section, and why would they want to keep going? *(BAL-10)*
- [ ] Can someone using only the simplest controls finish or hold their own? *(BAL-11)*
- [ ] Would your target player still understand the game after this rule is added? *(BAL-12)*
- [ ] Which specific dials set the risk in this game, and has each been set on purpose? *(BAL-13)*
- [ ] How much does this game help the player who is behind, and was that a decision? *(BAL-14)*
- [ ] Could a player lose this purely because of a random roll? *(BAL-15)*
- [ ] Does lowering or raising difficulty feel like a decision the player owns? *(BAL-16)*
- [ ] What do your testers and data say, and does each option still feel right? *(BAL-17)*
- [ ] Could a human have reacted the way this CPU just did? *(BAL-18)*
- [ ] What does the typical player see when they check their rank? *(BAL-19)*
- [ ] Has the final difficulty been checked on the final build on real hardware? *(BAL-20)*
- [ ] Is the game easier or harder with a second player, and is that intended? *(BAL-21)*
- [ ] Can a strong player and a weak player both enjoy the same session? *(BAL-22)*

## Planning, structure and pacing

Chapter: [04-planning-and-structure.md](04-planning-and-structure.md)

- [ ] **Within three minutes of first boot, is the player in control and has the core fun shown itself?** *(PLAN-L01)*
  - Good: A newcomer acts within moments, meets a climactic or meaningful situation, and can say what the game is about after three minutes; controls arrive in stages.
  - Red flags: Logos and lore before the first input; a detached tutorial room; the full control set demanded at once.
- [ ] **What share of a session is the player in control, and what is the longest stretch without it?** *(PLAN-L02)*
  - Good: Most of the session is in control; transitions are brisk; retries return control in about a second plus load; every non-interactive moment can be skipped or paused.
  - Red flags: Slow fades and black screens, long death sequences, unskippable scenes, trips back to town after an objective.
- [ ] **Can the concept be stated in one sentence, and does every task in progress serve it?** *(PLAN-L03)*
  - Good: One clear statement of appeal, naming the audience or hook; current work traces back to it; emergent fun is amplified in the same direction.
  - Red flags: Several competing visions; direction changes because development feels hard; nobody can say who the game is for.
- [ ] **Has the core fun been played in a build, before content production began?** *(PLAN-L04)*
  - Good: A bare prototype or finished-quality pilot exists and convinced people; open questions in the spec are tied to builds that will answer them.
  - Red flags: Months of content on top of an unproven core; a spec that pretends to know everything.
- [ ] **At any moment, what is the player working toward, and can they see how far they have left?** *(PLAN-L05)*
  - Good: A visible next reward or objective, progress shown as current versus required, peaks that pay off, rewards that suit the concept.
  - Red flags: Hidden progress, rewards bolted on late, a steady incline with no peaks or flats, stat growth that breaks a fixed-baseline game.
- [ ] **If the schedule halved tomorrow, what goes, what stays locked, and what hidden costs are we carrying?** *(PLAN-L06)*
  - Good: A ranked feature list with must-ship items protected, known test and maintenance costs per feature, every hard limit checked.
  - Red flags: Must-haves like the tutorial left to the end; growth systems added late with no test budget; guideline conflicts found after building.
- [ ] **Could a teammate implement and tune this spec without asking you a question?** *(PLAN-L07)*
  - Good: Bracketed plain-language parameters with units, timings in frames, a single committed option, an outline that mirrors the game.
  - Red flags: Terse or unitless values, 'or we could do this' alternatives, walls of flat text.
- [ ] **Does any cutscene or story beat delay play, undo the player's win, or serve as the reward?** *(PLAN-L08)*
  - Good: Story told from the protagonist's view and largely during play; cutscenes short and purposeful; showcase movies treated as marketing.
  - Red flags: A loss in a cutscene after a player victory; long exposition before control; movies locked behind progress.
- [ ] **Described without naming another game, what does ours offer that players can't get elsewhere?** *(PLAN-L09)*
  - Good: Several distinct kinds of appeal, inherited givens questioned, characters and options with one-word identities, a sequel that is clearly bigger.
  - Red flags: 'X, but with Y'; one small point of difference; characters that are basically the same.

More questions:

- [ ] If the schedule were halved tomorrow, which features go first, and which are locked? *(PLAN-05)*
- [ ] Is the length we are planning a by-product of the fun, or a number we are padding toward? *(PLAN-06)*
- [ ] Is any lesson delivered as text or in a separate room instead of through a real challenge, and does anything explain what the player already knows? *(PLAN-07)*
- [ ] Which controls does the first segment require, and could it require fewer? *(PLAN-08)*
- [ ] Can you say in one line who this game is for or what its hook is, and do its risk settings match that player? *(PLAN-09)*
- [ ] Described without naming any other game, what does ours offer that nothing else does? *(PLAN-10)*
- [ ] Which of our rules exist only because the genre always had them? *(PLAN-11)*
- [ ] Which parts of the spec are still guesses, and which build will answer them? *(PLAN-12)*
- [ ] Could a teammate implement and tune this spec without asking you a single question? *(PLAN-13)*
- [ ] Does every page carry one idea backed by an image, and could the whole deck be presented at a brisk pace? *(PLAN-14)*
- [ ] Does the document ever present two options where it should make a decision? *(PLAN-15)*
- [ ] What does the player earn in the first hour, and does each reward type suit what makes the game fun? *(PLAN-16)*
- [ ] At any moment, what is the player working toward, and can they see how far they have left? *(PLAN-17)*
- [ ] Where are the peaks and the flats in our first hour, and does the player feel stronger after each peak? *(PLAN-18)*
- [ ] Does any story beat stop play, undo the player's win, or take away something they invested in? *(PLAN-19)*
- [ ] For each cutscene, what does it do that play can't, and would anyone miss it if it were half as long? *(PLAN-20)*
- [ ] Can a player skip, pause or fast-forward every non-interactive moment, credits included? *(PLAN-21)*
- [ ] Which guidelines and hardware limits bind us, and has every planned feature been checked against them? *(PLAN-22)*

- [ ] Does each mode or segment have a rule that no other has? *(PLAN-24)*

## Game feel

Chapter: [05-game-feel.md](05-game-feel.md)

- [ ] **From each press to the first visible or audible response, how many frames pass, and does every press in every state get a reaction?** *(FEEL-L01)*
  - Good: The pose changes on the first frame after input; every accepted press has a distinct cue; display and audio lag have been checked on the actual setup.
  - Red flags: Easing out of idle before the windup; dead buttons in menus or cutscenes; testers saying the controls feel 'floaty' or 'heavy' on one screen but not another.
- [ ] **When a hit lands, a boss falls or the player dies, does the game stop and shake enough to feel the weight and see what happened?** *(FEEL-L02)*
  - Good: Hit stop scaled by strength, victim shaking more than attacker, boss stops with a flash, deaths with a visible point of impact, shake presets matched to impact size.
  - Red flags: 'Wait, I got hit?'; every hit feeling the same; shake so strong the screen is unreadable.
- [ ] **Does every attack that looks like it connects actually connect, at every damage level, from every direction?** *(FEEL-L03)*
  - Good: Hitboxes a little wider than the weapon in fast games, ranges crossing the median line, multi-hit strings that always connect, collision and animation that agree.
  - Red flags: Visible hits that whiff; hits after the swing ends; combos that drop at some damage percentages; complaints about hits 'not registering'.
- [ ] **How many seconds per session is the player not in control, and how long from failure to the next attempt?** *(FEEL-L04)*
  - Good: Hasty transitions that still read as transitions, loading hidden in-world or shown at once, failure effects of about a second, skippable and pausable cutscenes.
  - Red flags: Slow fades, long black screens, waits that look like mistakes, long death sequences, respawning into danger.
- [ ] **Can you state each action's startup, hit and recovery in frames, and does the build match by feel?** *(FEEL-L05)*
  - Good: Specs written in frames; hits about 1/4 to 1/3 in; strong moves with long recoveries; cancel frames where players expect control; a stable target frame rate.
  - Red flags: Timing described in words; strong attacks that are safe; control returning only at standby; frame drops during the heaviest scenes.
- [ ] **Does each character's movement, jump and knockback feel like its intended weight and serve the game's goal?** *(FEEL-L06)*
  - Good: Distinct weights with identical inputs, a jump shaped for its purpose, knockback that always produces a visible reaction, values retuned after every tempo change.
  - Red flags: Every character feeling the same; a default jump; launches too fast to follow; parameters carried over from another game.
- [ ] **Does the camera show the motion the player just made and the space they need?** *(FEEL-L07)*
  - Good: Characters rising on screen when they jump, drifting off center on dodges, look-ahead in scrollers, enough screen for play, comfort toggles for inertial effects.
  - Red flags: A center point locked to the character; jumps whose height can't be judged; tiny targets; motion-sick testers with no options.
- [ ] **Could a genre newcomer pick up the controls in minutes, and can every player change them?** *(FEEL-L08)*
  - Good: Direction-plus-button controls, analog tilt and flick used physically, standard layouts, remapping with profiles, adjustable sensitivity.
  - Red flags: Command strings for basic moves, two-step dashes, inputs added because the hardware has them, no remapping.

More questions:

- [ ] Is every timing in the spec a frame number, and could its author tell by feel whether the build matches? *(FEEL-07)*
- [ ] During hit stop, does the victim shake more than the attacker, does duration scale with strength, and does anything still move or animate? *(FEEL-08)*
- [ ] For every attack, where does the hit land in its timeline, and does a stronger move leave the attacker open for longer? *(FEEL-09)*
- [ ] Do players regain control at the moment they instinctively try to act after each move? *(FEEL-10)*
- [ ] Can you state what your jump's rise, peak, fall and air control are each for? *(FEEL-11)*
- [ ] Does each impact class have its own shake, and does the shake look the same size at every camera distance? *(FEEL-12)*
- [ ] With the same inputs, does each character feel like the weight you intended, and does moving feel good on its own? *(FEEL-13)*
- [ ] When the character jumps, dodges or gets launched, can the player see on screen how far they moved? *(FEEL-14)*
- [ ] Could a newcomer perform every core action within minutes, and does each input earn its place? *(FEEL-15)*
- [ ] Is there any situation where the player pressed one thing and the game did something else, like walking off a ledge or dashing by accident? *(FEEL-16)*
- [ ] Does every attack produce a visible, intended reaction at low, medium and high damage, and can players follow the launch? *(FEEL-17)*
- [ ] Does the game hold its target frame rate in the worst case, and was that target chosen for the controls rather than by default? *(FEEL-18)*
- [ ] Would a player of this genre know the controls without being told, and can every player change them? *(FEEL-19)*
- [ ] Since the last change in tempo, mode or platform, has every movement and combat value been re-felt, not just carried over? *(FEEL-20)*
- [ ] Does every speed in your specs have a unit everyone reads the same way? *(FEEL-21)*
- [ ] How much of the screen is actually used for play in your main camera, and are targets big enough to read? *(FEEL-22)*
- [ ] Can a player prone to motion sickness turn off every inertial camera effect without losing the game's readability? *(FEEL-23)*
- [ ] How long does it take to change one movement value and feel the result in the build? *(FEEL-24)*

## Animation

Chapter: [06-animation.md](06-animation.md)

- [ ] **Does the first frame after every attack press show a big pose clearly different from standby?** *(ANIM-L01)*
  - Good: An instant jump into the windup; charge moves holding and shaking; light moves out almost immediately.
  - Red flags: Ease-outs from idle; first frames you can't tell apart from standby; 'the controls feel delayed' on a lag-free display.
- [ ] **Pause on each attack's contact frame: is it the strongest, clearest pose, and does the hitbox sit on it?** *(ANIM-L02)*
  - Good: Strong, twisted, readable hit poses; collision starting once the swing is strong; visuals and hitboxes that agree.
  - Red flags: Half-formed poses frozen by hit stop; hits that look like they connect but don't; swings that keep hitting after they end.
- [ ] **From the farthest gameplay camera, can you read every attack and every hit reaction at a glance?** *(ANIM-L03)*
  - Good: Exaggerated poses, extreme damage frames unlike standby, faces pushed past 'done', review done at real size.
  - Red flags: Animations approved only in close-up; damage poses near standby; subtle faces on tiny characters.
- [ ] **Does the follow-through show what just happened and how heavy it was, and look nothing like standby?** *(ANIM-L04)*
  - Good: Big, momentum-carrying recoveries on their own path, held until cancel frames, full landings.
  - Red flags: Interpolating straight back to idle; recoveries that fizzle; reverse playback of the attack.
- [ ] **Is any motion mushy from interpolation or long blends, or too fast to register?** *(ANIM-L05)*
  - Good: Key poses arriving quickly, short blends, secondary motion with enough frames to read.
  - Red flags: Moves that feel slow despite low frame counts; distorted blends; wings or cloth snapping in two frames.
- [ ] **Does anything move like a machine: single-axis turns, center pivots, straight-line flight, straight wrists?** *(ANIM-L06)*
  - Good: Two-axis turns, linked joints, off-center pivots, wobble and recoil, hand-tuned curves.
  - Red flags: Robotic head turns, drill-like wrists, perfectly centered spins, default function curves.
- [ ] **Filled in black, from the gameplay camera, on both sides of the screen, is every key pose readable and asymmetric?** *(ANIM-L07)*
  - Good: All four limbs visible in profile, twists instead of side bends, faces visible from both sides.
  - Red flags: Symmetric poses, flat sideways knockback, characters facing away on one side.
- [ ] **Has every move been checked in all its versions: ground and air, each direction, each facing and each target stance?** *(ANIM-L08)*
  - Good: Side-by-side variant reviews, reach tests in all directions, combo tests against every reaction stance.
  - Red flags: Aerial versions with different poses; reach that shrinks in one direction; combos that drop on some stances.

More questions:

- [ ] If you pause on the frame where each attack connects, is it the strongest, most readable pose in the move? *(ANIM-03)*
- [ ] For each key animation, did someone try a bigger version, and is the shipped one the biggest that still reads well? *(ANIM-04)*
- [ ] Do your attacks hit their key poses quickly, or do in-between frames make them feel slow? *(ANIM-05)*
- [ ] Does every recovery clearly show what move just happened and how heavy it was, right up to standby? *(ANIM-06)*
- [ ] From the farthest gameplay camera, can you tell at a glance that a character just got hit, and how hard? *(ANIM-07)*
- [ ] Filled in black and seen from the gameplay camera on both sides of the screen, is every key pose asymmetric and instantly readable? *(ANIM-08)*
- [ ] Does every animation request state its length, its attack start frame and its key poses? *(ANIM-09)*
- [ ] Was this animation approved while playing at gameplay camera distance, or only in a paused close-up? *(ANIM-10)*
- [ ] Are your blends short enough to keep rhythm and free of distorted in-between poses? *(ANIM-11)*
- [ ] Where landings, crouches and impacts squash, does the volume stay about the same? *(ANIM-12)*
- [ ] Does any organic motion rotate on a single axis or move a joint without the joints connected to it? *(ANIM-13)*
- [ ] Which moving objects still follow straight lines or spin on their exact center, and what would give each one personality? *(ANIM-14)*
- [ ] Can your rig hit the most exaggerated pose your style calls for, and was that decided before animation began? *(ANIM-15)*
- [ ] Can players see the face of every expressive character from both sides of the screen? *(ANIM-16)*
- [ ] At the size players see them, do faces read clearly, and do they stay true to the source? *(ANIM-17)*
- [ ] Is any motion so fast it never registers, or so smooth it has no weight? *(ANIM-18)*

## Visuals, characters and effects

- [ ] **Freeze the busiest frame: can a newcomer tell where the ground is, who is who, what just hit and what's dangerous?** *(VIS-L01)*
  - Good: Terrain, characters, platforms and projectiles are the most prominent things; characters stay visible inside effects; threats have visual tells.
  - Red flags: Backdrop outshining terrain; fighters lost in shadow, stained glass or layered effects; projectiles lost against a contrasty floor.
- [ ] **Filled in solid black, is every character exaggerated, readable and distinct from the rest of the cast?** *(VIS-L02)*
  - Good: Exaggerated signature features, clear limbs in key poses from the main view, bright extremities.
  - Red flags: Similar outlines, realistic proportions that blur together, symmetric front-facing poses.
- [ ] **Through a color-blindness filter and in grayscale, does everything that matters still separate?** *(VIS-L03)*
  - Good: Gameplay-critical colors differ in brightness; background darker or calmer than foreground in value.
  - Red flags: Pieces or pickups that merge; backgrounds brighter than the foreground; a mishmash of colors.
- [ ] **On the brightest and darkest backgrounds, does every effect and flash still pop?** *(VIS-L04)*
  - Good: Crisp focal shapes, dark accents in bright effects, hit flashes with internal contrast.
  - Red flags: All-additive effects that wash out; white flashes that make characters vanish.
- [ ] **Is each effect as big, as long and as placed as the event it marks?** *(VIS-L05)*
  - Good: Hit marks at the contact point, sized to strength, short for rapid hits; explosions with flash, blast, smoke and a natural end, hitbox shorter than the visual.
  - Red flags: Generic sparks, floating marks, lingering clutter, explosions cut short or made of one sprite.
- [ ] **In the final frame at gameplay distance, does the scene have believable light and scale?** *(VIS-L06)*
  - Good: Visible light direction and color, saturation falling with distance, large objects that don't look like toys, flames with density.
  - Red flags: Flat base colors, oversaturated glossy giants, candle-like flames, art approved only in a model viewer.
- [ ] **Is the detail where players actually look, and are targets big enough on the real screen?** *(VIS-L07)*
  - Good: Detail in front of the camera and on playable surfaces; enemies and bosses sized for the screen and audience.
  - Red flags: Effort spent on unseen details; tiny targets on a handheld; models over-budgeted for their screen size.
- [ ] **Do all characters and assets look like one game, and do adapted ones feel like their originals?** *(VIS-L08)*
  - Good: A shared visual concept from image boards; mixed styles met in the middle; adaptations checked side by side with the source.
  - Red flags: A fight between live action and animation; literal copies that feel wrong; changes that betray what fans love.
- [ ] **Does each character's look and effects tell the player what it is and what its moves do best?** *(VIS-L09)*
  - Good: Weapon-specific trails, status shown on the body, poses and faces exaggerated enough to read at game size.
  - Red flags: Shared generic effects, subtle expressions on small characters, inherited realism nobody questioned.
- [ ] **After an hour of play, is anything glaring, strobing, blurry or nauseating, and can players turn it down?** *(VIS-L10)*
  - Good: Capped glow brightness, brief and mild flashing scaled by camera distance, subtle blur with an option, no gratuitous depth of field.
  - Red flags: Searing magma, full-screen strobes up close, fuzzy motion blur, focus pulls in gameplay.

- [ ] **How long does a problem take to go from noticed, to raised, to decided?** *(TEAM-L02)*

- [ ] **Can every teammate state the concept in one sentence and their task's role in it?** *(TEAM-L03)*
  - Good: Matching one-sentence answers; changes that tune how the game delivers its appeal rather than replacing the appeal; wrong calls corrected quickly.
  - Red flags: Different answers from different people; direction changing with each review; a known-bad decision kept because it was already made.
- [ ] **Is our feedback shown, specific and heard by everyone it affects?** *(TEAM-L04)*
  - Good: Retouches or sketches beside the original; notes that name the axis, frame or reference; whole-discipline review sessions; changes explained to everyone involved.
  - Red flags: Vague notes like "make it look better"; onomatopoeia-only sound notes; feedback relayed through one person; the same note coming back reversed.
- [ ] **What is the oldest unanswered review request or pending decision?** *(TEAM-L05)*
  - Good: Nothing older than a working day; a single review channel; a delivery cutoff; the reviewer has a protected block for their own work.
  - Red flags: Requests piling up overnight; reviews starting late at night; the whole team waiting on one inbox.
- [ ] **Could a new teammate build this spec without asking a question?** *(TEAM-L06)*
  - Good: Frame counts, units and bracketed parameters; posed-figure or reference images; each asset's gameplay role stated; one committed option instead of A-or-B.
  - Red flags: Words like "fast"; unlabeled numbers; Plan B left open; convenience choices that players will feel.
- [ ] **When did someone new to the game last play it from the start while we watched silently?** *(TEAM-L07)*
  - Good: Fresh testers each used once; children or outsiders observed, not interviewed; testers who get motion sick checking the camera; reactions shared with the team.
  - Red flags: Only the dev team has played; the same outsider reused; asking testers what they think instead of watching.
- [ ] **Is the director spending time where only the director can help?** *(TEAM-L08)*
  - Good: Specialists and testers trusted with execution and analysis; the director supervising, playing the build, cutting scope and stepping into anything stalled.
  - Red flags: The director doing data entry; a core system still broken after long delegation; cuts left to producer-versus-director fights.
- [ ] **Are we keeping what makes the game and its characters distinctive?** *(TEAM-L09)*
  - Good: Weaknesses amplified to offset strengths; director interventions aimed at individuality; deliberate quirks kept despite outside pressure.
  - Red flags: Every character converging on average; balance changes that only cut strengths; design swayed by whichever opinions agree with us.
- [ ] **Does the team see the whole game and feel heard and respected?** *(TEAM-L10)*
  - Good: A daily report people actually read; regular showcases; an answered opinion board; polite requests instead of orders; calm, blame-free problem reviews.

## Case studies

Chapter: [14-case-studies.md](14-case-studies.md)

- [ ] **What named problem, platform strength or market gap does our concept answer?** *(CASE-L01)*
  - Good: A one-sentence problem statement and a core rule that changes what the player does; a design element that only this platform can deliver.
  - Red flags: Novelty that renames the usual verb; a concept that would be the same on any platform; a crowded genre chosen from trend data.
- [ ] **Can a novice finish with only the core, and does the optional depth reward veterans?** *(CASE-L02)*
  - Good: A playthrough with only the basic verbs reaches the end; depth systems and extras sit where players choose them; the harshest penalty suits the target player.
  - Red flags: Progress gated by the depth system; extras forced on everyone; risk tuned to the team's skill.
- [ ] **Given our team, tools and remaining time, is this a concept we can finish, and what does our situation make uniquely possible?** *(CASE-L03)*
  - Good: Dev time estimated for each candidate; carried-over team, tools and assets listed; the plan uses that head start boldly.
  - Red flags: A concept chosen without a time estimate; a one-time opportunity left unused.
- [ ] **Do our characters, stages or vehicles change the rules, or only the art?** *(CASE-L04)*
  - Good: Each variant is a parameter set that changes how it plays and sounds; players can name favorites and say why.
  - Red flags: Reskins with barely different stats; separate code per variant.
- [ ] **Has the final balance been checked on production hardware after the last optimization?** *(CASE-L05)*
  - Good: Processing speed and timing locked before tuning; difficulty and timers replayed on retail units; a narrow fix ready if launch reveals a problem.
  - Red flags: Tuning only on dev boards; optimizations after balance sign-off; game speed tied to processing speed.
- [ ] **Before cutting a feature or content, have we tried changing its context or using the limit as a design idea?** *(CASE-L06)*
  - Good: Screen space, arena bounds, speed, camera and roles tried as levers; memory and screen limits answered with reuse, authored patterns or bigger targets.
  - Red flags: Signature mechanics cut at the first balance problem; limits treated only as subtraction.
- [ ] **For a sequel, what is the one-line headline, and does every element have an upgrade target?** *(CASE-L07)*
  - Good: A headline tied to new hardware, audience or team continuity; an element-by-element upgrade list.
  - Red flags: 'More of the same' at the same scale.
- [ ] **If players or partners don't get the game, where can they learn why it is deep?** *(CASE-L08)*
  - Good: A players' guide or regular explanation in the words of whoever knows the game best.
  - Red flags: Waiting for the press to explain a new concept.
