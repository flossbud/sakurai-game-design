# Glossary

Sakurai's terms and coinages, as used in the series.

| Term | Meaning | Episodes |
|---|---|---|
| **'Ugh, I blew it!' window** | The time from the player realizing they failed until they regain control; interest cools fast inside it. | [117](../episodes/notes/117-make-retries-quick.md) |
| **1-and-2-and** | Sakurai's frame-counting method: clap seconds with an in-between beat, giving about 30 frames per beat and 15 per half beat at 60 fps. | [76](../episodes/notes/076-learn-to-count-frames.md) |
| **1-and-2-and counting** | His method for feeling frames: each beat is about 30 frames at 60 fps and half a beat about 15. | [76](../episodes/notes/076-learn-to-count-frames.md) |
| **A fight between live action and animation** | What a cast or world looks like when realistic and cartoony designs are dropped together unadjusted; his shorthand for a failure of style unification. | [138](../episodes/notes/138-a-fight-between-live-action-and-animation.md), [130](../episodes/notes/130-unifying-visual-style.md) |
| **A world without footprints** | Playing a game completely blind, 'as if I'm the first person walking on freshly-fallen snow', with no trailers, guides or Let's Plays; his preferred way to study others' work. | [199](../episodes/notes/199-a-world-without-footprints.md) |
| **Actual playtime** | The time the player has control. Sakurai tracks how long the player is in control, how long not, and how much is emptiness. | [73](../episodes/notes/073-say-no-to-slow.md), [250](../episodes/notes/250-be-mindful-of-the-time-players-are-giving-you.md) |
| **Additive blending** | Adding an element's colors to the background so the result moves toward white; distinct from translucency, which moves toward the element's own colors. Useful but not enough on its own. | [16](../episodes/notes/016-make-it-pop.md), [223](../episodes/notes/223-flashing-effects.md) |
| **Aerial perspective** | The way distant things lose contrast and saturation and take on atmospheric color; used to push backdrops back and to make big things read as big. | [30](../episodes/notes/030-emphasize-objects-with-collision.md), [10](../episodes/notes/010-draw-the-light-not-the-asset.md), [174](../episodes/notes/174-making-things-look-miniature.md), [253](../episodes/notes/253-odds-and-ends-of-supervising-sora-edition.md) |
| **agon, alea, mimicry, ilinx** | Roger Caillois's four elements of play (competition, chance, imitation, vertigo); Sakurai places game essence mainly in agon. | [178](../episodes/notes/178-what-is-play.md) |
| **Air Battle / Land Battle** | Kid Icarus: Uprising's two halves: a rail-shooter flight to each battlefield (Pit's Power of Flight lasts five minutes) followed by third-person shooting on the ground. | [125](../episodes/notes/125-kid-icarus-uprising.md), [227](../episodes/notes/227-gameplay-as-part-of-a-live-performance.md), [235](../episodes/notes/235-start-with-the-climax.md) |
| **Ambient sounds** | Background sounds of a place, the lowest priority in his mix, kept quiet and fairly realistic but never cut to dead silence. | [204](../episodes/notes/204-balancing-ambient-sounds.md), [69](../episodes/notes/069-game-music-and-ambient-sounds.md) |
| **Animatics** | Rough edits made by moving and stretching storyboard art; in Sakurai's reveal pipeline, the point at which a video's structure and length are essentially set. | [163](../episodes/notes/163-fighter-reveal-videos.md) |
| **animation blending** | Combining two or more animations, such as blending a canceled attack into a walk; keep blends quick and snappy. | [194](../episodes/notes/194-animation-blending.md) |
| **animation list** | Sakurai's written document listing every animation with a text description, its length and when the attack occurs; loops are marked 'Loop' or 'Any'. | [14](../episodes/notes/014-assigning-animations.md) |
| **Antithesis (to fighting games)** | How Smash is described relative to fighting games. Sakurai stresses it is not a rejection: he admires their push and pull but felt combos removed it after the first hit. | [27](../episodes/notes/027-super-smash-bros.md) |
| **Assignment** | How Sakurai frames every project: a game made to fit a given genre or goal, not a game made for his own taste. | [7](../episodes/notes/007-game-development-isnt-a-game.md), [261](../episodes/notes/261-masahiro-sakurai-on-creating-games-finale-special.md) |
| **attack (key pose)** | The part of an attack animation when it can register as a hit, plus a little motion around it; hit stop freezes it into a firm, readable pose. | [36](../episodes/notes/036-breaking-down-attack-animations.md), [62](../episodes/notes/062-attack-poses.md) |
| **Attack (of a sound)** | The opening of a sound's waveform as it rises to its peak; for impact sounds he wants it short so the sound never feels late. | [167](../episodes/notes/167-strong-attack-light-reverb.md) |
| **attack start frame** | One of the four values Sakurai specifies for each attack: the frame on which the attack begins to register. | [36](../episodes/notes/036-breaking-down-attack-animations.md) |
| **Attribute (Famicom)** | The hardware rule that palettes are shared across 16x16 areas, so placing an object can recolor the ground under it. | [118](../episodes/notes/118-family-basic.md) |
| **Audio as fiction and non-fiction** | His framing of the choice between exaggerated sounds (fiction, to sell impact) and realistic ones (non-fiction, to convey material and information). | [89](../episodes/notes/089-audio-as-fiction-and-non-fiction.md) |
| **average and mediocre** | His warning that balancing everything toward the middle makes options interchangeable and the game bland. | [218](../episodes/notes/218-average-and-mediocre-are-the-same-thing.md) |
| **beginners (not children)** | His target for the original Kirby: people new to games of any age or gender, as opposed to a demographic slice. | [226](../episodes/notes/226-targeting-and-market-research.md) |
| **Billboard** | A flat object, usually a two-polygon mesh, that always faces the camera; cheap to render and ideal for particles and light flashes. A Y-axis billboard turns only around the vertical axis. | [153](../episodes/notes/153-billboards.md) |
| **Billboard / Y-axis billboard** | A flat object that always faces the camera; a Y-axis billboard rotates only around the vertical axis and can look too skinny when misused for effects. | [153](../episodes/notes/153-billboards.md) |
| **blade trails** | Sakurai's name for the effect left behind by a sword swing, usually a fading polygon ribbon; the swing animation often has to be adjusted for it to read. | [179](../episodes/notes/179-blade-trails.md) |
| **Boost** | How Sakurai describes what his lessons offer: a small addition on top of your own experience, skill and way of thinking. | [1](../episodes/notes/001-about-this-channel.md), [261](../episodes/notes/261-masahiro-sakurai-on-creating-games-finale-special.md) |
| **boss stop** | Sakurai's own coinage for the stop when a boss is defeated: the screen stops and flashes for a moment, used since Kirby's Adventure. | [3](../episodes/notes/003-stop-for-big-moments.md) |
| **Bracketed parameters** | Sakurai's spec notation: each tunable value is written in plain language inside brackets with an 'O' placeholder, e.g. '[Special startup X-velocity is O]', and anything in brackets becomes a parameter. | [68](../episodes/notes/068-put-parameters-in-brackets.md) |
| **Brackets (parameter notation)** | Sakurai's spec convention: write each tunable value as a plain-language phrase in brackets with "O" as the number, e.g. [Special startup X-velocity is O]; anything inside brackets becomes a parameter. | [68](../episodes/notes/068-put-parameters-in-brackets.md) |
| **Brackets and O** | Sakurai's spec notation: a tunable value is written as a plain-language phrase in brackets with O for the number, and anything in brackets becomes a parameter. | [68](../episodes/notes/068-put-parameters-in-brackets.md) |
| **breaking point** | The point at which any player stops improving and gives up, which is why stuck players need a way forward. | [213](../episodes/notes/213-better-than-not-being-able-to-beat-the-game.md) |
| **brick wall** | How a risk that is minor to an experienced player can feel to a less experienced one. | [148](../episodes/notes/148-more-game-essence-means-less-broad-appeal.md) |
| **broad appeal** | How many kinds of players a game can reach; Sakurai holds that increasing game essence reduces it. | [6](../episodes/notes/006-risk-and-reward.md), [148](../episodes/notes/148-more-game-essence-means-less-broad-appeal.md) |
| **Broad input, broad output** | His habit of taking in many games and media reflectively while producing regular output (column, daily report, screenshots, the channel): 'Take a lot in, put a lot out.' | [126](../episodes/notes/126-broad-input-broad-output.md) |
| **bundodo** | Japanese word for zooming an action figure around by hand; Sakurai suggests it as a way for animators to train an eye for poses. | [106](../episodes/notes/106-posing-suggestions.md) |
| **cancel frames** | Frames late in an attack's follow-through during which the player can move, jump or attack again before the animation returns to standby; added to Smash after Melee. | [36](../episodes/notes/036-breaking-down-attack-animations.md), [77](../episodes/notes/077-follow-throughs-make-the-impact.md), [194](../episodes/notes/194-animation-blending.md) |
| **center point** | The usually invisible point a 3D camera looks at. Sakurai's advice: don't anchor it to the character, especially on the Y axis. | [61](../episodes/notes/061-dont-anchor-your-center-point.md), [103](../episodes/notes/103-screen-shake.md) |
| **cheat the system** | Getting gains without losses inside the game's rules, such as metal slime farming; fun and memorable, but not extended to rewriting parameters. | [48](../episodes/notes/048-let-players-cheat-the-system.md) |
| **Checklist** | Kirby Air Ride's achievement grid, predating console trophies; clearing a square reveals the conditions for its neighbors. | [59](../episodes/notes/059-kirby-air-ride.md) |
| **Checklist (Challenges)** | A grid of objectives introduced in Kirby Air Ride: clearing a panel reveals the conditions of its neighbors. Later used in Smash as Challenges. | [59](../episodes/notes/059-kirby-air-ride.md), [151](../episodes/notes/151-planning-your-games-rewards.md) |
| **City Trial** | Kirby Air Ride's sandbox mode with varying power-ups and stadiums, built to widen play types rather than reward one perfect line; later the basis of Smash Run. | [59](../episodes/notes/059-kirby-air-ride.md), [135](../episodes/notes/135-super-smash-bros-for-3ds-wii-u.md) |
| **Class S / Class A** | Bug severity classes: S for hangs or possible save corruption, A for bugs that stop progression; lower classes are sometimes waived on purpose. | [208](../episodes/notes/208-bug-testing-systems.md) |
| **Class S / Class A bugs** | Severity grades a manager assigns: Class S for hangs and bugs that might corrupt save data, Class A for bugs that stop players progressing; lower classes are sometimes waived on purpose. | [208](../episodes/notes/208-bug-testing-systems.md) |
| **cling to life** | Last-moment survival options, like rotating a Tetris piece after it lands, that invite greater risk-taking. | [86](../episodes/notes/086-game-essence-in-falling-block-puzzle-games.md) |
| **Color blindness filter** | A simulation filter (now built into Photoshop) that shows how art looks with types of color blindness such as protanopia; he used it to check Meteos. | [214](../episodes/notes/214-a-world-with-less-color.md) |
| **Color disparity** | Differences in how each monitor (and room lighting) shows the same color, which he treats as fatal for art reviews. | [184](../episodes/notes/184-dealing-with-color-disparity.md) |
| **Concept (the lighthouse)** | What gives a game its appeal and direction, e.g. 'the fun of sending opponents flying'. Sakurai compares it to a lighthouse beam: keep heading toward it through any storm and never change that trajectory midway. | [187](../episodes/notes/187-staying-true-to-your-concept.md) |
| **Conversion** | The step that puts edited parameter data into a build where it can be tested; one button press at Bandai Namco Studios, ten minutes plus a reboot on early Smash games. | [46](../episodes/notes/046-making-your-game-easy-to-tune.md) |
| **Copy Ability** | Kirby's power to take an inhaled enemy's ability, introduced in Kirby's Adventure as an optional depth layer on top of inhale-and-spit, so every enemy gained meaning. | [11](../episodes/notes/011-kirbys-adventure.md), [2](../episodes/notes/002-masahiro-sakurais-creative-works.md), [20](../episodes/notes/020-kirby-super-star.md) |
| **Crisp effects** | Polygons or simple images mixed into an effect and made its focus, giving Smash Ultimate's effects a clean, anime-style look. | [16](../episodes/notes/016-make-it-pop.md) |
| **Daily Report** | Sakurai's internal daily post to his team (a screenshot plus three to five topics, open to comments), started during Brawl. | [123](../episodes/notes/123-daily-report-suggestions.md), [43](../episodes/notes/043-sharing-info-within-a-team.md), [261](../episodes/notes/261-masahiro-sakurai-on-creating-games-finale-special.md) |
| **damage pose** | The first frame of a damage animation, held by hit stop; in Smash it's an extreme pain pose nothing like standby. | [142](../episodes/notes/142-damage-animations.md) |
| **de facto standards** | Genre control conventions (such as FPS fire and grenade buttons) that Sakurai says you're better off using, because they warm 'the handshake between human and computer'. | [175](../episodes/notes/175-its-easier-if-they-already-know.md) |
| **Debug mode (developer mode)** | In-development features for testing and analyzing the game, stripped from the release build. | [195](../episodes/notes/195-debug-mode.md), [217](../episodes/notes/217-transparency-features.md) |
| **deconstruct, analyze, rebuild** | Sakurai's method: pick what is fun, work out why (usually risk and reward), then rebuild it into an original mechanic. | [57](../episodes/notes/057-deconstruct-analyze-and-rebuild.md) |
| **Dev board** | Development hardware that may run differently from the retail unit; Mushiking's was just under one square meter. | [92](../episodes/notes/092-sodatete-kouchuu-ouja-mushiking.md), [141](../episodes/notes/141-unexpected-results.md) |
| **diagonal grip** | How a hand holds something swung with the forearm; a perpendicular grip makes characters look like action figures. | [210](../episodes/notes/210-portraying-fingers.md) |
| **display latency** | Delay added by a screen's picture post-processing, bypassed by Game Mode or Low Latency Mode; distinct from response time, the time a pixel takes to change color. At 60 fps, 16.67 ms is one frame. | [8](../episodes/notes/008-down-with-lag.md) |
| **Display latency vs. response time** | Response time is how long a pixel takes to change color; display latency comes from picture post-processing and is what Game Mode or Low Latency Mode removes. | [8](../episodes/notes/008-down-with-lag.md) |
| **Display priority** | The draw order that decides whether characters or effects appear in front; easy to force in 2D, but forcing it in 3D causes depth disparity. | [35](../episodes/notes/035-let-your-characters-shine.md), [131](../episodes/notes/131-hit-marks.md), [165](../episodes/notes/165-odds-and-ends-of-supervising.md) |
| **DPCM** | The Famicom channel that allowed sampled sounds, used sparingly early on because samples took a lot of cartridge space; Super Mario Bros. 3's timpani is the famous example. | [239](../episodes/notes/239-famicom-and-nes-audio.md) |
| **Dragon King: The Fighting Game** | The N64 prototype that became Super Smash Bros.; it had no special moves, dodges or items but already used Smash's rules. Iwata programmed it. | [27](../episodes/notes/027-super-smash-bros.md), [259](../episodes/notes/259-mr-iwata.md) |
| **Draw the light, not the asset** | His most common note to background artists: an asset isn't finished when it's modeled and textured with base colors; it must express the light it reflects in its environment. | [10](../episodes/notes/010-draw-the-light-not-the-asset.md) |
| **Drawers on top of drawers** | Nested window hierarchies needed by feature-packed games like his own; he calls organizing them a constant struggle. | [18](../episodes/notes/018-clarity-vs-style.md) |
| **dynamic range** | How far apart options are allowed to be; he wants it pushed as far as possible. | [247](../episodes/notes/247-amplify-both-strengths-and-weaknesses.md) |
| **Echo chamber** | A circle where your views keep being reinforced by agreement, hiding the many people who disagree; it distorts how you judge design ideas, reviews and balance. | [228](../episodes/notes/228-echo-chambers.md), [218](../episodes/notes/218-average-and-mediocre-are-the-same-thing.md), [247](../episodes/notes/247-amplify-both-strengths-and-weaknesses.md) |
| **Elite Smash** | A status tier for top players that also matches them against each other. | [170](../episodes/notes/170-the-pros-and-cons-of-leaderboards.md) |
| **Enbugging** | Introducing new bugs while fixing one, the reason fixes and patches cannot ship instantly. | [136](../episodes/notes/136-bug-testing-never-ends.md) |
| **enemy table** | Xevious's predefined spawn order that advances as you play and can be rewound by destroying a specific enemy. | [156](../episodes/notes/156-special-crossover-2.md) |
| **Errands** | Stretches where the story sends the player back and forth; mechanically just a few flags, so their packaging (rewards, small joys, route choice, quick exit, mystery) decides whether they're fun. | [64](../episodes/notes/064-good-errands-and-bad-errands.md) |
| **Everyone is here!** | Super Smash Bros. Ultimate's core concept: bringing back every past fighter, feasible because the team and dev environment carried over. | [160](../episodes/notes/160-super-smash-bros-ultimate.md) |
| **expanding play types** | Kirby Air Ride's aim of broadening how people play instead of encouraging one perfect method. | [59](../episodes/notes/059-kirby-air-ride.md) |
| **Expansion audio** | Extra sound chips in Famicom cartridges, or the Disk System's wave memory channel, that gave Japanese versions richer music; the NES lacked the pins for them. | [239](../episodes/notes/239-famicom-and-nes-audio.md), [96](../episodes/notes/096-the-family-computer-and-the-nes.md) |
| **Expression** | His umbrella word for everything (visuals, audio, programming, parameters) that together makes a massless game world feel real. | [205](../episodes/notes/205-making-the-imaginary-feel-real.md) |
| **Fiend's Cauldron / Intensity** | Kid Icarus: Uprising's difficulty wager: spend hearts to raise Intensity (default 2.0, up to 9.0) for bigger rewards or lower it (0.0 is nearly invincible); failure spills hearts and eases the retry. | [41](../episodes/notes/041-the-fiends-cauldron.md), [227](../episodes/notes/227-gameplay-as-part-of-a-live-performance.md), [125](../episodes/notes/125-kid-icarus-uprising.md) |
| **Fighter reveal** | Smash's flashy announcement videos for new fighters, split into CG and gameplay segments; made official from Smash for 3DS/Wii U, 37 in all. | [163](../episodes/notes/163-fighter-reveal-videos.md), [135](../episodes/notes/135-super-smash-bros-for-3ds-wii-u.md), [80](../episodes/notes/080-marketing-is-multiplicative.md) |
| **Final output** | The finished frame after everything is drawn into the framebuffer and post-processed; on powerful hardware it matters more than any single model. | [215](../episodes/notes/215-final-output.md) |
| **Final Smash** | Brawl's per-character super move; it could have been a generic item, but each fighter got their own to boost individuality. | [108](../episodes/notes/108-super-smash-bros-brawl.md) |
| **Finish Zoom** | Smash Ultimate's zoomed stop on a match-deciding hit; Sakurai calls it an extension of stop effects, closer to boss stop. | [3](../episodes/notes/003-stop-for-big-moments.md) |
| **fixed knockback** | A knockback value that ignores damage so the target always reacts the same way; used on the early hits of multi-hit moves like Mario's Super Jump Punch. | [145](../episodes/notes/145-knockback-in-super-smash-bros.md) |
| **Flashing** | His term for making the screen or a character shine: a full-screen flash for impact, a character flash to emphasize a change in status. | [223](../episodes/notes/223-flashing-effects.md) |
| **flick input** | Sakurai's name for reading how fast the analog stick reaches the edge of its range to trigger a dash (localized as 'Tap' in the N64 Super Smash Bros.). | [154](../episodes/notes/154-flick-input.md) |
| **flipped animation** | The Smash team's internal name for mirroring poses and movements when a character changes facing, so they face the camera from both sides. | [181](../episodes/notes/181-flipped-animation.md) |
| **follow-through** | The motion after the attack, when the character is briefly vulnerable (the recovery period); it takes the most screen time and determines the attack's impact. | [36](../episodes/notes/036-breaking-down-attack-animations.md), [77](../episodes/notes/077-follow-throughs-make-the-impact.md) |
| **Frame** | One redraw of the screen and games' usual unit of time; at 60 fps, one second is 60 frames, and distance equals time times speed. | [5](../episodes/notes/005-frame-rates.md), [76](../episodes/notes/076-learn-to-count-frames.md) |
| **Free bonus (updates)** | Sakurai's framing of post-launch balance and quality-of-life updates: welcome extras beyond the promise kept at launch, unlike fixes for fatal bugs. | [231](../episodes/notes/231-online-updates.md) |
| **fun beyond game essence** | Sources of fun other than push and pull: control feel, story, licensed fandom, simulation, building and crafting, rhythm. | [26](../episodes/notes/026-fun-beyond-game-essence.md) |
| **game essence** | Sakurai's name for a game's push and pull, or risk and reward: the fun of managing risks to get rewards. He calls it one part of a game's identity, not the whole. | [6](../episodes/notes/006-risk-and-reward.md), [26](../episodes/notes/026-fun-beyond-game-essence.md), [193](../episodes/notes/193-game-essence-in-action-games.md) |
| **givens** | Genre conventions taken for granted; he re-examines them at the start of each project. | [82](../episodes/notes/082-do-competitive-games-have-to-be-complex.md), [232](../episodes/notes/232-manga-like-visuals.md) |
| **Global Smash Power (GSP)** | Smash Ultimate's rank, where higher is better, measured against the whole player count, and the top player is hidden. | [170](../episodes/notes/170-the-pros-and-cons-of-leaderboards.md), [200](../episodes/notes/200-presenting-scores.md) |
| **guaranteed minimum knockback** | A per-attack minimum launch so even a barely damaged target reacts; paired with knockback scaling to keep it from inflating launches. | [145](../episodes/notes/145-knockback-in-super-smash-bros.md) |
| **handicap** | Any system or setting that evens out unequal players, from catch-up items to per-player difficulty. | [157](../episodes/notes/157-special-crossover-3.md), [202](../episodes/notes/202-handicap-systems.md), [256](../episodes/notes/256-give-yourself-a-handicap-when-balancing-your-game.md) |
| **Handshake between human and computer** | His phrase for the controls and conventions that connect player and game; standard controls make it a little warmer. | [175](../episodes/notes/175-its-easier-if-they-already-know.md) |
| **Help text** | A short description of the highlighted menu item, often at the bottom of the screen; his rule is never to explain an item using its own name. | [229](../episodes/notes/229-adding-help-text-to-menus.md) |
| **Helper** | Kirby Super Star's co-op system: an enemy's ability becomes a second playable character in a supporting role, with the camera following only the lead player. | [20](../episodes/notes/020-kirby-super-star.md) |
| **Helper system / Space Jump** | Kirby Super Star's co-op solution: the camera follows the lead player, and a supporting Helper who drifts too far off screen 'Space Jumps' back. | [20](../episodes/notes/020-kirby-super-star.md) |
| **Hierarchical outline** | A collapsible, folder-like text outline (heading, subheading, detail) he uses for ideas and specs, e.g. fighter, then special moves, then each move's spec. | [137](../episodes/notes/137-staying-organized-with-hierarchical-outlines.md) |
| **Hit marks** | Sakurai's name for the visual flair shown when an attack connects (sparks, flashes, shockwaves); guard effects count as hit marks too. | [131](../episodes/notes/131-hit-marks.md) |
| **hit stop** | A brief stop of both attacker and target when an attack lands; whether it's there completely changes how heavy a hit feels. In Smash it always includes some shake. | [3](../episodes/notes/003-stop-for-big-moments.md), [47](../episodes/notes/047-eight-hit-stop-techniques.md) |
| **Home base** | One official place, usually the game's website, that delivers all of a game's information alongside social media. | [143](../episodes/notes/143-official-websites.md) |
| **HUD (heads-up display)** | Information overlaid on the play area, named after fighter-plane displays that let pilots read data without looking away; in games, everything placed at the forefront of the screen. | [254](../episodes/notes/254-huds.md) |
| **human adjustments** | Easing off by hand, like letting a child grab the Smash Ball; possibly the most effective handicap there is. | [202](../episodes/notes/202-handicap-systems.md) |
| **Ideal spec** | The best version of a design, written first; you then course-correct from it for cost-effectiveness instead of bending the proposal out of shape up front. | [109](../episodes/notes/109-dont-rely-on-a-plan-b.md) |
| **Image boards** | A few pieces of art by an exceptional artist showing a game's world, scenery, characters and items, used as the reference that unifies the team's style. | [130](../episodes/notes/130-unifying-visual-style.md) |
| **individuality** | A fighter's distinct identity; protecting it is the main reason he steps into balance decisions. | [218](../episodes/notes/218-average-and-mediocre-are-the-same-thing.md), [220](../episodes/notes/220-play-testing-keeps-going.md) |
